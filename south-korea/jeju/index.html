MOVE / 01

A car adds reach;
it does not add daylight

Jeju has no passenger rail or subway. Buses connect the airport, the two cities and regional hubs, while rural stops may involve longer waits and walks. Driving helps only when parking, fatigue, navigation, insurance and an alcohol-free driver are already solved.

Quick answers

Jeju planning FAQ

How many days do you need on Jeju Island?

Four full days support one inland or mountain day, two coherent coastal regions and one flexible day. Five or six days make a two-base stay, weather changes and a ferry attempt much easier to absorb.

Do I need a rental car?

No. A bus-first trip works when you choose fewer places, sleep near useful corridors and accept that rural transfers consume real time. A car expands reach, but parking, fatigue, insurance and a designated driver remain part of the plan.

Should I stay in Jeju City or Seogwipo?

Jeju City favors airport access, northern routes and short stays. Seogwipo favors the south coast and a more compact evening base. Split the stay only when saved travel time exceeds the cost of changing hotels.

Can I combine Hallasan and Udo in one day?

No useful first-trip plan should. Hallasan is an early, condition-dependent mountain day; Udo is a separate ferry day with a last-return constraint. Keep them on different days with independent fallbacks.
