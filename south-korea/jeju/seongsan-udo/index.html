Tuff-cone gate

Climb the path that is open—not the photograph you saved

Seongsan Ilchulbong is a UNESCO-listed hydrovolcanic tuff cone with managed free and paid walking areas. The summit route uses steep stairs. Current access, weather, time and the group's knees decide the useful route.

OPEN?Read the entrance notice, current operating information and any restriction.
ABLE?Use the free lower area if the paid summit stairs do not suit the group.
ENOUGH?Stop early enough to eat, collect documents and reach the port calmly.

Resident island protocol

Move through a home, not an attraction circuit

Udo contains farms, fishing work, homes, protected shore material and narrow roads. Keep private lanes, rhodoliths and resident movement outside the souvenir economy.

  • RoadYield, slow down and park only where permitted.

  • WasteCarry out what local bins cannot responsibly accept.
Quick answers

Seongsan and Udo FAQ

Can Seongsan Ilchulbong and Udo fit into one day?

Yes when conditions and pace allow, but success requires two separate checks. Do not let a slow dawn ascent consume the ferry margin, and never shorten the return buffer to add another island stop.

Where do Udo ferries leave from?

Official guidance currently lists Seongsan Port and Jongdal Port. Confirm the exact operator, terminal, outward sailing, arrival port and return on the day.

Do I need identification?

Current terminal guidance describes identification and passenger paperwork. Verify the accepted document for every traveler before leaving the hotel.

What if ferries are cancelled?

Keep a mainland east-coast route ready. Use Seongsan, a verified coastal segment or an indoor cultural anchor rather than chasing another unconfirmed crossing.
