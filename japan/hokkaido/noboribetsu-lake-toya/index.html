A volcanic road, two distinct resort bases

Treat the stations, hot-spring towns and lake shore as separate stops

Noboribetsu and Toyako Onsen sit on the same south-Hokkaido rail corridor, but neither resort is beside its JR station. Noboribetsu Station is about a 15-minute bus ride from the onsen district; Toya Station connects to Toyako Onsen by a separate Donan Bus route, about 26 minutes on the currently posted service. Reserve the limited-express train first, then match each bus or hotel transfer to its arrival.

Jigokudani is a lived landscape

The valley’s sulfur springs drew workers before they drew visitors. In 1857, sulfur miner Hanbei Okada built a bathhouse for laborers; in 1858, Kinzo Takimoto opened an inn and built the road that helped establish today’s resort. Walk the boardwalk as a working geothermal edge, not an invitation to leave it.

Toya is a caldera lake, not a hot-spring extension

The geopark dates Lake Toya’s caldera basin to roughly 110,000 years ago and the volcanic domes of Nakajima to about 45,000 years ago. Usu has erupted in living memory: Showa-shinzan rose in 1943–45, and the 2000 eruption reshaped nearby routes. The lake shore, volcano trails and ropeway are different visits.

Make the intercity leg explicit

Since 14 March 2026, JR Hokkaido’s limited-express services use reserved seating throughout. Book the train between Sapporo, Noboribetsu, Toya and Hakodate as needed, then check the separate bus schedules to both resorts. Do not assume a late train has a connecting bus.

Jigokudani, Oyunuma and the onsen town

Read the vents from the marked valley paths

Jigokudani is a volcanic explosion crater at the foot of Mount Kuttara. The short boardwalks bring steam vents and mineral-stained ground within reach of town; the longer forest approach continues toward Oyunuma and its river. Keep the day’s route inside marked, currently open paths—hot water and unstable ground are not safe shortcuts.

Start at the basin

Jigokudani boardwalk

Begin at the valley overlook, then follow the open boardwalk loop to the vent viewpoints. Take the short version in wind, snow or poor footing; do not cross ropes or touch deposits.

Extend only when open

Oyunuma and Taisho Jigoku

The forest trail links the volcanic ponds and Oyunuma River. The official route places the natural footbath beside Taisho Jigoku; it is reached on foot, not by a short drive to the water. Check closures and allow time to retrace the path.

Understand the bathing town

Noboribetsu Onsen

The sulfur trade and Takimoto’s early inn turned hot water into a local livelihood. Give the town an evening and a morning bath rather than treating it as a single photo stop; ask the exact property about tattoos and day-use rules.

Plan for a closure

Bath, museum or rest

If a trail closes, use the onsen and an indoor local stop. No scenic detour justifies walking beyond a barrier or onto icy volcanic ground.

A caldera, its younger domes and the towns around them

Use Toyako Onsen for the shore; allow a separate day for Usu

From the Toyako Onsen waterfront, Nakajima sits within the caldera lake and Usu forms the southern volcanic edge. A ferry or lakeside walk gives a water-level view; the ropeway and geopark trails give a different, higher view. The UNESCO Global Geopark also interprets the 1943–45 birth of Showa-shinzan and the 2000 eruption, alongside the communities that live with volcanic change.

  • Check current volcanic notices before choosing a trail or ropeway.
  • Confirm the ferry, ropeway and local bus schedules for your date and weather.
  • Keep the Toyako Onsen waterfront as the low-effort plan if an operation pauses.
  • Do not plan to walk between the lakeside resort and Usu’s trailheads without checking the actual route and transport.
A two-base volcanic journey

Walk Jigokudani, transfer once, then open to the lake

Two nights work best without a car: spend the first at Noboribetsu Onsen and the second at Toyako Onsen. Add a third when you want a full Usu day or need slower bus connections. Reverse the direction for a northbound trip.

Bathing without guesswork

Facility rules are part of the reservation

Onsen conventions are consistent in principle but policies vary. Read the property's official guidance—especially for tattoos, children, mixed-gender facilities, towels and day-use hours.

Before

Wash completely

Clean at the shower station before entering the communal bath, and keep the small towel out of the water.

During

Keep the bath quiet

No swimwear unless explicitly designed for it, no phone photography and no splashing through a shared soaking area.

Policy

Ask, do not assume

Tattoo rules differ. Contact the exact property about covers, private baths or access before paying a non-refundable rate.

Quick answers

Noboribetsu and Lake Toya FAQ

Can I see both places in one day?

You can touch both with a car or exact transport, but the result sacrifices onsen and landscape time. Stay one night in each or choose the stronger match.

Which place should I choose?

Noboribetsu for close volcanic walks and concentrated onsen; Lake Toya for open water, caldera scale and Mount Usu. Transport direction may decide.

Are the train stations beside the resorts?

No. Both Noboribetsu Onsen and Toyako Onsen require a final local road connection from their respective railway stations.

Can I visit an onsen with tattoos?

Policies vary by property. Ask the exact facility about public baths, covers or reservable private baths before booking.
