Follow the flowers from Biei to Furano

Lavender is a short midsummer chapter, not a promise that every field peaks together. Furano Tourism places the classic display around mid to late July; cultivars and elevation shift the edges, so choose a farm from its current bloom report rather than a fixed calendar.

June into early July

Earlier colour, cooler routes

Early varieties can begin before the familiar purple season. Treat each farm as its own display and check its current opening and flower notes before crossing the valley.

Mid to late July

Peak colour, peak demand

This is the usual lavender window and the busiest time for roads, parking and seasonal trains. Start with one named farm and arrive early rather than collecting distant photo stops.

Late July into August

Later plots vary

Higher or later varieties may continue after the central fields change, but harvest and weather move the finish. Keep a green-season alternative such as the hills, forest or local food.

Stations are only the first leg

Connect the rural links before you leave town

Furano, Biei, the hill roads and Shirogane are a spread-out agricultural landscape. Rail gets you between towns; it does not join every farm or viewpoint into a walkable circuit.

Rail for the town-to-town spine

JR connects Asahikawa, Biei and Furano, with extra seasonal sightseeing service on selected dates. The temporary Lavender Farm Station is a summer stop on designated train days, not a year-round station; verify the timetable for your exact travel date.

Bus to Blue Pond and Shirogane

The Biei–Shirogane route bus serves the Blue Pond corridor. Check the current departure and return pair before travelling from Biei Station; do not assume a frequent onward bus if you linger at the pond.

Hills need a plan of their own

A rental car or small-group tour is the simplest way to join several Biei viewpoints. Cycling suits confident riders who are ready for rolling grades, changing weather and open-road traffic—not a flat town promenade.

Check each year’s operating dates

JR’s lavender services and Farm Tomita’s temporary stop run only on published dates. Recheck the official timetable and allow for the last return, especially if you are staying outside Furano or Biei.

Three useful bases

Sleep near the landscape you will repeat

The correct base changes with transport. A rental-car loop can split nights; a rail or tour plan often benefits from one better-connected city.

Southern field country

Furano

Useful for flower farms, Ningle Terrace, restaurants and ski-season access. It is farther from Blue Pond and northern Biei hills.

Best southern base
Hill and pond access

Biei

Closer to patchwork roads and Shirogane. Evening dining and transport choice are smaller, so plan both before arrival.

Two days, two distinct landscapes

Give the hill country and flower farms their own time

This sequence avoids doubling back and gives the water, fields and working farmland room to be more than quick roadside photographs. Reverse it around your bed and the day’s weather.

BIEI NORTH / FURANO SOUTH

Biei Station → Shirogane → Furano

2 days
Day 1 / morning

Choose one Biei hill loop

Start from a mapped public viewpoint route. Park only in signed areas; fields, farm tracks and driveways remain private workplaces even when the view looks open.

Day 1 / afternoon

Blue Pond and Shirogane

Use the route bus or your arranged driver from Biei Station. Leave time for the pond, the nearby falls or an onsen, then confirm the ride back before the final stop.

Day 2 / morning

One Furano farm, well chosen

Check current flowers and opening hours, then give one farm enough time to walk its paths. Add a second nearby display only if bloom and transport make the detour worthwhile.

Day 2 / afternoon

Return without a photo chase

Choose town food, a forest-side stop or an early train. Avoid stacking another distant viewpoint against a fixed departure.

Access note

Biei Tourism lists an accessible restroom at Blue Pond and a loan wheelchair at the Biei Tourist Information Center near the station. That does not establish that every farm path or viewpoint surface suits every mobility aid; confirm the exact route and current surface with each operator.

Beautiful because people protect it

A field is not public scenery

Biei's roads pass working farms and private land. Stay on signed public space, use official parking and accept a missed photograph rather than entering crops, driveways or dangerous road edges.

  • Never step into a field for a cleaner frame.
  • Do not block farm vehicles or private entrances.
  • Fly no drone without explicit legal permission.
  • Carry waste away from rural viewpoints.
Quick answers

Furano and Biei FAQ

When is lavender most likely to be in bloom?

Mid to late July is Furano Tourism’s usual lavender window, but weather, variety and elevation shift it. Check the chosen farm’s current flower information before booking around a specific field.

Can I see Blue Pond by public transport?

Yes. The Biei–Shirogane route bus serves the pond area, but service is timetable-led. Check the return departure before leaving Biei Station and keep enough time for the walk and wait.

Why does Blue Pond change colour?

The pond formed beside erosion-control works after Mount Tokachi’s 1988 eruption. Mineral-bearing water and light conditions help create its blue appearance, which can look different after rain, snowmelt or under cloud.

Is Blue Pond step-free?

Biei Tourism lists an accessible restroom and a wheelchair loan in Biei town. Path surfaces and conditions vary; confirm the approach and viewpoint route that suits your mobility needs before travelling.

Should I stay in Furano, Biei or Asahikawa?

Furano is practical for southern flower country, Biei for the hills and Shirogane, and Asahikawa for rail and city services. For a no-car trip, compare the first and last bus or train each day before choosing a hotel.
