Four useful starting points

Three city breaks. One island-scale journey.

Tokyo, Kyoto and Osaka are city guides; Hokkaido is a prefecture spanning a large northern island. Their distances and transit assumptions are different, so choose a travel shape before adding stops.

City guide · Kanto

Tokyo

A metropolis spread across many centers, where eastern districts, central wards and western neighborhoods make different day clusters. Eight area guides help you group stops and choose a hotel base by train line.

Start with Sensō-ji and Ueno in the east; plan Shibuya, Harajuku or Shinjuku as separate west-side clusters.

City guide · Kansai

Kyoto

A historic basin city of temple precincts, gardens and hillside approaches. Keep Nishiki and central Kyoto, eastern Higashiyama and the north-west temples in separate day clusters; check the bus or rail leg for each.

Pair Kiyomizu-dera with nearby Higashiyama; reach Kinkaku-ji separately in the northwest.

City guide · Kansai

Osaka

A commercial port city with a strong after-dark food culture. Namba and Dotonbori, Umeda, the castle park and Osaka Bay are separate clusters; use the five district guides to avoid crossing town mid-day.

Kuromon Market is near Namba; Osaka Aquarium Kaiyukan belongs to a separate bay-side day.

Island and prefecture guide · 9 corridors

Hokkaido

Ainu heritage, volcanic parks, wetlands and long coasts sit across a much larger travel area. Rail connects major cities, while many rural legs need careful local-bus, seasonal or driving plans; choose one corridor first.

Pair Sapporo with Otaru, or reserve a separate leg for Kushiro Marsh or Shiretoko.

First-trip rule

Choose bases by the days you want

Fast intercity rail can hide the time lost to hotel check-outs, station changes and airport access. Treat arrival, transfer and departure days as partial days when you count sightseeing time.

  • Use a city as a base for several nights unless the stop has one clear purpose; avoid changing hotels just to tick off a nearby city.
  • Count hotel-to-hotel time, station walks, airport access and check-in—not just the train or flight segment.
  • Group districts by geography: Kyoto's eastern temple slopes and north-west temples are not one short walk; Osaka Bay is not a Namba detour.
  • Keep a flexible half-day after demanding sightseeing and check luggage rules before choosing a transfer train.

Transport decoded

Use the network without buying every pass

Japan's transport is excellent. The confusing part is the number of operators and products, not the act of riding a train.

IC cards for ordinary movement

Use a compatible IC card for most urban trains, buses and small purchases. Limited express and shinkansen trips can require separate tickets.

Passes are a calculation

Compare the pass price with the exact intercity legs in your route. A famous pass is not automatically a saving.

Stations are neighborhoods

Major station complexes can be large. Save the exit number, operator and nearby landmark—not only the station name.

Compare the dated fare with the pass

As checked on 6 October 2026, the official Japan Rail Pass page lists an online 7-day Ordinary adult pass at ¥50,000. This is a comparison point, not a recommendation: price your actual eligible JR journeys, regional pass options and single tickets. For the Tokyo–Kyoto Shinkansen leg, check JR Central's SmartEX fare and booking service; choose a local operator for Kyoto–Osaka based on your stations. City subway and private-rail rides may be separate.

Season strategy

Choose weather trade-offs, not just postcard dates

Japan stretches across several climates. The best month depends on the region, activity and your tolerance for crowds.

Spring · Mar–May

Mild in many cities and famous for blossoms. Peak bloom is brief, variable and often accompanied by higher demand.

Summer · Jun–Aug

Rainy periods, heat and humidity shape many urban trips. Festivals and mountain or northern routes can still make summer worthwhile.

Autumn · Sep–Nov

Comfortable conditions and foliage in many regions, with timing that shifts by altitude and latitude.

Winter · Dec–Feb

Strong for snow destinations, hot springs and clearer low-season city days outside holiday peaks.

Public holidays matter

Golden Week, New Year and other holiday periods can affect transport and accommodation. Check the current calendar.

Typhoon awareness

Late summer and autumn trips should keep some schedule flexibility and monitor official weather and operator notices.

Low-stress etiquette

Observe first, then follow the room

You do not need perfect cultural fluency. A few habits remove most friction.

Keep public transport quiet

Use headphones, silence calls and let passengers exit before boarding. Queue markings usually show where to stand.

Handle waste intentionally

Public bins can be limited. Carry a small bag and dispose of trash at your accommodation or an appropriate collection point.

Respect photo boundaries

Do not block streets, photograph people closely without permission or enter restricted private lanes for a famous angle.

Check entry requirements with the authorities

Visa and entry rules depend on nationality, passport, travel purpose and length of stay. Start with the Ministry of Foreign Affairs short-stay guidance, then confirm your case with the appropriate Japanese embassy or consulate and your airline or transit authority. The Digital Agency's Visit Japan Web explains online arrival procedures; it is not a visa-eligibility check. This guide cannot determine individual entry eligibility.

Quick answers

Japan planning FAQ

How many days should I plan for Japan?

Seven days: stay in Tokyo or focus on Kyoto and Osaka. Ten days can fit Tokyo, Kyoto and Osaka using a three-base route with light arrival and departure days. Fourteen days gives room for slower travel or a longer regional stop.

Can I visit Tokyo, Kyoto and Osaka in ten days?

A workable 10-day shape is 4 nights in Tokyo, 3 in Kyoto and 2 in Osaka. It assumes 9 nights and that arrival and departure days are not full sightseeing days. Take the Tokaido Shinkansen between Tokyo and Kyoto, then choose a local rail operator that fits your Kyoto and Osaka stations. Adjust the split for a late arrival or early flight.

What changes if I add Hokkaido?

A 14-day trip has 13 nights. One workable shape is four nights in Tokyo, three in Kyoto or Osaka, and six in one Hokkaido corridor, with arrival on Day 1, departure on Day 14 and two transfer days; this trades away the other Kansai city. It assumes flights line up for a Tokyo arrival and Hokkaido departure; if you must return to another airport, add a positioning day or choose fewer stops. To keep Tokyo, Kyoto, Osaka and Hokkaido, consider about 19–20 days (18–19 nights) as a flexible planning range, not a minimum.

Does the Japan Rail Pass pay off?

As of 6 October 2026, the official listing shows a 7-day Ordinary adult pass at ¥50,000 when purchased online. Compare the current total for your eligible JR legs and regional passes with dated individual fares; the nationwide pass does not cover many subway and private-rail journeys, and prices can change. Check current eligibility and coverage with the railway operators.

How do I check Japan's entry requirements?

Visa and entry rules depend on nationality, passport, purpose and length of stay. Check the Japanese Ministry of Foreign Affairs and the appropriate Japanese embassy or consulate. Visit Japan Web explains online arrival procedures; it is not a visa-eligibility check. This guide does not provide personal immigration advice.
