Choose a useful home base

Sleep near the evening you want

Tokyo is too large for one universally best district. A base saves energy when it matches your late dinners, early departures and most repeated rail corridor.

West-side energy

Shinjuku

Broad transport reach, skyscrapers and late dining; the station takes patience.

Street culture

Shibuya

Fast access to Harajuku and lively nights; expect crowds and higher central prices.

Intercity rail

Tokyo Station

Excellent for Shinkansen days and polished evenings; less casual after dark.

East-side pace

Asakusa / Ueno

History, museums and a calmer base; some west-side trips take longer.

Transit decoder

Read the operator before the platform

“Tokyo subway” is not one company, and not every useful train is a subway. Search the full station and line, then save the exit nearest your destination.

JR East

Useful for the Yamanote loop and many cross-city or regional journeys. Tokyo, Shinjuku, Shibuya, Ueno and Ikebukuro are major JR anchors.

Tokyo Metro

Nine lines connect many central districts. Line color, letter and station number help more than memorizing every Japanese station name.

Toei and private rail

Toei runs four subway lines; Keikyu, Keisei, Tokyu, Odakyu and others matter for airports, suburbs and selected city corridors.

An IC card does not make every train identical

It simplifies ordinary fares, but some limited-express or reserved-seat services still need an additional ticket. Follow the operator's current rules.

Airport decision

Compare the hotel door, not the airport name

Haneda is much closer to central Tokyo, while Narita sits outside the metropolis in Chiba. The right choice still depends on airfare, arrival time and your final transfer.

  • Haneda: compare Keikyu toward Shinagawa with Tokyo Monorail toward Hamamatsucho.
  • Narita west-side route: compare JR's Narita Express for Tokyo, Shibuya and Shinjuku.
  • Narita east-side route: compare Keisei Skyliner for Nippori and Ueno.
  • Late arrival: check the dated final service before booking a nonrefundable hotel connection.
A first-trip framework

Five days grouped by city geography

This is a route skeleton, not a race. Use the area guides to select depth, reservations and weather swaps inside each day.

Day 1 · West

Meiji Jingu → Harajuku → Shibuya

Begin with the shrine forest, move through fashion streets and finish around Shibuya after dark. Keep the walk one-way.

Day 2 · Shinjuku

Garden → west skyline → east evening

Use the quieter daylight side first, orient at the west towers and cross only once for dinner or nightlife.

Day 3 · East

Asakusa → Kappabashi → Ueno

See the temple streets early, browse a focused interest stop, then protect time for one Ueno museum or park walk.

Day 4 · Central

Tokyo Station → palace edge → Ginza

Use a palace-garden visit only when current opening information fits, then continue toward Ginza for architecture and dinner.

Day 5 · Choose

Bay, art, subculture or a day trip

Pick Odaiba/Toyosu, Roppongi/Azabu, Akihabara/Kanda or Ikebukuro according to interests—not because every list says it is compulsory.

Quick answers

Tokyo planning FAQ

How many days do you need in Tokyo?

Five full days is a practical first core. Add nights for slower shopping, several museums, theme parks or day trips without stripping the city days of breathing room.

Which area is best for a first stay?

Shinjuku and Shibuya favor west-side energy; Tokyo Station favors intercity rail; Asakusa and Ueno favor a calmer east-side rhythm. Choose around your evenings and longest repeat journeys.

Is Haneda always better than Narita?

No. Haneda is closer, but airfare, flight schedule and the final hotel transfer can outweigh distance. Compare the full door-to-door journey and last service.

Do I need a Tokyo rail pass?

Not by default. Use an IC card for simple pay-as-you-go travel, then price any pass against the exact operators and rides you will take.

Should I take day trips from Tokyo?

Only when the destination adds a priority that Tokyo does not. A first five-day visit already fills easily; add day trips with extra nights instead of treating them as mandatory.
