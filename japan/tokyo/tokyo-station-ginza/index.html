A station with two fronts

Pick the side before you leave the platform

Tokyo Station is long, layered and connected to underground commercial areas. The correct side matters more than the nearest visible exit.

West

Marunouchi

The restored red-brick facade, office towers and broad axis toward the Imperial Palace. Best for architecture and the classic station photograph.

Use for · palace and facade
East

Yaesu

Highway-bus facilities, office towers and access toward Nihombashi. Practical, but visually different from the famous station front.

Use for · buses and east side
Inside

Gransta and transfer levels

Food, gifts and long internal corridors can consume time. Confirm whether a shop sits inside or outside ticketed gates.

Use for · planned shopping
South

Yurakucho connection

A useful bridge between the station district and Ginza, especially when the route includes architecture, cafés or an evening meal.

Arrival and departure day

Do the luggage decision before sightseeing

Tokyo Station works beautifully as an arrival-day district only after bags are stored and the correct collection point is saved.

Before arrival

Save train and gate

Record the service, car position if useful, transfer line and the hotel-facing exit.

With luggage

Use a defined storage plan

Coin lockers can fill. Compare hotel storage and staffed services, then save closing time.

Before departure

Rehearse the return

Know whether a final purchase is inside the gates and allow time for the long platform walk.

Do not photograph a locker and assume that is enough

Save the station level, nearby shop or gate, locker number and closing/access conditions. A giant station can contain many identical corridors.

Calendar-aware planning

Never hard-code the palace garden into Monday

The Imperial Household Agency publishes variable hours, regular closure days and temporary restrictions for the East Gardens. Check the exact date, then keep Ginza as the reliable evening anchor.

  • Open the official date calendar before routing.
  • Allow security and entry time.
  • Carry no plan that depends on a last-minute entry.
  • Use Marunouchi or Nihombashi as the closed-day alternative.

Quick answers

Tokyo Station and Ginza FAQ

What is the difference between Marunouchi and Yaesu?

Marunouchi is the west, red-brick-facade side toward the Imperial Palace; Yaesu is the east side toward Nihombashi and bus facilities.

Can I walk from Tokyo Station to Ginza?

Yes, especially via Yurakucho. Use a train or taxi instead when carrying luggage, in severe weather or when the destination sits at Ginza's far edge.

Is Tokyo Station a good hotel base?

It is excellent for Shinkansen trips, central access and refined evenings. Shinjuku and Shibuya offer more casual late-night energy.

Are the East Gardens open daily?

No. Check the Imperial Household Agency's official date calendar for closures, hours and entry restrictions.
