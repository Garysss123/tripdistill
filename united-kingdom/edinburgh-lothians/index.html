Edinburgh & the Lothians

Use one ridge, one booked interior and one level change at a time.

Treat Edinburgh as two city days plus a separate East Lothian coast day: the Old Town is a ridge, the New Town sits on the opposite side of Waverley, and Dean Village drops into a river valley. The coast train is easy to reach but the harbour, boat window and return still make North Berwick a full-day choice.

BEFORE THE FIRST DEPARTURE

Four moving parts control this region.

BASE

Sleep beside the useful departure.

Aim for three full sightseeing days: one Old Town ridge, one New Town/Dean Village route, and one North Berwick coast day. A fourth day gives you room to move the coast outing when wind or the boat schedule looks poor. Waverley is the useful rail base for the east-coast branch; Haymarket suits western connections, while either area can require a steep final street depending on the hotel entrance.

TRANSFER

Name the whole connection.

Waverley lies in the valley between Old and New Town, so choose the station exit and street level before walking with luggage. The Scottish Seabird Centre describes North Berwick as about 35 minutes by train from Waverley, then a 15-minute downhill walk to the harbour-side centre and uphill on return; check the dated ScotRail journey and return before leaving.

WINDOW

Let current conditions edit the route.

Rain can turn the New Town into a gallery-and-street day; wind can make both high viewpoints and the East Lothian shore uncomfortable. Scottish Seabird Centre wildlife trips are seasonal (its current page lists late March to early October), so put North Berwick on a date with a confirmed sailing only if the boat is the reason for going. Festival periods also add crowd and ticket pressure around the ridge.

FALLBACK

Keep a complete lower-risk day.

For a wet city day, keep one indoor gallery or the National Museum with the surrounding street grid and skip the Dean Village stair descent. If a North Berwick sailing is cancelled, the harbour, Scottish Seabird Centre Discovery Experience and town make a land-based visit; if sea wind makes the shore unpleasant too, take an earlier checked train back instead of chasing another exposed coast.

COMPARE BEFORE BOARDING

Each route buys something by leaving something else out.

Ridge-entry and descent clock

Old Town, Castle & Royal Mile

Gateway: The full ridge is not a level stroll. The Castle approach includes a 110 m uphill esplanade and its interior route rises over uneven cobbles; HES lists a 15-degree steepest internal section and a 70-step exit. A mobility vehicle to Crown Square can be arranged at the entrance on request, but the rest of the route still needs a personal access check. If stairs, cobbles or sustained slopes are a barrier, make the public lower-ridge section or a bus-linked visit the complete plan.

Sacrifice: Castle, every close, a museum and Holyroodhouse exceed a useful single day. The Castle is the steep upper anchor; the Palace stands at the end of the Royal Mile and is a separate timed visit. Pick one interior and let the other end become street context.

Time: Allow six to eight hours for one royal interior and the Old Town ridge. The castle alone can use three to four hours during busy periods.

Terrace-to-water level chart

New Town, Galleries & Dean Village

Gateway: The recommended descent from the Modern galleries to the Water of Leith uses a long flight of steps; cross the footbridge and turn left toward Dean Village. The walkway is only partly wheelchair accessible. If stairs, wet steps or uneven riverside surfaces do not suit, stay at street level and use Lothian Buses 24 or 29 to Raeburn Place in Stockbridge instead.

Sacrifice: One gallery plus the valley walk makes a focused day; adding both Modern galleries, the full Georgian grid and more river miles turns it into a long route. Dean Village is worth the lower-level detour only if the group can manage the stairs and return climb.

Time: Allow five to seven hours for one gallery, the New Town and a bounded Water of Leith segment. A gallery-and-grid visit can fit four hours.

Coast-train-and-seabird board

North Berwick & East Lothian

Gateway: The Scottish Seabird Centre lists the station-to-centre walk as about 15 minutes downhill and uphill on return. Its centre is wheelchair accessible, but it says boat trips are unsuitable for wheelchair users and people with limited mobility; its RIB is also unsuitable for people with back/neck problems or who are pregnant. Bring waterproofs: the operator says they are not provided. Check the dated ScotRail return before leaving Edinburgh.

Sacrifice: The Waverley rail trip, harbour walk and boat already use most of a day; adding a long shore walk makes the return fragile. Choose the boat for an island-focused view, or choose the centre and town for a flexible day without the RIB’s physical demands or sea-cancellation risk.

Time: Allow a full day from Edinburgh with a boat, or five to six hours for town and coast. A long walk needs the full daylight window.
