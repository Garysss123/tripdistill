Edinburgh & the Lothians

Use one ridge, one booked interior and one level change at a time.

Edinburgh’s Old Town ridge, New Town terraces and lower valleys create a vertical city where short map distances conceal stairs and steep streets. East Lothian adds a separate coast railway. A strong plan fixes one controlled interior and uses the city’s level changes as the route rather than an inconvenience.

BEFORE THE FIRST DEPARTURE

Four moving parts control this region.

BASE

Sleep beside the useful departure.

Three to five nights supports Old Town, New Town and an East Lothian branch with weather flexibility. Staying near Waverley is central but can involve steep exits; Haymarket suits western connections and some airport journeys.

TRANSFER

Name the whole connection.

Waverley station sits in the valley between Old and New Towns, with exits at different levels. Trams, buses and rail serve distinct corridors. Festival periods, rugby and major events can alter central access and accommodation pressure.

WINDOW

Let current conditions edit the route.

Wind and rain affect exposed viewpoints year-round. Festival season increases crowds and ticket pressure; winter shortens Arthur’s Seat and coast plans but gives strong museum and street-light alternatives.

FALLBACK

Keep a complete lower-risk day.

Keep a complete National Museum, gallery or lower-city route for poor weather. If a castle or palace booking fails, remain on the same ridge instead of crossing the city for another timed monument.

COMPARE BEFORE BOARDING

Each route buys something by leaving something else out.

Ridge-entry and descent clock

Old Town, Castle & Royal Mile

Gateway: Waverley exits reach different street levels; the castle esplanade is uphill and controlled. Buses can place visitors near the upper or lower ridge. Plan the direction first and do not assume a palace ticket includes a route through adjacent royal grounds.

Sacrifice: A deep castle visit, every close, a museum and Holyrood Palace exceed one useful day. Choosing the castle sacrifices lower-ridge depth; choosing Holyrood means the upper fortress becomes exterior context.

Time: Allow six to eight hours for one royal interior and the Old Town ridge. The castle alone can use three to four hours during busy periods.

Terrace-to-water level chart

New Town, Galleries & Dean Village

Gateway: Princes Street and Waverley sit near the New Town edge, while Dean Village is below street level. Water of Leith paths have specific access points. Select the descent and exit before entering the valley; do not rely on the nearest-looking bridge.

Sacrifice: A deep gallery visit, complete New Town architecture circuit and long Water of Leith walk exceed a relaxed day. Choosing the valley sacrifices a second collection; choosing galleries keeps Dean Village brief.

Time: Allow five to seven hours for one gallery, the New Town and a bounded Water of Leith segment. A gallery-and-grid visit can fit four hours.

Coast-train-and-seabird board

North Berwick & East Lothian

Gateway: North Berwick station is inland from the harbour. Boats use specific departure points and weather policies; coast paths and beaches have different surfaces and tide exposure. Save the final ScotRail service before leaving town.

Sacrifice: A substantial boat trip, long coast walk and relaxed town visit do not fit one weather-resilient day. Choosing the boat sacrifices walking distance; choosing land gives up the island approach but protects flexibility.

Time: Allow a full day from Edinburgh with a boat, or five to six hours for town and coast. A long walk needs the full daylight window.
