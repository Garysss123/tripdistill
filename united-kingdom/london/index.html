London

Choose the London base by the last return, then give each day one district spine.

London is not one compact centre but a chain of walkable districts joined by rail, Underground, bus and river services. A useful plan fixes one timed interior, reads the correct station exit and keeps the rest of the day on a coherent street or river line instead of repeatedly crossing the city.

BEFORE THE FIRST DEPARTURE

Four moving parts control this region.

BASE

Sleep beside the useful departure.

Four nights usually means two or three full sightseeing days after the arrival and departure days are protected: choose Westminster, one museum district and either the Tower/City or Greenwich. Five nights lets you add the other eastern choice or a genuine weather-and-rest day without changing rooms. Six nights can support both east routes or one outer-day trip, but only if that extra journey matters more than a slower London day. Keep one hotel for the stay: a one-night move often trades away the time it saves. Bloomsbury or Holborn suits museum-and-West-End walking; King’s Cross/St Pancras suits a rail arrival and onward train, with more cross-city trips to southern sights; the South Bank/Waterloo area shortens river-side returns but can feel busy around events. Compare the actual evening route to the hotel, not just the daytime map pin.

TRANSFER

Name the whole connection.

Start with the station or terminal that matches the room. Heathrow offers Elizabeth line and Piccadilly line routes into London, but the best interchange depends on whether you are staying near Paddington, Bloomsbury, Westminster or the eastern City; confirm the dated service and step-free path before choosing. Gatwick, Stansted, Luton and London City use different rail links and London arrival points, so do not treat “central London” as one terminal. For sightseeing, Westminster is the shared starting area for Parliament and the Abbey; Russell Square, Holborn and Tottenham Court Road serve different sides of Bloomsbury; Tower Hill is not London Bridge; and Bank–Monument is a large, multi-level interchange. Name the operator, station and exit for each leg, then check the Transport for London map/status board and National Rail for the travel date.

WINDOW

Let current conditions edit the route.

Rain and gusts can arrive in any month, so keep an indoor anchor and a short outside loop in the same district. Summer gives longer evening light but more pressure at timed doors, popular museums and river piers; book the one interior that shapes the day, then verify current entry rules rather than assuming space remains. Short winter daylight argues for ending the South Bank or Greenwich walk at a station before dusk. Parliamentary business, ceremonies, demonstrations, security perimeters and engineering work can change street access or the useful station exit at short notice. As reviewed on 11 October 2026, Royal Museums Greenwich says the Royal Observatory closes from 2 November 2026 for refurbishment; check its current notice if the trip overlaps that date, and do not assume this means every Greenwich museum is closed.

FALLBACK

Keep a complete lower-risk day.

If weather, a closure or a late arrival removes an anchor, shorten the same district instead of crossing town: keep Parliament Square and one bridge; use a prechecked Bloomsbury museum or a shorter Seven Dials walk; or choose the Tower/City day or the Greenwich day, not an improvised chain of both. For Greenwich, retain the low-ground National Maritime Museum/Queen’s House/Cutty Sark option when hill access or weather is poor, but verify which sites are operating on the date. A central hotel base makes the shortened return practical without a second check-in.

COMPARE BEFORE BOARDING

Each route buys something by leaving something else out.

Ceremony-and-crossing clock

Westminster & the South Bank

Gateway: Westminster station is served by the District, Circle and Jubilee lines and sits beside Parliament Square, but the pavement exit is not the visitor entrance. Parliament’s main public entrance for tours is Cromwell Green on St Margaret Street; the Abbey’s own visit instructions govern its entrance. Parliament currently advises allowing time for security searches and reports longer average queues at busy periods, so use its live guidance rather than a fixed transfer estimate. Waterloo is a practical South Bank return; Blackfriars only makes sense if you deliberately extend east.

Sacrifice: A Parliament tour, Abbey visit, ceremonial detours and a South Bank attraction cannot all be equal priorities in one day. One booked door already creates a security and entry block; choosing it means giving up the other interior and keeping the river walk bounded. An exterior day trades interior access for a looser route and more time at bridges and public spaces.

Time: Treat six to eight hours as an editorial day allowance for one interior, a proper break and a bounded South Bank finish; it is not a measured visit time. A shorter exterior circuit can work in roughly three to four hours if the event calendar and walking pace cooperate. Do not attach another major booked site.

Collection-attention register

Bloomsbury, the British Museum & Covent Garden

Gateway: Russell Square on the Piccadilly line is a useful north-side approach; Holborn (Central/Piccadilly) and Tottenham Court Road (Central/Northern/Elizabeth line) serve different southern approaches and onward connections. Treat each as a different start point, not as interchangeable “British Museum” stops. Follow the Museum’s current entrance, bag and room notices; use Covent Garden or Leicester Square only for the south-side finish, after checking service and step-free access for the date.

Sacrifice: The British Museum’s scale makes a short highlight list feel like a whole visit when the internal walking and standing still take time. This plan gives one collection a clear morning block and releases the afternoon for a real break and a single southern street sequence. A second major museum or a matinee must replace part of that sequence, not be stacked on top.

Time: Allow about five to seven hours for a bounded museum visit, a seated break, the southbound street walk and time in Covent Garden. The museum and street allocations are planning estimates; crowds, room availability and walking pace change them. Add a performance only when its entrance and start time are confirmed, and do not add a second major collection.

Tower of London, the City & Greenwich

Gateway: Tower Hill Underground (District/Circle) is the Tower-side approach; Tower Gateway DLR is a separate station, and London Bridge is a different entrance to the wider City. For Greenwich, choose Cutty Sark DLR or Greenwich/Maze Hill rail by the first and last venue, not by a generic “Greenwich” pin. DLR, National Rail and river boats have separate service, pier and ticket rules; confirm the chosen operator and return for the exact date.

Sacrifice: The Tower’s controlled entrance, grounds and internal queues can use a large part of the day; Greenwich then needs its own journey, low-ground museums and possibly a steep park climb. Choosing one district leaves the other for another day but gives the selected visit a real margin. A tower-exterior and City walk is a lighter alternative, not a substitute for a Tower ticket.

Time: Use about five to seven hours as an editorial allowance for a Tower visit and one compact City line, or a separate half-to-full day for Greenwich’s selected museums and park. These are planning estimates, not operator visit times. Do not add the other district, Westminster or Bloomsbury to the same schedule.

REGIONAL ANSWERS

London FAQ

How many nights should I book for London?

Four nights can support two or three full days if arrival and departure are not treated as sightseeing days. Five nights makes room for a fourth district day or a weather reserve. Six nights can add both eastern choices or an outward trip; keep one hotel unless the second base serves a separately planned journey.

Where should I stay for a first visit?

Bloomsbury or Holborn works well for museum and theatre walks; King’s Cross/St Pancras favors rail arrivals and onward trains; Waterloo or the South Bank shortens some river-side returns. Compare the specific hotel-to-station route you will use at night, and do not assume one famous landmark is a useful base for every district.

Should I combine the Tower of London and Greenwich in one day?

Usually choose one anchor. The Tower plus a compact City walk is a full day; Greenwich has its own clustered museums, park and hill. The two districts sit on different operating routes, so combining them cuts the time available at both and adds a transfer that depends on the day’s live service.
