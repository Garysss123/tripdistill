Set the day

Three useful choices, each with a real sacrifice.

01

Tower and nearby City

Use Tower Hill for the Tower’s own entry and visit, then add one compact City line such as St Dunstan-in-the-East and Leadenhall Market. This prioritizes the fortress and urban fabric; Greenwich is another day.

02

Greenwich low ground

Start at Cutty Sark DLR or Greenwich station, then choose the National Maritime Museum, Queen’s House and Cutty Sark according to current entry and available time. This builds a complete river-and-maritime day without the uphill Observatory.

03

Greenwich with the Observatory hill

Choose Greenwich Park and the hill only if the Royal Observatory’s date-specific opening, ticket and access notice supports the visit. At review on 11 October 2026, Royal Museums Greenwich says the Observatory closes from 2 November 2026 for refurbishment; do not infer a reopening date.

ACCESS

Meet the day at its real gateway.

Tower Hill Underground (District/Circle) is the Tower-side approach; Tower Gateway DLR is a separate station, and London Bridge is a different entrance to the wider City. For Greenwich, choose Cutty Sark DLR or Greenwich/Maze Hill rail by the first and last venue, not by a generic “Greenwich” pin. DLR, National Rail and river boats have separate service, pier and ticket rules; confirm the chosen operator and return for the exact date.

SACRIFICE

Know what this route leaves out.

The Tower’s controlled entrance, grounds and internal queues can use a large part of the day; Greenwich then needs its own journey, low-ground museums and possibly a steep park climb. Choosing one district leaves the other for another day but gives the selected visit a real margin. A tower-exterior and City walk is a lighter alternative, not a substitute for a Tower ticket.

TIME WINDOW

Use about five to seven hours as an editorial allowance for a Tower visit and one compact City line, or a separate half-to-full day for Greenwich’s selected museums and park. These are planning estimates, not operator visit times. Do not add the other district, Westminster or Bloomsbury to the same schedule.

PAIR ONLY WHEN USEFUL

Pair the Tower with St Dunstan-in-the-East or Leadenhall Market on the same City line. Pair Greenwich with the National Maritime Museum, Queen’s House, Cutty Sark and—only if open and suitable—the Observatory hill. Choose one pair; keep the other district for another day.

Four checks before the return

Arrive, read the window, commit once, then get back.

Check Historic Royal Palaces’ Tower entry and security guidance, Royal Museums Greenwich’s site-by-site hours and dated Observatory notice, TfL DLR status, National Rail service and any chosen river operator’s named pier and return. None of these pages guarantees same-day availability.

  1. 01Arrive

    Before leaving · select a district

    Check the Tower admission/security instructions or the Royal Museums Greenwich site and ticket notices. If choosing Greenwich, read the current Observatory refurbishment notice first; there is no assumption that the hilltop site is open on every travel date.

  2. 02Read the window

    Tower day · Tower Hill to one City line

    Enter at the Tower’s instructed door and protect a substantial visit block. Afterward, choose either St Dunstan-in-the-East and Leadenhall Market or a direct riverside return; finish at Tower Hill, Monument/Bank or London Bridge according to the last stop.

  3. 03Commit

    Greenwich day · DLR/rail to low ground

    Arrive at Cutty Sark DLR or Greenwich station, then group the National Maritime Museum, Queen’s House and Cutty Sark from their current operating notices. Leave a seated break before deciding whether the park slope is worth adding.

  4. 04Return

    Return · match the final door

    For Greenwich low ground, leave from Cutty Sark DLR, Greenwich or Maze Hill only after checking the live route and walking connection; the Observatory hill adds a climb back down. Use a named river pier only when its current departure and return fit the day. Do not cross back to the Tower as a default finale.

WHEN THE SIGNAL CHANGES

Keep a complete lower-risk day.

If the Tower entry or City service changes, keep a Tower exterior-and-nearby-street day or move the interior to a date with confirmed admission. If the Observatory is closed or the park hill is unsuitable, use Greenwich low ground only after checking the National Maritime Museum, Queen’s House and Cutty Sark individually; each site’s status is separate. Rain or a delayed river boat is a reason to omit the transfer, not to assume a replacement sailing.

Three break points

Change the plan while the return is still strong.

01

A river boat is a separate operating choice

A river-bus connection and a sightseeing cruise are different products and stopping patterns. Confirm the exact operator, pier, departure and return; never build the Tower-to-Greenwich sequence on an assumed sailing.

02

The Observatory is a dated gate

Royal Museums Greenwich currently lists a refurbishment closure from 2 November 2026. Recheck its notice for later dates, and do not describe the whole Greenwich quarter as closed if only one site is affected.

03

The Greenwich Park climb changes the finish

The Royal Observatory sits uphill from the low-ground museums. If rain, fatigue or mobility makes that climb unsuitable, leave the hill out and return from the museum-side station or pier you have checked.

PLANNING ANSWERS

Tower of London, the City & Greenwich FAQ

Can I visit the Tower of London and Greenwich in one day?

It is possible to travel between them, but the guide recommends choosing one as the main day. A Tower visit plus one City walk is already a full plan; Greenwich has its own museums and park. A river boat does not guarantee a convenient connection or enough visit time.

Is the Royal Observatory open for a late-2026 visit?

At editorial review on 11 October 2026, Royal Museums Greenwich says the Observatory closes from 2 November 2026 for refurbishment and does not state a reopening date on the planning page. Recheck the current notice for your travel date; the National Maritime Museum, Queen’s House and Cutty Sark each have separate status.

Which station should I use for Greenwich?

Cutty Sark DLR suits the low-ground maritime quarter; Greenwich and Maze Hill are separate rail approaches. Choose by the first and final venue, current step-free access and the live return rather than assuming one station serves the whole park.
