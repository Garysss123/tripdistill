Kent, Sussex & the South Coast

Choose the city, castle or cliff section first; their gateways do not share one coast corridor.

Brighton, Canterbury, Dover and the Seven Sisters are four different days connected by different rail and bus legs. Brighton is a downhill city-and-seafront plan from its station; Canterbury has two stations on distinct corridors; Dover Priory sits below its hilltop castle; and the Seven Sisters has no railway station. Build the visit around one anchor, name the final bus or return station, and leave chalk paths conditional on wind, tide, daylight and official access notices.

BEFORE THE FIRST DEPARTURE

Four moving parts control this region.

BASE

Sleep beside the useful departure.

Two nights: base in Brighton and give it one full city day; use the other for either a short Eastbourne or Seaford coast visit only if the bus and daylight work. Three nights: keep Brighton for two nights and move to Canterbury for one night if the cathedral and east Kent are priorities, or stay put and make one dedicated Seven Sisters day. Four nights: two Brighton nights plus two Canterbury nights can support one city day, one coast day and one cathedral day without repeating a long east-west transfer; Dover then deserves its own day only if the castle is the main event. A single Brighton base works for a day trip, but do not assume the fast London–Canterbury corridor and the south-coast bus route meet conveniently. Choose lodging near the station for early rail starts or near the old centre for evenings; Brighton station is above the Pavilion and seafront, so test the uphill return against luggage and mobility.

TRANSFER

Name the whole connection.

Use National Rail for the exact date, station and return: Southern serves Brighton and the Eastbourne/Seaford approaches, while Southeastern links Canterbury and Dover through different London and Kent corridors. Canterbury West is the high-speed London St Pancras station; Canterbury East serves another service pattern, so choose the station printed for the actual train. Dover Priory is not at Dover Castle: budget the climb or verify the local bus before calculating admission. For Seven Sisters, the Exceat visitor centre on the A259 is the named bus-and-walk gateway between Brighton, Seaford and Eastbourne; confirm the operating route, stop direction and final return, not just a map pin. Never build the coast day around an unverified last bus or an assumed through-ticket.

WINDOW

Let current conditions edit the route.

On 12 October 2026, the Royal Pavilion published seasonal hours: October–March 10:00–17:15 with last entry 16:30; April–September 09:30–17:45 with last entry 17:00. Check the live venue page because hours and special events can change. Summer brings busier beaches, road approaches and cliff paths; book any timed heritage admission before locking an east Kent day, and still check for event or worship changes. Spring and autumn can suit walking, but wind, rain, visibility and Cuckmere tide determine whether a cliff segment is sensible. Winter makes Brighton, Canterbury and the castle’s interiors better anchors; shorter daylight means replace a long cliff traverse with a signed viewpoint and an earlier train. The operator and park pages differed on whether service 12A was operating in this dated snapshot, so use the live bus planner for the day rather than relying on an old route number.

FALLBACK

Keep a complete lower-risk day.

If exposed paths are unsuitable, do not swap in another headland. Keep Brighton in the Pavilion, museum, covered streets and a short sea view; keep Canterbury in the cathedral precinct and historic lanes; or make Dover Castle the day’s single booked interior if access and the hill connection fit. If an east Kent train or Seven Sisters bus is disrupted, stay in the city where you woke or use the confirmed return toward it. At Cuckmere, follow the signed coastal path around the river: do not try to cross the mouth, and do not descend to the cliff base for a viewpoint.

Brighton Lanes, Pavilion & Seafront

Gateway: Brighton station is about a 15-minute walk uphill from the Royal Pavilion; the Pavilion is around five minutes from the sea. Local buses stop on Old Steine and North Street, while the station, North Laine, the historic Lanes and waterfront are separate points. Pick the route down before arrival and budget for the return climb; after a long beach walk, use a suitable bus rather than assuming the uphill leg is trivial. If step-free access matters, confirm the chosen station exit, bus and venue route directly with their operators.

Sacrifice: The Pavilion, Brighton Museum, two distinct street districts and a long promenade do not fit as full visits in one relaxed day. A Pavilion-led plan gives architecture and a shorter coast; a lanes-and-food plan keeps the interior shallow; a coast-heavy plan trades covered time for weather exposure. Pick west toward the historic beachfront or east toward Madeira Drive, not both, and set a turnaround point before leaving the pier.

Time: Allow five to seven hours for one interior, both central street districts and a short seafront leg, plus your arrival and return. A longer west or east promenade should be the day’s main walk and use the available daylight.

Canterbury Cathedral & Dover Castle

Gateway: Canterbury West is the high-speed London St Pancras station and Canterbury East serves a different Southeastern corridor, including services associated with Victoria and Charing Cross; check the actual origin, station and return in National Rail. The bus station is about a five-minute walk from the cathedral precinct. Dover Priory is below the hilltop castle, not at its gate: allow for the uphill approach or confirm a local bus and its return before booking. Dover Castle’s official dated page listed 10:00–17:00 opening and 16:00 last entry on 12 October 2026; timed slots and advance booking rules can change, so check the current page before setting a train.

Sacrifice: A cathedral precinct visit and Dover Castle each deserve several hours, and both add a separate rail leg. The fast Canterbury West train does not make a same-day castle transfer automatic: two interior admissions compress meals, the cathedral precinct, castle grounds and disruption margin. Choose one deep anchor. If you want both, stay overnight in Canterbury or Dover and use the second day, or keep the second place to a brief exterior stop only when its correct station and return are verified.

Time: Allow a full day for either cathedral or castle, including the station approach and meal. A Canterbury–Dover same-day sampler can consume eight to ten hours but still means a short visit at one anchor; an overnight split gives each a usable opening window.

Seven Sisters & Eastbourne

Gateway: There is no Seven Sisters railway station. The Exceat Visitor Centre is on the A259 between Seaford and Eastbourne; the park says Brighton, Seaford and Eastbourne buses connect there with mainline stations. On the checked operator page, services 12 and 12X were listed; the park page also named 12A, so resolve that discrepancy in the live planner and follow the direction-specific stop and final bus. Official park walking estimates include Exceat to West Dean at about 0.8 km/15 minutes, to the beach about 1.6 km/30 minutes, and to Birling Gap about 5.6 km/2–3 hours; these are route estimates, not a promise of a return service. Do not mistake Exceat for a rail station or the river mouth for a safe crossing.

Sacrifice: A short Exceat–valley–viewpoint out-and-back gives a useful landscape visit and preserves a bus margin; it does not traverse the Seven Sisters ridge. The longer Birling Gap or Beachy Head sections are dedicated exposed walks: the official estimates put Exceat–Birling Gap at 2–3 hours and Beachy Head at 3–4 hours, with your transport legs and rest stops added. A point-to-point route offers more cliff profiles but requires a verified exit at the far end, enough daylight and no expectation of rescuing the plan with a late bus.

Time: Allow five to eight hours including the rail-and-bus approach for a measured Exceat outing. Exceat–Birling Gap or Beachy Head estimates alone are 2–4 hours depending on endpoint; add the onward return, rests and conditions. A full point-to-point coast walk is a dedicated daylight day, not an add-on after Brighton.
