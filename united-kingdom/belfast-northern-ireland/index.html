Belfast, Causeway Coast & Derry

Give Belfast, the Causeway Coast and Derry separate days with their own evidence and return.

Belfast’s civic quarters and Maritime Mile are urban transit days; the Causeway Coast is a rail-and-bus or road corridor; Derry is a separate walled city whose recent history requires careful, site-led interpretation. Belfast, the coast and Derry need separate base, access and return decisions rather than one highlights loop.

BEFORE THE FIRST DEPARTURE

Four moving parts control this region.

BASE

Sleep beside the useful departure.

Four nights is a two-anchor trip: use two nights in Belfast plus two in either Portrush/Bushmills or Derry, and leave the other region for next time. Five nights can support three Belfast nights and two in Derry, with a Causeway day trip only if the dated train-and-bus return works. Give all three areas a real day with seven nights: three in Belfast, two on the north coast and two in Derry. A north-coast night saves backtracking but does not create a frequent evening bus.

TRANSFER

Name the whole connection.

NI Railways reaches Coleraine from Belfast; the Giant’s Causeway connection is a separate Ulsterbus 172 leg, not a station walk. Belfast-to-Derry also has Translink rail and coach choices, but Grand Central, Lanyon Place and Europa Buscentre are different departure points. Recheck the exact service, seasonal bus, final stop and return before fixing a timed visit.

WINDOW

Let current conditions edit the route.

Coast access is a daylight-and-weather decision, not a summer-only guarantee. On 12 October 2026, the National Trust calendar showed the Giant’s Causeway Visitor Centre open 09:00–17:00 and the coastline dawn to dusk; its Causeway Rambler and other buses have their own seasonal dates. The Museum of Free Derry lists shorter weekday hours from 1 November 2026 and last entry one hour before closing. Recheck both calendars, wind and Translink notices for the actual date; book Titanic Belfast or a coast-base room once the route is stable.

FALLBACK

Keep a complete lower-risk day.

If wind, wet basalt or a service change removes the coast, keep the day in Belfast: use the city-centre walk, a confirmed City Hall visit or a prebooked Titanic Belfast slot. If Derry’s wall top is slippery or the museum hours do not fit, use the street-level river and civic route. Do not replace a closed cliff section with another exposed edge.

FOUR ROUTE BLOCKS · CHOOSE TWO, THREE OR ALL FOUR

Keep the city, coast and western-city days in their own corridors.

This is a planning sequence, not a timetable. Four nights means choosing two anchors; a seven-night version can give Belfast, the north coast and Derry separate bases without compressing the last connection.

DAY 01

Belfast civic core

Start at City Hall, read the Donegall Square and Linen Quarter grid, then continue on foot to the Cathedral Quarter and St Anne’s Cathedral. Keep this central route together; choose one museum or guided-history stop rather than adding residential mural streets without context.

DAY 02

Choose the second Belfast district

Use a separate day for the Queen’s Quarter, Botanic Gardens and Ulster Museum, or cross the Lagan to Titanic Belfast, the Slipways and SS Nomadic. Titanic Belfast is a timed anchor with its own visitor map; do not treat it as the next block from City Hall.

DAY 03

Make the Causeway a complete coast day

For the rail-first route, take NI Railways from Belfast to Coleraine and confirm Ulsterbus 172 to the Visitor Centre. Choose the official access route, then add at most one connected coast stop. An overnight in Portrush or Bushmills suits an early start; a Belfast day return is only for a timetable that leaves a secure margin.

DAY 04

Give Derry its own arrival and return

Take a current Translink rail or coach service from Belfast, then build one walk from the walled centre through a chosen wall section to the Bogside or across the Peace Bridge. Stay one or two nights if the Museum of Free Derry is a priority; do not make its last entry depend on a same-day cross-region transfer.

COMPARE BEFORE BOARDING

Each route buys something by leaving something else out.

Civic-grid-to-shipyard route book

Belfast Civic Quarter & Maritime Mile

Gateway: Your ticket may name Belfast Grand Central or Lanyon Place; choose the first walking leg and lodging from the actual station printed on it. Walk from City Hall through Donegall Place toward St Anne’s, and treat the route east over Queen’s Bridge to Titanic Quarter as a distinct leg. Check Translink Metro or Glider options against the exact entrance rather than an old station pin.

Sacrifice: City Hall and the central quarters reward a slow walking half-day; the Queen’s Quarter and Titanic Quarter each pull the route in a different direction. A one-day visit should choose one cross-town branch, not collect all three districts.

Time: Use one full day for a central walk plus one district branch. Titanic Belfast is a timed anchor that can occupy most of its own day; do not stack it with a distant museum visit.

Giant’s Causeway & the Causeway Coast

Gateway: The National Trust’s current access guidance names regular trains from Belfast or Derry to Coleraine, then Ulsterbus 172 to the Causeway. Other buses, including the Causeway Rambler, have separate and seasonal patterns. This is a rail-plus-bus route, not a station-to-stones walk; confirm both legs and the final return on Translink for your date.

Sacrifice: The free coastal walk and the booked Visitor Experience are different choices: the ticket adds the exhibition, guided interpretation and visitor facilities, while the main on-site car park is reserved for ticket holders and members. A full trail day plus a distillery or second landmark trades away return margin.

Time: Treat the Causeway as a full day from Belfast because the rail, bus and walking legs are separate. From a north-coast base, use the saved hours for one route and interpretation rather than assuming several landmarks fit.

Derry Walls, Bogside & River Foyle

Gateway: Visit Derry lists Translink train services from Belfast and bus services from Belfast and Dublin. Use the actual rail or coach stop shown by Translink as your arrival pin; the Foyle separates the Waterside from the walled centre, and the Peace Bridge is one named pedestrian crossing. Check the current map and access before choosing that route. The wall walk includes uneven surfaces and stairs.

Sacrifice: A full wall circuit, the Museum of Free Derry and the Peace Bridge are separate pieces of a meaningful day. Choose one wall arc plus one interpretation site, or trade the ramparts for the street-level river route; do not rush testimony and community history to complete a loop.

Time: Protect a full day for a wall section, one major interpretation site and the river. A focused Guildhall-and-Peace-Bridge walk is the shorter alternative; arrival from Belfast is a separate corridor, not part of the walking estimate.

REGIONAL ANSWERS

Belfast, Causeway Coast & Derry FAQ

How many nights do I need for Belfast, the Causeway Coast and Derry?

Four nights is better spent on two anchors than three rushed day trips. Five nights can fit Belfast and Derry with a Causeway excursion if the dated return is practical. Seven nights—three Belfast, two north coast and two Derry—gives each system a dedicated day and protects the transfers.

Can I use Belfast as the only base?

Yes for a city-led trip and a single Causeway day, if the NI Railways-to-Coleraine and Ulsterbus 172 connection fits both ways. Derry is a separate full-day rail or coach corridor; an overnight is the better choice when its walls and museum are central to the trip.

What should I book before I travel?

Choose the route and base first, then check Titanic Belfast’s timed visitor information, National Trust access and any north-coast lodging. Recheck seasonal bus service, Derry museum hours, weather and event notices before departure.
