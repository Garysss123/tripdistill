Set the day

Three useful choices, each with a real sacrifice.

01

Conwy depth

Use the castle, walls and compact medieval town as the main block. This gives the strongest heritage argument and simple rail access.

02

Llandudno and Great Orme

Prioritize promenade, pier and one tramway, cable or signed headland route. It adds coast and height but depends on weather and operation.

03

Two-town sampler

Use a shorter castle visit plus Llandudno promenade, linked by rail. It gains contrast but sacrifices the complete walls or headland.

ACCESS

Meet the day at its real gateway.

Conwy station is small and close to the walls; Llandudno and Llandudno Junction are different stations. Great Orme attractions start beyond the main station. Choose the town order and final rail station before entering the castle or headland.

SACRIFICE

Know what this route leaves out.

A deep castle, full walls and Great Orme circuit exceed one relaxed day. Choosing Conwy sacrifices headland depth; choosing Llandudno gives up some medieval interpretation.

TIME WINDOW

Allow a full day for one deep town plus a short second stop. Conwy or Llandudno alone needs five to seven hours.

PAIR ONLY WHEN USEFUL

Combine Conwy with Llandudno by one rail move. Keep Yr Wyddfa, Caernarfon and Anglesey for separate days.

Castle, mountain and coast board

Use North Wales & Eryri as an operating system, not a pin collection.

Conwy and Llandudno, Eryri’s mountain gateways, and Caernarfon with Anglesey form three distinct North Wales systems. Rail follows the coast, while mountain routes and castles add buses, seasonal railways, parking and weather. One named gateway per day is the practical standard.

BASE

Four to six nights supports a coast town, one Eryri day and a western castle or island branch. Llandudno Junction is useful for rail; Betws-y-Coed, Llanberis and Caernarfon open different mountain approaches and are not interchangeable bases.

CONNECTION

Transport for Wales serves the north coast and Conwy Valley; buses and heritage railways continue to mountain gateways. Yr Wyddfa routes start from different points. Anglesey road and rail access does not automatically reach every coast site.

WEATHER WINDOW

Mountain wind, cloud, snow and rain matter year-round. Summer creates parking and summit-rail demand; winter requires lower routes and short daylight. Coast and castle days remain useful fallbacks.

LOWER-RISK DAY

Use Conwy, Llandudno, Caernarfon or a rail-valley town when mountain access fails. Do not replace a closed Yr Wyddfa route with another high summit without a new forecast and transport plan.
