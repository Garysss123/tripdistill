Fen waterline register

Use Cambridge & East Anglia as an operating system, not a pin collection.

Cambridge, Ely and Norwich sit on useful rail lines, but their stations, historic cores and surrounding fen or coast landscapes operate at different scales. A good plan distinguishes a walkable academic city from a cathedral stop and a wider East Anglian excursion with its own onward connection.

BASE

Use Cambridge for two nights and an Ely day; add a third or fourth night before choosing Norwich or a north-coast branch. The Norwich–Sheringham rail line and onward coast bus are separate connections, so protect a return and choose one branch per day rather than stacking several Norfolk villages into a short stay. Norwich can also function as a separate base for northern Norfolk.

CONNECTION

Cambridge station is south-east of the colleges, Cambridge North serves a different edge and Ely station sits below the cathedral core. Greater Anglia routes are useful but do not remove the final walk or rural bus; check engineering works, especially at weekends.

WEATHER WINDOW

College access follows academic use, while fen paths, river activities and coast services respond to weather and daylight. Summer brings punting and visitor pressure; winter gives clear urban days but shorter rural returns.

LOWER-RISK DAY

When college access or an outdoor branch fails, Cambridge and Norwich both have complete museum-led alternatives. Keep the fallback in the same rail city rather than chasing a distant village.
