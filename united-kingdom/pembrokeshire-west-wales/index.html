Pembrokeshire & West Wales

Use one harbour, saint’s city or rail coast at a time; the path does not shorten the roads.

Tenby, St Davids and Aberystwyth are three different bases linked by long road or rail legs, not one compact coastal loop. Tenby and Aberystwyth have rail stations; St Davids does not. On 12 October 2026, the county listed the summer 400, 403 and 404 coastal buses as ended for the season; only the reduced 387/388 winter Coastal Cruiser was listed, Thursdays and Saturdays between Pembroke, Angle, Bosherston, Stackpole and Freshwater East. That winter service does not reach St Davids or Tenby. Match every coast walk to its actual return.

BEFORE THE FIRST DEPARTURE

Four moving parts control this region.

BASE

Sleep beside the useful departure.

Four nights in Tenby keeps the south-coast trip on one walkable base but leaves St Davids and Ceredigion for another visit. Six nights split between Tenby and St Davids (for example, three each) gives the west coast its own overnight and avoids a long same-day return; verify the bus or road transfer before booking St Davids. About eight nights can add Aberystwyth as a third base, but treat the northbound move as a transfer day and check the Transport for Wales route rather than trying to combine it with a coastal walk. Book summer rooms and any timed heritage, boat or rail tickets before fixing the route.

TRANSFER

Name the whole connection.

Tenby and Aberystwyth sit on different TfW rail branches; St Davids requires a bus or road connection. The coastal bus calendar matters: as checked 12 October 2026, 400 Puffin Shuttle, 403 Celtic Coaster and 404 Strumble Shuttle had passed their 27 September summer end; winter 387/388 runs only Thursday and Saturday on the Pembroke–Angle–Bosherston–Stackpole–Freshwater East corridor. Check the current timetable and exact stop pair for each walking stage. Do not use a coast-path segment to bridge a missing bus or assume a boat lands where it departs.

WINDOW

Let current conditions edit the route.

Pembrokeshire County Council’s dated 12 October 2026 notice said winter Coastal Cruiser 387/388 service ran from 1 October 2026 to 28 May 2027 on Thursdays and Saturdays; 400/403/404 summer routes ended 27 September. The current Caldey visitor page says the island is closed for the season and reopens Easter 2027. Cliff and heritage-rail operation is separate: check the operator for the visit date. In winter, short light, exposed path conditions and reduced buses make a town day the sensible fallback; in summer, reserve lodging and timed activities early and still confirm tides and sea state.

FALLBACK

Keep a complete lower-risk day.

Use Tenby, St Davids or Aberystwyth as a complete town day. If a boat or coast segment fails, do not replace it with another distant exposed headland.

Cathedral-to-cliff weather card

St Davids & the Pembrokeshire Coast

Gateway: St Davids has no railway station. The Cathedral Close, Bishop’s Palace approach, High Street and Oriel y Parc visitor centre make a compact city zone; St Non’s Chapel/Caerfai and boat harbours are separate outward legs, not extensions of that stroll. County information checked 12 October 2026 listed the 400 St Davids–Marloes route only as a summer service ending 27 September, and the winter 387/388 runs on the far-south Pembroke–Angle–Stackpole corridor. Confirm a current local bus, booked transfer or car for the exact coast start and return; do not walk the A487 verge to fill a gap.

Sacrifice: A deep cathedral visit, substantial coast walk and boat trip do not fit one resilient day. Choosing the coast sacrifices palace and city depth; choosing the city gives up marine reach but protects the return.

Time: Allow a full day from a Pembrokeshire base. The city and Cathedral Close can occupy four to six hours; a coast or boat branch needs its own transport buffer and most remaining daylight. From Tenby or Haverfordwest, avoid planning a long coast walk on the same day as the inbound transfer.

Harbour-tide-island manifest

Tenby, Caldey & the South Coast

Gateway: From Tenby station, follow the signed streets into the walled centre, then descend through Castle Hill to the harbour; return uphill through the town rather than depending on the beach at every tide. The Caldey ticket office is in Castle Square and the licensed boats depart Tenby Harbour, but the operator says sailings depend on sea conditions. Its live page checked 12 October 2026 says the island is closed for the season and reopens Easter 2027; the 2026 window ended 30 September. That is not a confirmed 2027 timetable. Saundersfoot or other coast-path endpoints need their own dated bus/rail return.

Sacrifice: Caldey, a long coast walk and relaxed Tenby are separate priorities. Choosing the island sacrifices path distance; choosing the coast gives up the sailing but creates a land-based return.

Time: Allow five to six hours for Tenby’s walled core, harbour and one tide-safe shore section from a nearby base. A Caldey sailing, when the operator publishes a season, uses a separate substantial block plus check-in and sea-delay margin; do not combine it with a long walk to Saundersfoot.

Rail-end coast-and-hill board

Aberystwyth & the Ceredigion Coast

Gateway: Aberystwyth station opens onto the town-centre streets; head west toward the castle remains and promenade, or walk north along the seafront to Constitution Hill and the cliff-railway base. The National Library is uphill from town and is a distinct return climb. The Vale of Rheidol platform is beside the standard-gauge station area but uses its own booking and timetable. Pick the hill or the valley before setting out; the long Cambrian mainline return is not a safe tight connection after a heritage service.

Sacrifice: A complete heritage-rail journey, library visit and long seafront day do not fit comfortably. Choosing the valley sacrifices town depth; staying in Aberystwyth gives up the inland branch but protects flexibility.

Time: Allow a full town day from a closer base, or stay overnight after a long cross-Wales rail arrival. At the 12 October 2026 check, the Vale of Rheidol homepage advertised daily steam trains only until 1 November; its 24-mile Devil’s Bridge round trip takes 2 hours 45 minutes, so treat it as the day’s main branch and book the exact date in advance.
