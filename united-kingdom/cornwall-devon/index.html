Cornwall & Devon

Choose a rail spine, then add only the coast or moor return you can protect.

This is a long peninsula, not one compact day-trip region. The dependable rail spine reaches Exeter St David’s, Plymouth, Truro and Penzance; the St Ives and Maritime branches serve separate west and south-coast bases. St Erth is the transfer for the St Ives branch; Truro is the Maritime Line gateway to Perranwell, Penryn, Penmere, Falmouth Town and Falmouth Docks. Dartmoor is a different Exeter-based day: the Dartmoor Line reaches Okehampton, but the station, town interchange and open-moor trailheads are not interchangeable. Choose one corridor per day and preserve its final return.

BEFORE THE FIRST DEPARTURE

Four moving parts control this region.

BASE

Sleep beside the useful departure.

For four to six nights, use two bases only if the trip genuinely crosses the peninsula: St Ives or Penzance for west Cornwall, Falmouth or Truro for the Fal, and Exeter for the city and Dartmoor Line. St Ives keeps the harbour and Tate close but does not make Land’s End a casual extension; Penzance is the stronger western rail-and-bus interchange. Falmouth suits the Maritime Line and harbour; Truro is the better central rail junction. Exeter is the resilient Devon base; Okehampton is useful for a deliberately moor-focused overnight. A two-night trip should stay on one line rather than spend a daylight block changing hotels.

TRANSFER

Name the whole connection.

Use named rail corridors: the St Erth–Lelant–Carbis Bay–St Ives branch; Truro’s Maritime Line through Perranwell, Penryn and Penmere to Falmouth Town and Falmouth Docks; and the Exeter St David’s–Crediton–Okehampton Dartmoor Line. They solve different days, not one coast circuit. Transport for Cornwall’s route 16/16A timetable has inland trips via Gulval and Nancledra and selected 16A journeys via Gurnards Head Hotel and Zennor Turn; the variants make a date- and direction-specific check essential. A ferry from Falmouth is a separate water crossing, not a rail replacement, and rural Dartmoor buses vary by day and season.

WINDOW

Let current conditions edit the route.

Atlantic wind, rain, surf and tide affect cliff paths and ferry operation throughout the year. Fal River lists the St Mawes passenger ferry as year-round but says sailings remain subject to weather and tide; its Enterprise Boats Falmouth–Trelissick–Malpas product is seasonal, with the 2026 window shown by the operator. Do not carry that 2026 season into a later year. Short winter daylight also makes an exposed Penwith or moor walk a poor late start. Before leaving the base, compare the dated operator timetable, live disruption, tide, forecast and sunset; if the last useful connection cannot be protected, choose the town day.

FALLBACK

Keep a complete lower-risk day.

Keep one complete indoor-and-town alternative beside each base: Tate and the harbour at St Ives; the National Maritime Museum Cornwall and Falmouth town; or Exeter Cathedral, RAMM and the quays. If a boat is cancelled, remain on its departure shore. If a rural bus return is uncertain, shorten to a signed walk from the rail station or make the city the whole day. Never treat a taxi as the planned rescue for a remote coastal or moor route.

COMPARE BEFORE BOARDING

Each route buys something by leaving something else out.

Branch-line and headland clock

St Ives & the Penwith Coast

Gateway: Change at St Erth for the St Ives branch; its named stops are Lelant, Carbis Bay and St Ives, whose station stands above town. Transport for Cornwall route 16/16A connects Penzance and St Ives: the operator timetable shows inland journeys via Gulval and Nancledra plus selected 16A journeys at Gurnards Head Hotel and Zennor Turn. Do not assume every 16A run serves the coastal stops; choose a journey for the exact date and direction and confirm the return before leaving St Ives. The separate 14/18 corridor links St Ives/Penzance with Truro via Hayle, Camborne and Redruth. None of these claims a Land’s End connection; verify a separate current route and stop if adding that area.

Sacrifice: Tate St Ives, the Hepworth garden and a substantial coast extension compete for the same daylight. Choosing art gives up the distant headland; choosing Penwith gives up collection depth and a relaxed harbour meal.

Time: Allow a full day from a nearby base for St Ives plus one chosen extension. The harbour and one gallery need at least four to five hours without Penwith.

Estuary vessel decision card

Falmouth Harbour & the Roseland

Gateway: From Truro the Maritime Line calls at Perranwell, Penryn, Penmere, Falmouth Town and Falmouth Docks; select the stop for the first anchor rather than assuming all are interchangeable. Fal River’s St Mawes Ferry runs between Prince of Wales Pier, Falmouth and The Quay, St Mawes; the operator describes it as year-round, with a reduced winter pattern, but all sailings are subject to tide and weather. Enterprise Boats is a separate seasonal Falmouth–Trelissick–Malpas route; the operator also lists special high-tide cruises to Truro for 2026. Bus 496 from Malpas is a separately charged onward connection. King Harry Ferry is a vehicle crossing between Feock and Philleigh, not the Falmouth passenger ferry. Confirm a named outward and return product, its landing and live sailing status before making a two-shore day.

Sacrifice: A full museum, Pendennis and a relaxed Roseland crossing do not share one comfortable day. Crossing the estuary sacrifices collection or castle depth; staying in Falmouth gives up the village but removes the marine cutoff.

Time: Allow six to eight hours for Falmouth and one ferry branch, or five hours for the museum-and-harbour route. Roseland depth is better with an overnight or separate day.

Cathedral-to-moor weather gate

Exeter & the Dartmoor Edge

Gateway: For a car-free northern edge, board the Dartmoor Line from Exeter St David’s via Crediton. The line serves both Okehampton Station and the newer Okehampton Interchange: the station is the named start for the Yes Tor and High Willhays walk, while the interchange is on the town’s eastern side and is not the trailhead. The walk is a serious open-moor route, not a station stroll: carry a map, set a daylight turn-back, check weather and heed military red flags. Haytor Visitor Centre is a different eastern gateway at Haytor Vale, with the lower car park and walks to Haytor Rocks and the old Granite Tramway; use it by car or only with a dated bus/tour plan verified for that day. Do not infer a direct Exeter-to-Haytor bus from the rail map.

Sacrifice: A full Exeter heritage day and a meaningful moor walk are separate days. Choosing the moor sacrifices cathedral and museum depth; staying in the city gives up open landscape but avoids an exposed, timetable-bound return.

Time: Allow a full day for Exeter or one Dartmoor gateway. A city-plus-short-rural combination works only when the branch schedule creates a clear five-to-seven-hour window.
