Set the day

Three useful choices, each with a real sacrifice.

01

Exeter city depth

Prioritize the cathedral, Roman and medieval streets, museum and quays. This is the strongest all-weather choice and needs no rural connection.

02

Rail-to-moor gateway

Use a current Dartmoor Line or other rail gateway and a bounded local walk. It adds landscape while preserving a train-based return.

03

Bus or guided moor day

Reach a deeper moor village or trail by verified bus, tour or car. This gives broader scenery but depends most on weather and sparse frequency.

ACCESS

Meet the day at its real gateway.

For a car-free northern edge, board the Dartmoor Line from Exeter St David’s via Crediton. The line serves both Okehampton Station and the newer Okehampton Interchange: the station is the named start for the Yes Tor and High Willhays walk, while the interchange is on the town’s eastern side and is not the trailhead. The walk is a serious open-moor route, not a station stroll: carry a map, set a daylight turn-back, check weather and heed military red flags. Haytor Visitor Centre is a different eastern gateway at Haytor Vale, with the lower car park and walks to Haytor Rocks and the old Granite Tramway; use it by car or only with a dated bus/tour plan verified for that day. Do not infer a direct Exeter-to-Haytor bus from the rail map.

SACRIFICE

Know what this route leaves out.

A full Exeter heritage day and a meaningful moor walk are separate days. Choosing the moor sacrifices cathedral and museum depth; staying in the city gives up open landscape but avoids an exposed, timetable-bound return.

TIME WINDOW

Allow a full day for Exeter or one Dartmoor gateway. A city-plus-short-rural combination works only when the branch schedule creates a clear five-to-seven-hour window.

PAIR ONLY WHEN USEFUL

Combine Exeter’s cathedral with the quays, or a rail gateway with one signed local walk. Keep the deeper moor, Plymouth and Cornwall for separate days.

Peninsula weather log

Use Cornwall & Devon as an operating system, not a pin collection.

This is a long peninsula, not one compact day-trip region. The dependable rail spine reaches Exeter St David’s, Plymouth, Truro and Penzance; the St Ives and Maritime branches serve separate west and south-coast bases. St Erth is the transfer for the St Ives branch; Truro is the Maritime Line gateway to Perranwell, Penryn, Penmere, Falmouth Town and Falmouth Docks. Dartmoor is a different Exeter-based day: the Dartmoor Line reaches Okehampton, but the station, town interchange and open-moor trailheads are not interchangeable. Choose one corridor per day and preserve its final return.

BASE

For four to six nights, use two bases only if the trip genuinely crosses the peninsula: St Ives or Penzance for west Cornwall, Falmouth or Truro for the Fal, and Exeter for the city and Dartmoor Line. St Ives keeps the harbour and Tate close but does not make Land’s End a casual extension; Penzance is the stronger western rail-and-bus interchange. Falmouth suits the Maritime Line and harbour; Truro is the better central rail junction. Exeter is the resilient Devon base; Okehampton is useful for a deliberately moor-focused overnight. A two-night trip should stay on one line rather than spend a daylight block changing hotels.

CONNECTION

Use named rail corridors: the St Erth–Lelant–Carbis Bay–St Ives branch; Truro’s Maritime Line through Perranwell, Penryn and Penmere to Falmouth Town and Falmouth Docks; and the Exeter St David’s–Crediton–Okehampton Dartmoor Line. They solve different days, not one coast circuit. Transport for Cornwall’s route 16/16A timetable has inland trips via Gulval and Nancledra and selected 16A journeys via Gurnards Head Hotel and Zennor Turn; the variants make a date- and direction-specific check essential. A ferry from Falmouth is a separate water crossing, not a rail replacement, and rural Dartmoor buses vary by day and season.

WEATHER WINDOW

Atlantic wind, rain, surf and tide affect cliff paths and ferry operation throughout the year. Fal River lists the St Mawes passenger ferry as year-round but says sailings remain subject to weather and tide; its Enterprise Boats Falmouth–Trelissick–Malpas product is seasonal, with the 2026 window shown by the operator. Do not carry that 2026 season into a later year. Short winter daylight also makes an exposed Penwith or moor walk a poor late start. Before leaving the base, compare the dated operator timetable, live disruption, tide, forecast and sunset; if the last useful connection cannot be protected, choose the town day.

LOWER-RISK DAY

Keep one complete indoor-and-town alternative beside each base: Tate and the harbour at St Ives; the National Maritime Museum Cornwall and Falmouth town; or Exeter Cathedral, RAMM and the quays. If a boat is cancelled, remain on its departure shore. If a rural bus return is uncertain, shorten to a signed walk from the rail station or make the city the whole day. Never treat a taxi as the planned rescue for a remote coastal or moor route.

Four checks before the return

Arrive, read the window, commit once, then get back.

    Complete the urban anchor

    Use the cathedral and green or the museum and quays as one city line. For a moor day, keep this step short and reach the branch train on time.

    Pass the weather gate

    Reach the gateway before the final comfortable service and allow any change at St David’s. Use Exeter as the fallback evening, not another rural detour.

WHEN THE SIGNAL CHANGES

Keep a complete lower-risk day.

If Dartmoor weather or transport fails, use Exeter Cathedral, the Royal Albert Memorial Museum and the quays as a complete city day. If a city interior closes, retain the walls, green and river route.

Three break points

Change the plan while the return is still strong.

01

Dartmoor is not one stop

Rail and bus gateways serve different edges and trails. Name the exact start instead of navigating to “Dartmoor.”

02

Open moor conditions change quickly

Mist, wind, rain and saturated ground can erase easy-looking routes. Carry a proper route and turn back early.

03

Exeter stations are not interchangeable

Central is higher and closer to the core; St David’s is lower and serves mainline connections. Preserve the transfer time.

PLANNING ANSWERS

Exeter & the Dartmoor Edge FAQ

Which station starts the Okehampton moor walk?

Okehampton Station is the walk gateway identified for Yes Tor and High Willhays. Okehampton Interchange is a separate station on the town’s eastern side; do not confuse it with the trail start. Check the Dartmoor Line timetable and your return before setting out.

Can I take the train to Haytor?

No rail station serves Haytor Visitor Centre. It is at Haytor Vale, about three miles west of Bovey Tracey. Plan a car journey or verify a dated seasonal bus or tour; if that connection is not confirmed, choose the Okehampton rail gateway or remain in Exeter.

What is the safer bad-weather alternative?

Make Exeter the full day: Cathedral Green and Exeter Cathedral, the Royal Albert Memorial Museum when open, and the quays. Open-moor visibility, wind, wet ground, military red flags and short daylight can make the walk unsuitable even while the train is running.
