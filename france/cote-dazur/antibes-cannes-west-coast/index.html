Antibes & Cannes West Coast

TER links Antibes and Cannes, whose stations sit near their centers. Start with Antibes’s market, old walls and one museum or Cannes’s Forville–Suquet–Palais axis; Lérins boats and Cap d’Antibes need their own day.

01

Antibes old town and Picasso

From Antibes station, walk to the old town, Marché Provençal and ramparts, then reserve the Picasso Museum if it fits. Finish around Port Vauban; the Cap d’Antibes coastal walk is a separate branch.

02

Cannes market, Suquet and bay

Walk from Cannes station through Rue Félix-Faure/Forville to Le Suquet, then the old port and Palais des Festivals exterior. Continue along only one Croisette segment; event security can change the open route.

03

A selective Antibes–Cannes pair

Use TER once in each direction, keeping one short old-town loop and meal in each. Skip the Picasso interior, island boats and Cap d’Antibes to leave a reliable station margin.

Access first

Know where the day actually begins.

TER from Nice-Ville reaches Antibes and Cannes; use the dated SNCF timetable for both directions and check works before buying an island boat or museum slot. Antibes station is west of the old town; Cannes station opens toward Rue Félix-Faure and the center. Port Vauban, Cap d’Antibes trailheads and Cannes island quays are separate access points.

Trade-off

Choose what the route leaves out.

A Lérins boat, Cap d’Antibes walk, Picasso Museum and full Cannes circuit exceed a day. Choose one town in depth, or pair compact street loops and give up the island/peninsula and long interior visit.

Time envelope

Allow five to seven hours for Antibes or Cannes in depth. A compact two-town pair needs most of a day including two station walks and a meal; an island or Cap d’Antibes excursion replaces the second town.

Combine only when it helps

Combine Antibes and Cannes by TER only as short district loops. For more time, stay overnight in Antibes and use it as a west-coast base; keep Nice, Monaco and Èze on separate dates.

WEST COAST WALKING ZONES

Keep each town beside its own station spine

The TER journey is the link between the towns; within each town, use one station-to-center walk and one planned finish instead of crossing back for every landmark.

Antibes old town

From the station, head toward Place Général-de-Gaulle and Marché Provençal, then follow the walled lanes to the Picasso Museum, ramparts and Port Vauban.

Cap d’Antibes

Treat the cape path and beaches as a separate walking day with a named bus/starting point, water, weather check and return. It is not an extension of the old-town loop.

Cannes center

Walk Rue Félix-Faure to Forville, Le Suquet and the old port, then use a bounded Croisette stretch. Check event security before approaching the Palais.

Lérins Islands

Choose one island excursion from the current boat operator and protect its return; do not combine both towns and the islands in one day.

Four-stage operating line

Arrive, read the place, commit, then protect the return.

Check the dated TER timetable/works, market calendar, Picasso or exhibition entry, Cannes event restrictions, island operator and sea state, Cap d’Antibes path status and the final train.

  1. 01Arrive

    Antibes: station to old town

    From Antibes station, follow the center approach toward Place Général-de-Gaulle and Marché Provençal; continue through the old lanes to the Picasso Museum/ramparts and Port Vauban. Check the museum’s own entrance and hours.

  2. 02Read the place

    Choose one finish in Antibes or board TER

    For a one-town day, finish the ramparts and harbor loop near the station. For the pair, use TER to Cannes only after checking the departure board and arrival platform.

  3. 03Commit

    Cannes: market to Suquet and port

    From Cannes station, walk Rue Félix-Faure toward Marché Forville, climb into Le Suquet, then descend to the old port and Palais des Festivals. Follow event barriers and use public streets.

  4. 04Return

    Close on the Croisette or return rail

    Walk one bounded Croisette section, then return to Cannes station with time in reserve. If visiting only Antibes, replace this branch with a reserved Cap d’Antibes/boat plan and a confirmed return.

Lower-risk fallback

Keep the day useful when the headline plan disappears.

If Cannes has event barriers or a museum visit changes, stay on public streets in the old town and market district or use Antibes as the full day. If sea state cancels the Lérins boat, keep the land-side Cannes or Antibes route; do not substitute an unplanned coastal hike.

Three failure points

Change the plan before the problem compounds.

01

Festival and congress setup change Cannes

Security, build-out and private events may close the Palais, port edges or Croisette sections. Check the official event/access notice on the exact date.

02

Museum doors have their own calendar

Check the Picasso Museum or selected exhibition page for dated hours, ticket conditions and closures before arranging the TER. No current opening or ticket inventory is assumed.

03

The island is a separate day

Confirm the operator’s sailing, sea condition and last return before going to the quay. A boat replaces Cannes or Antibes sightseeing; do not append it after both towns.

Planning answers

Antibes & Cannes West Coast FAQ

Can I do Antibes and Cannes in one day?

Yes as a compact rail pair: choose one short district loop and a meal in each. Skip the Lérins Islands, Cap d’Antibes hike and long museum visits, and leave time for the actual TER return.

Can I add the Lérins Islands after Cannes?

Treat the boat as the main outing, not a late add-on after Cannes. Check the operator’s date-specific departure and return, sea state and quay access.

What changes during Cannes events?

Festival and congress setup can change security perimeters and access around the Palais, port and Croisette. Check Cannes’s official event notice and follow barriers on the day.
