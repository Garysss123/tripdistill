Èze, Monaco & Menton

TER connects Èze-sur-Mer, Monaco–Monte-Carlo and Menton, but Èze Village requires a separate uphill bus or steep path. Build the day around one vertical transfer and a confirmed coastal return.

Decision this guide solvesChoose the hilltop village, one Monaco district or a Monaco–Menton rail pair. The three stops are connected by different vertical and rail decisions, not one continuous flat promenade.

Èze Village as the main stop

Use a Lignes d’Azur or ZOU! bus that is listed to Èze Village for your date, or reach Èze-sur-Mer by TER and take the signed Nietzsche Path only if fit and conditions allow. Reserve the garden only after checking its official calendar.

02

Monaco, one level at a time

Choose the Rock and old town, the Oceanographic Museum, or Monte-Carlo—not all three. Use signed public lifts and buses to bridge levels, and save the station exit for the return.

03

Monaco and Menton by rail

Use TER for a bounded two-town day: one Monaco district, then Menton’s old town or one garden. This plan deliberately omits Èze and its extra climb.

Access first

Know where the day actually begins.

TER serves Nice-Ville, Èze-sur-Mer, Monaco–Monte-Carlo and Menton; check the official SNCF timetable and planned works for the exact date. Èze Village is uphill from Èze-sur-Mer, so confirm a bus to the village itself or assess the steep signed path before boarding. In Monaco, exits connect different levels and districts; note the exit and lift/bus link for your chosen destination.

Trade-off

Choose what the route leaves out.

Èze Village plus Monaco and Menton is a rushed three-stop list. Give Èze its own half-to-full day, or pair one Monaco district with Menton by rail; adding a second Monaco interior or garden means dropping the other town.

Time envelope

Allow five to seven hours for Èze Village or one Monaco district once transfers, stairs and a meal are counted. A selective Monaco–Menton pair needs most of a day; adding Èze makes three stops and should be another date.

Combine only when it helps

Combine Monaco and Menton by TER with one chosen district in each, or give Èze Village its own day from Nice. Keep Antibes/Cannes on a separate westbound trip.

THREE EAST COAST CONTRACTS

Plan vertically before choosing the train

The railway follows the sea; the village, principality districts and gardens sit beyond the station platform. Use the transfer that matches the place you actually booked.

Èze Village

Use a bus that the live Lignes d’Azur/ZOU! planner shows to Èze Village, or TER to Èze-sur-Mer only with a suitable uphill plan. The village lanes and garden involve slopes and steps.

Monaco–Monte-Carlo

Choose the Rock/old town, port-side museum or Monte-Carlo. Match the station exit, public lift or bus to that district and check event controls.

Menton

Walk from Menton station toward the old town and seafront; add one garden only after confirming its current date-specific hours and entry.

Rail return

Keep the last useful TER return visible before the visit begins. A disruption or event may make a planned cross-border direction slower than the map suggests.

Four-stage operating line

Arrive, read the place, commit, then protect the return.

Check dated TER service/works, bus destination and last return, Nietzsche Path condition, garden or museum booking, Monaco events and weather before leaving Nice.

  1. 01Arrive

    Choose the vertical transfer first

    For Èze, check a date-matched bus to the hilltop or take TER to Èze-sur-Mer only with a viable uphill and return plan. For the rail towns, select Nice-Ville → Monaco–Monte-Carlo or Menton before leaving.

  2. 02Read the place

    Walk one district, not the whole map

    In Èze, follow public village lanes and signed visitor paths. In Monaco, choose the Rock, port/Oceanographic area or Monte-Carlo using the named station exit and public lifts; in Menton, connect the station to the old town.

  3. 03Commit

    Put the reservation at the center

    Book or verify one garden, museum or palace-area visit for the exact date. Keep a second paid interior optional until the return trains and event notice are clear.

  4. 04Return

    Protect the return leg

    Finish beside Monaco–Monte-Carlo or Menton station, or reach the confirmed Èze bus stop early. Recheck SNCF works, the live transit departure and event barriers before starting the last downhill segment.

Lower-risk fallback

Keep the day useful when the headline plan disappears.

If the Èze bus is unsuitable or the trail is wet, remain on the rail coast in Monaco or Menton; do not improvise a road shoulder walk. If Monaco has event restrictions, choose a compact Menton or Nice route instead of bypassing barriers.

Three failure points

Change the plan before the problem compounds.

01

Èze requires an uphill contract

Èze-sur-Mer and Èze Village are different stops. Confirm a village-bound bus or judge the steep Nietzsche Path honestly; do not rely on a taxi being available at the station.

02

Monaco is a vertical station

The station exits and public lifts lead toward different districts. Use the current station map and allow time for stairs, escalators or an event detour.

03

Gardens, events and rail works move

Check the official garden/museum calendar, Monaco event access and TER notice for the chosen date. No current opening, ticket inventory or future train time is guaranteed here.

Planning answers

Èze, Monaco & Menton FAQ

Does Èze-sur-Mer station serve Èze Village?

No. Èze-sur-Mer is on the coast; the hilltop village needs a separately checked bus, car transfer or strenuous signed climb. Verify the destination shown in the live planner.

Can I combine Èze and Monaco in one day?

It is possible only as a deliberately short day with a confirmed bus and one Monaco district. For a first visit, give Èze or Monaco the main block and keep Menton for a separate rail day.

Should I book the Exotic Garden?

Check its official date-specific opening and ticket instructions before building the route around it. If unavailable, the public village lanes remain a shorter visit, but do not assume the garden is open.
