Nice & the Côte d’Azur

Use the coast train for one direction at a time.

Treat Nice as its own walkable city day, then choose one eastern or western coast contract at a time. TER reaches the principal towns, but Èze Village sits above Èze-sur-Mer and Monaco’s districts sit on different levels; station arrival is only the first leg.

01 · Base

Sleep where the useful departures are.

Two nights fit Nice and one focused excursion. Four nights give Nice a full day, one eastbound day and one westbound day, with the arrival and departure edges protected. Five or six nights make a slower two-base stay possible: Nice for the eastern rail corridor and Antibes for the western towns. Stay near Nice-Ville/Jean Médecin for transfers, in Vieux Nice for early market access with more stairs and noise, or in Antibes near its station for a west-coast morning. Changing hotels only pays when it removes a real return journey.

02 · Transfer

Solve the complete last mile.

Use TER from Nice-Ville for Antibes, Cannes, Èze-sur-Mer, Monaco–Monte-Carlo and Menton; check the dated SNCF timetable, planned works and last return together. Lignes d’Azur tram line 2 links the airport, Jean Médecin and Port Lympia; line 1 crosses the central city. Èze Village needs a separately checked Lignes d’Azur or ZOU! bus from the correct stop, or a demanding climb from Èze-sur-Mer. No current fare, headway or future departure is assumed.

03 · Season

Let current conditions edit the map.

Summer brings heat, full trains, beach crowding and event barriers; plan the exposed seafront or hill early, carry water and keep an indoor option. Spring and autumn suit longer walks but garden calendars, sea conditions and daylight still vary. Monaco race week and major Nice or Cannes events can change access and prices; check event dates before booking lodging or a timed visit.

04 · Fallback

Keep a real lower-risk day.

If a coast train, bus, boat or hill route changes, keep the day in the town already reached: Nice has tram-linked museums and shaded central streets; Antibes has the walled old town and museums; Cannes has Le Suquet and the market quarter; Menton has its compact old town. Do not add a second coast direction to recover a missed connection.

Four-night Nice base · sample sequence

Use the coast as two separate rail directions.

Keep this order flexible around arrival, weather, reservations and the operator’s dated timetable. Treat each numbered day as a full-day choice, not a timed connection promise.

Day 1 · Nice

Settle near Nice-Ville/Jean Médecin or a tram stop. Walk Place Masséna, Cours Saleya and Vieux Nice, then choose either Castle Hill or one museum; do not force both after an arrival.

Day 2 · East by TER

Choose Èze Village with a confirmed uphill bus and return, or Monaco with one district and one booked interior. Add Menton only to a bounded Monaco–Menton rail day, leaving Èze for another trip.

Day 3 · West by TER

Choose Antibes in depth, Cannes in depth, or a compact Antibes–Cannes pair. Keep Lérins Islands or Cap d’Antibes as a separate branch that replaces the second town.

Day 4 · Buffer or second base

Use the day for a weather-shifted coast route, a slower Nice district, or a hotel move to Antibes only if it helps the next departure. Keep the final night close to the station or airport route you will actually use.

Before you choose

Three routes, three different sacrifices.

Market-to-belvedere city ribbon

Nice Old Town, Promenade & Hills

Access: From Nice-Ville, follow Avenue Jean-Médecin to the center or take tram line 1 toward Place Masséna, Garibaldi and the port. Tram line 2 runs from the airport through Jean Médecin to Port Lympia. Start the walk after dropping luggage; check current stop works and the airport operator’s dated service notice.

Trade-off: Choose one elevation: Castle Hill above the old town or a tram/bus trip to Cimiez. A major museum, full beach block and both hills make a second day more useful than a faster checklist.

Time: Allow five to seven hours for Vieux Nice, one hill and a meal. A bounded old-town/seafront loop takes about half a day; add Cimiez or a substantial museum as a separate transit block.

Vertical-coast transfer ladder

Èze, Monaco & Menton

Access: TER serves Nice-Ville, Èze-sur-Mer, Monaco–Monte-Carlo and Menton; check the official SNCF timetable and planned works for the exact date. Èze Village is uphill from Èze-sur-Mer, so confirm a bus to the village itself or assess the steep signed path before boarding. In Monaco, exits connect different levels and districts; note the exit and lift/bus link for your chosen destination.

Trade-off: Èze Village plus Monaco and Menton is a rushed three-stop list. Give Èze its own half-to-full day, or pair one Monaco district with Menton by rail; adding a second Monaco interior or garden means dropping the other town.

Time: Allow five to seven hours for Èze Village or one Monaco district once transfers, stairs and a meal are counted. A selective Monaco–Menton pair needs most of a day; adding Èze makes three stops and should be another date.

Two-port rail promenade

Antibes & Cannes West Coast

Access: TER from Nice-Ville reaches Antibes and Cannes; use the dated SNCF timetable for both directions and check works before buying an island boat or museum slot. Antibes station is west of the old town; Cannes station opens toward Rue Félix-Faure and the center. Port Vauban, Cap d’Antibes trailheads and Cannes island quays are separate access points.

Trade-off: A Lérins boat, Cap d’Antibes walk, Picasso Museum and full Cannes circuit exceed a day. Choose one town in depth, or pair compact street loops and give up the island/peninsula and long interior visit.

Time: Allow five to seven hours for Antibes or Cannes in depth. A compact two-town pair needs most of a day including two station walks and a meal; an island or Cap d’Antibes excursion replaces the second town.

Regional answers

Nice & the Côte d’Azur FAQ

How many nights should I book in Nice?

Two nights can support a city day and one coast day. Four nights allow a Nice day plus one eastern and one western excursion without turning the travel edges into sightseeing. Add a night for a second town base or an island/peninsula day.

Should I stay in Nice or Antibes?

Use Nice for the eastern corridor, airport tram and multiple city days. Consider Antibes for a west-coast stay focused on the old town, Cannes or the Lérins boats; compare the full hotel change and station walk before splitting.

Can I reach Èze Village by train?

TER reaches Èze-sur-Mer, not the hilltop village. Check the current Lignes d’Azur or ZOU! bus to Èze Village, or plan the steep signed path only if its condition and your mobility make it suitable.

Can I see Nice, Èze, Monaco, Menton, Antibes and Cannes in four days?

Not with useful time in each. Use Nice plus one eastbound and one westbound day; combine Monaco with Menton or Antibes with Cannes only when you accept a shorter visit in both.
