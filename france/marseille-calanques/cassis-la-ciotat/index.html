Access first

Know where the day actually begins.

Marseille Tourism lists bus 78 for Cassis; check its actual start, stop, timetable and return for your date. SNCF TER offers live Marseille–Cassis and Marseille–La Ciotat planners, but those stations are inland from their ports. Confirm the local bus or taxi from the station to Quai Saint-Pierre or Port Vieux before booking a boat. Do not use a map’s straight-line walk as the last-mile plan.

Trade-off

Choose what the route leaves out.

Choose Cassis or La Ciotat for the core of the day, then add one boat, cliff or town-history layer. Both ports plus a long boat trip and Route des Crêtes turn the day into transfers and leave little margin for a missed service.

Time envelope

Allow a full day for one port, its station transfer and either one boat circuit or one confirmed viewpoint. A town-only visit still needs four to six hours including both transport legs; check real services before treating that as a day-trip fit.

Combine only when it helps

Pair Cassis with one boat circuit or a confirmed Cap Canaille viewpoint. Pair La Ciotat with Port Vieux and cinema history. Keep the second town and Marseille museums for another day.

THREE PORT-AND-CLIFF ZONES

Station, quay and viewpoint are different pins.

Choose one port and solve the connection twice. Cassis has the boat quay and the Cap Canaille road; La Ciotat has Port Vieux and its own station transfer.

Cassis station to Quai Saint-Pierre

Use a date-specific TER search or Marseille Tourism’s line 78 information, then verify which stop reaches the port and when it runs back. The railway station is inland; confirm a local bus or taxi rather than assuming you have arrived at the harbor.

Cassis port to Cap Canaille

Keep the port walk and boat departure around Quai Saint-Pierre. The Route des Crêtes is a separate exposed road toward La Ciotat, subject to fire and wind closures; travel it only with an open-road plan.

La Ciotat station to Port Vieux

Use the exact SNCF TER station listing, then precheck the local bus/taxi to the old port. Make Port Vieux and the town’s cinema-history sites the core walk; add a coastal path only after checking local access.

Four-stage operating line

Arrive, read the place, commit, then protect the return.

Check bus 78 or the exact TER planner, the station-to-port local link, boat departure and cancellation terms, Route des Crêtes daily fire/wind closure and the return service.

  1. 01Arrive

    Choose the port before the ticket

    Compare Cassis port with La Ciotat Port Vieux, then use the live TER planner or bus 78 page for the actual terminal and return. Leave room for the inland station-to-port transfer.

  2. 02Read the place

    Walk one waterfront zone

    In Cassis, keep the port and old streets together around Quai Saint-Pierre. In La Ciotat, use Port Vieux and the town center as the walkable core; do not count the rail station as waterfront access.

  3. 03Commit

    Add one coast layer

    Board a confirmed boat from that same port, choose a currently open Cap Canaille viewpoint by vehicle/tour, or stay with La Ciotat’s cinema-and-port history. Do not combine all three.

  4. 04Return

    Return through the same last mile

    Use the prechecked bus/taxi to the actual station and protect a margin for queues or service change. If the link is disrupted, stay in town and rebook rather than gamble on a final train.

Lower-risk fallback

Keep the day useful when the headline plan disappears.

If the sea cancels a boat, stay in Cassis around Quai Saint-Pierre or use La Ciotat’s Port Vieux and town-history walk. If fire, wind or a road barrier closes Route des Crêtes, do not set off on foot along it. If station-to-port transport fails before departure, keep the Marseille museum day instead of chasing the connection.

Planning answers

Cassis, Cap Canaille & La Ciotat FAQ

Is Cassis station in the port?

No: plan the station-to-port leg separately. Marseille Tourism lists bus 78 to Cassis, and the TER page provides a date-specific train planner; confirm the actual bus stop or taxi and the return before selecting a boat.

Can I combine Cassis and La Ciotat in one day?

It is possible only if the live bus/train transfers and one chosen activity line up, but it leaves little margin. This guide recommends one port plus one boat, viewpoint or town-history layer.

Can I walk the Route des Crêtes if it is closed to cars?

Do not treat a vehicle closure as walking permission. Fire or wind restrictions can close the road and adjacent area; obey the current prefecture/commune notice and barrier.
