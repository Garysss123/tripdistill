Bastia & Cap Corse

Bastia connects ferry, rail and a listed airport shuttle; Cap Corse is a slower road excursion. Choose Erbalunga, Macinaggio or the full D80 loop only after checking the date-specific outward and return legs.

01

Bastia without a car

From the ferry or rail node, walk Terra Vecchia and the old port, then climb once to Terra Nova/citadel and the museum only if open. Bastia Airport lists a city shuttle to the station; verify the current stop and transfer before a flight.

02

East-coast Cap Corse

Take the D80 north from Bastia toward Erbalunga, Sisco and Macinaggio, then stop at villages you have chosen. The listed Macinaggio–Bastia bus is described as school-term weekday service, not an all-day visitor shuttle; use a car or prebooked tour unless the current date-specific outbound and return both work.

03

A full peninsula circuit

Drive or book a guided day that deliberately covers both D80 coasts, with Nonza/Centuri or other stops selected in advance. Curves, village parking and slower photo stops make the loop a full day; stay north overnight if the return to Bastia or ferry margin would otherwise be tight.

Access first

Know where the day actually begins.

Bastia has separate airport, ferry, rail-station and old-port nodes; the airport authority lists a city shuttle to Bastia station. The Cap Corse D80 loop is a slow coastal road rather than a hop-on circuit. The official Visit Corsica listing describes the Macinaggio–Bastia bus as Monday–Friday during school term, so it cannot be assumed for weekends, holidays or convenient village hops. Check the current operator timetable for both legs, or choose a rental car/prebooked tour; do not depend on a taxi appearing in a small village.

Trade-off

Choose what the route leaves out.

Base in Bastia for ferry/rail and an easy city evening; sleep in Macinaggio when the north Cape or coastal path is the point; use a rental car for a full west-and-east D80 loop. Bastia plus one east-coast village can fit a long day, but a full peninsula loop needs its own day and preferably an overnight. Spend the last night in Bastia before a ferry or flight rather than combining the loop with departure.

Time envelope

Allow four to six hours for Bastia’s port-to-citadel walk and a meal. Give one D80 coast a full day; a full Cape loop is a long day from Bastia or an easier two-day plan with a Macinaggio overnight.

Combine only when it helps

Combine Bastia with Erbalunga for a bounded east-coast day only when road/bus return is solved. Keep the west coast and a full Cap Corse loop separate from ferry departure day and from Calvi/Saint-Florent transfers.

BASTIA TO CAP CORSE

A city walk, an east-coast outing and the peninsula loop are three different plans

The D80 skirts steep coast and villages with slow turns. Pick an end point and return contract before leaving Bastia; the local bus listing has limited school-term service.

Bastia, no car

Airport shuttle terminates at the station; the ferry and old port are separate arrival points. Store bags first, walk Terra Vecchia/old port and climb to Terra Nova, then return to your actual rail or sailing node.

East D80: Erbalunga to Macinaggio

Use a car or tour for a flexible day. The official Macinaggio–Bastia listing says Monday–Friday during school term; verify its current exact route and return before considering it for a visit.

West Cape and full circuit

Plan a separate D80 day for the west-coast villages and headlands. Leave time for narrow-road speeds, legal parking, wind and daylight; do not use the loop as a pre-ferry transfer.

Where to sleep

Choose Bastia for ferry/rail, Macinaggio for a Cape-focused overnight and early walk, or a mid-route lodging only if a car is already arranged. Move back to Bastia the night before a fixed departure.

BASE, TRANSFER AND WALK

Sleep by the leg you need, then choose one Cape direction.

The port-to-citadel walk is independent of the airport shuttle; a Cap Corse outing needs its own vehicle or a date-matched return.

Three to four nights

Use Bastia for ferry and rail access, the old port and one full east-coast outing. With four nights, add a Macinaggio night only if the north Cape or a coastal walk is the main goal; otherwise keep Bastia as the single base and skip the full peninsula loop. Return to Bastia the night before a fixed ferry or flight.

Named transport legs

The airport operator lists Bastia Airport–Bastia gare at about 25 minutes; verify the current stop and departure on your date. Bastia gare is the rail node, while the ferry terminal and old port are different stops. For Cap Corse, D80 runs north through Erbalunga and Sisco toward Macinaggio; the published Macinaggio–Bastia bus is school-term weekdays, so confirm both legs or use a booked car or tour. No frequency or convenient village hop is assumed.

City walk sequence

Start at Place Saint-Nicolas or your actual ferry/rail arrival, leave luggage at the lodging, then walk through Terra Vecchia to the old port. Climb once by public streets to Terra Nova and the citadel; choose a church or museum only if open. Descend by a different street toward the station or port you actually need. Do not turn the hill walk into a bag-carrying transfer.

Season and fallback

Summer adds heat, narrow-road traffic and demand for guided outings, so book required transport and check parking before choosing a far Cape overnight. School holidays do not make a school-term bus universal; verify the service calendar. Wind, fire restriction or a missed return means stay in Bastia for its center and an open indoor visit, not an improvised coastal drive after dark.

Four-stage operating line

Arrive, read the place, commit, then protect the return.

Check airport shuttle, ferry check-in, current Macinaggio–Bastia bus operating days and exact return, D80 road notices, selected village parking, Cap Corse coastal-path/fire access and museum opening.

  1. 01Arrive

    Choose the travel node

    On arrival, confirm whether you are at the airport, ferry port or Bastia station and the onward transfer. Store luggage before walking; the old port is the city center, not the airport.

  2. 02Read the place

    Walk Bastia from Terra Vecchia uphill

    Follow the old port and market edge through public lanes, then climb to the citadel/Terra Nova and choose one open museum or church. Return by a different public street rather than carrying bags up the stairs.

  3. 03Commit

    Commit to an east or west Cape road

    For a short north excursion, select the D80 east coast to Erbalunga and a further stop only if the return is certain. For a full loop, arrange the vehicle/tour, fuel, parking stops and daylight before leaving Bastia.

  4. 04Return

    Sleep near the next transport

    Return to Bastia with ample ferry check-in or airport time. If the trip continues north, an overnight around Macinaggio avoids forcing the loop back after dark; verify the next day’s return separately.

Lower-risk fallback

Keep the day useful when the headline plan disappears.

If wind, rain, road work, fire restrictions or the limited bus service defeats the Cape plan, keep a Bastia day: old port, Terra Nova and a currently open museum/church. If the ferry is delayed, stay near the port/station and keep luggage and check-in arrangements flexible instead of starting a distant road trip.

Three failure points

Change the plan before the problem compounds.

01

The bus listing is not an all-day Cape service

The official Macinaggio–Bastia description is school-term weekdays. Verify service on your date and both legs; it is not a dependable weekend peninsula loop.

02

D80 driving is slow and exposed

Narrow curves, limited shoulders, wind and village traffic reward few planned stops. Do not count every headland as a quick pull-off.

03

Ferry and coast access have separate rules

Follow ferry check-in/vehicle instructions, and check local fire/erosion closures before the customs tower, coastal path or any exposed route.
