Access first

Know where the day actually begins.

Regional coach 338 is shown by Castelnaud as a summer Sarlat–Castelnaud-la-Chapelle link; verify the date, stop and return in the Nouvelle-Aquitaine schedule search before using it. At Beynac, use the designated village parking: the lower parking-to-château walk takes about 15 minutes uphill. Gabares Norbert sails from and returns to Port de la Malartrie in La Roque-Gageac; check its current departures and river notices.

Trade-off

Choose what the route leaves out.

Beynac and Castelnaud both involve steep historic access; combining both plus a village and boat usually turns a scenic day into a transfer race. Pick one château, then either the La Roque-Gageac boat or a lower riverside walk. A canoe route has separate launch, endpoint and shuttle logistics.

Time envelope

Allow a full day for one castle, one village and a river activity. A castle-and-village route without paddling needs five to seven hours.

Combine only when it helps

Combine Beynac with a nearby river stop or Castelnaud with one neighboring village. Keep the Vézère caves and Sarlat market separate.

Regional operating model

Fit this route into Dordogne & Périgord Noir, not into an isolated checklist.

Sarlat, the Vézère prehistoric sites and the Dordogne’s cliff villages belong to one broad Périgord trip, but not to one compact day. Rail gets you to Sarlat or Les Eyzies; it does not put you at Lascaux, Font-de-Gaume or a hilltop château. Pick a base around the first two nights, book any timed cave before lodging, then give each valley a full day with its return already solved.

Base strategy

For three nights, sleep in Sarlat and choose only two full days: Sarlat plus either the Vézère or the castle valley. Four nights suit one base—Sarlat for the market and southern castles, Les Eyzies for early cave entry—and allow three complete touring days between separate arrival and departure days. Use one of those three days as a weather or transport buffer only if you are willing to drop an anchor. Five nights can split two nights in Sarlat and three in Les Eyzies only when timed visits and the luggage transfer justify moving; otherwise keep one room and book the last mile.

Transfer system

TER line D33/L33 links Bordeaux, Bergerac and Sarlat; TER line L34 connects Périgueux, Les Eyzies and Sarlat on its route. Seasonal regional coaches list line 337 Sarlat–Les Eyzies, 338 Dordogne Valley and 339 Vézère Valley; their operating dates and onward links change, and some coaches require advance reservation. No train or coach stop is a promise of a cave shuttle. Check the Nouvelle-Aquitaine timetable search, exact stop, reservation rule and return; prebook a driver or tour where the final leg is absent.

Season gate

Sarlat’s food market is Wednesday and Saturday morning; the Saturday market is larger, while Thursday’s organic market and winter truffle schedule are seasonal. Regional coach 338 to Castelnaud is shown as a summer service, and the other valley coaches have date-bound calendars. Cave slots, château openings, canoe launches, river levels, heat and harvest traffic also move with season. Check the dated operator calendar, attraction ticket and river notice together before buying a non-refundable room.

Regional fallback

If a cave slot, seasonal coach, driver or river operator drops out, keep the day in the base town: Sarlat’s market-and-heritage loop or Les Eyzies’ National Museum of Prehistory is a real alternative. Replace a missed river activity with one open château reached by confirmed transport; do not patch rural gaps with an assumed taxi or an unverified bus connection.

Four-stage operating line

Arrive, read the place, commit, then protect the return.

Check line 338 dates and return, Beynac parking/approach, the château calendar, Gabares Norbert or canoe booking, river-level notice and driver pickup for the exact travel date.

  1. 01Arrive

    Select the transport branch

    For Castelnaud, test seasonal line 338 against your date-specific Sarlat departure and return. For Beynac or Domme, arrange the vehicle/driver and legal parking before leaving Sarlat.

  2. 02Read the place

    Visit one château

    At Beynac, allow the designated lower parking’s roughly 15-minute uphill walk and use the marked village approach. At Castelnaud, check the museum calendar and parking entrance before climbing.

  3. 03Commit

    Add a named river option

    From La Roque-Gageac, book Gabares Norbert at Port de la Malartrie for its listed round trip, or use a licensed canoe operator only after confirming the endpoint and shuttle.

  4. 04Return

    Close at the same agreed return

    Reach the bus stop or driver pickup before the final planned departure; check the river operator’s last return and current water restrictions before adding a paddle.

Lower-risk fallback

Keep the day useful when the headline plan disappears.

If coach 338 does not run or the return is weak, keep Sarlat or book a driver rather than attempting an improvised taxi chain. If river levels cancel the boat or canoe, retain one open château and village walk; for steep climbs, choose a lower riverside stop and confirm its accessible path.

Three failure points

Change the plan before the problem compounds.

01

Cliff villages are steep

Cobbles, heat and stairs require footwear, water and a conservative route.

02

Coach 338 is seasonal

The castle’s own access page describes a summer link. Do not treat it as year-round; check the operator’s date-specific timetable and any reservation requirement.

03

Parking pressure affects residents

Use designated areas and never enter narrow village lanes to save a climb.
