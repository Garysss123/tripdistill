Sarlat Market & Old Town

01

Wednesday or Saturday market

The city lists food stalls from 08:00 to 13:00; Saturday also has a broader market. Arrive early, keep an open walking lane and choose one heritage stop after the busiest period.

02

Non-market heritage loop

Walk from Place du Peyrou and La Boétie’s house to Saint-Sacerdos Cathedral, the Lantern of the Dead and Sainte-Marie’s covered market hall. Its panoramic lift has its own seasonal hours and ticket; check before relying on it.

Sarlat as rural base

Keep the town for early or evening hours and reserve the main day for one valley circuit with transport already arranged.

Access first

Know where the day actually begins.

Sarlat station is south of the historic core. Check TER D33/L33 or the SNCF itinerary for your date, then walk into town with luggage kept out of the market lanes; drivers should use signed public parking outside restricted streets. Do not rely on a taxi appearing for the return.

Trade-off

Choose what the route leaves out.

The food market is a morning commitment, while the old-town walk and lift need their own time. Adding Lascaux or a castle creates a second transport contract and usually turns the day into a rushed transfer rather than a Sarlat visit.

Time envelope

Allow five to seven hours for the market, old-town walk and meal; reserve a separate day for caves or castles. A non-market landmark loop needs about three to four hours, plus the station transfer.

Combine only when it helps

Pair the morning market with the named old-town loop only. Save Montignac/Lascaux, Les Eyzies and the Dordogne castles for full days with a confirmed return.

Regional operating model

Fit this route into Dordogne & Périgord Noir, not into an isolated checklist.

Sarlat, the Vézère prehistoric sites and the Dordogne’s cliff villages belong to one broad Périgord trip, but not to one compact day. Rail gets you to Sarlat or Les Eyzies; it does not put you at Lascaux, Font-de-Gaume or a hilltop château. Pick a base around the first two nights, book any timed cave before lodging, then give each valley a full day with its return already solved.

Base strategy

For three nights, sleep in Sarlat and choose only two full days: Sarlat plus either the Vézère or the castle valley. Four nights suit one base—Sarlat for the market and southern castles, Les Eyzies for early cave entry—plus three full days and a weather or transport buffer. Five nights can split two nights in Sarlat and three in Les Eyzies only when timed visits and the luggage transfer justify moving; otherwise keep one room and book the last mile.

Transfer system

TER line D33/L33 links Bordeaux, Bergerac and Sarlat; TER line L34 connects Périgueux, Les Eyzies and Sarlat on its route. Seasonal regional coaches list line 337 Sarlat–Les Eyzies, 338 Dordogne Valley and 339 Vézère Valley; their operating dates and onward links change, and some coaches require advance reservation. No train or coach stop is a promise of a cave shuttle. Check the Nouvelle-Aquitaine timetable search, exact stop, reservation rule and return; prebook a driver or tour where the final leg is absent.

Season gate

Sarlat’s food market is Wednesday and Saturday morning; the Saturday market is larger, while Thursday’s organic market and winter truffle schedule are seasonal. Regional coach 338 to Castelnaud is shown as a summer service, and the other valley coaches have date-bound calendars. Cave slots, château openings, canoe launches, river levels, heat and harvest traffic also move with season. Check the dated operator calendar, attraction ticket and river notice together before buying a non-refundable room.

Regional fallback

If a cave slot, seasonal coach, driver or river operator drops out, keep the day in the base town: Sarlat’s market-and-heritage loop or Les Eyzies’ National Museum of Prehistory is a real alternative. Replace a missed river activity with one open château reached by confirmed transport; do not patch rural gaps with an assumed taxi or an unverified bus connection.

Four-stage operating line

Arrive, read the place, commit, then protect the return.

Check the City of Sarlat market calendar, lift tickets and seasonal hours, TER D33/L33 for the exact date, the station walk and designated parking before departure.

  1. 01Arrive

    Start at Place du Peyrou

    On Wednesday or Saturday, reach the market early and keep Place de la Liberté clear for vendors. On another day, begin at Place du Peyrou and La Boétie’s house.

  2. 02Read the place

    Walk the medieval center

    Continue to Saint-Sacerdos Cathedral and the Lantern of the Dead, then turn toward the former Sainte-Marie church, now the covered market hall.

  3. 03Commit

    Choose market or viewpoint time

    Eat and shop before 13:00 on a food-market day, or check the former church’s panoramic lift hours and tickets for a compact view.

  4. 04Return

    Return through the south edge

    Walk back toward the station or your designated parking. Use TER D33/L33 only after checking the day’s final useful departure; skip a distant add-on if it makes the return fragile.

Lower-risk fallback

Keep the day useful when the headline plan disappears.

If it is not a market day, follow the Place du Peyrou–cathedral–Lantern–Sainte-Marie walk and confirm the lift or a guided visit. If rain closes the viewpoint or rail is disrupted, keep the day within the old town and lodging base instead of adding a one-way rural taxi.

Three failure points

Change the plan before the problem compounds.

01

Market hours are not interchangeable

The Wednesday and Saturday food markets run in the morning; the wider Saturday market and seasonal Thursday/truffle markets follow separate calendars. Check the City of Sarlat listing for the date.

02

The lift is a seasonal attraction

The Sainte-Marie panoramic lift operates on its own calendar and may not be open when the covered hall is. Confirm its ticket and hours before planning around the viewpoint.

03

Food names need informed buying

Ask about origin and season rather than assuming every stall product is local or currently harvested.
