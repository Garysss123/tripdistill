Dordogne & Périgord Noir

Choose a base and valley before choosing the headline sights.

Sarlat, the Vézère prehistoric sites and the Dordogne’s cliff villages belong to one broad Périgord trip, but not to one compact day. Rail gets you to Sarlat or Les Eyzies; it does not put you at Lascaux, Font-de-Gaume or a hilltop château. Pick a base around the first two nights, book any timed cave before lodging, then give each valley a full day with its return already solved.

01 · Base

Sleep where the useful departures are.

For three nights, sleep in Sarlat and choose only two full days: Sarlat plus either the Vézère or the castle valley. Four nights suit one base—Sarlat for the market and southern castles, Les Eyzies for early cave entry—and allow three complete touring days between separate arrival and departure days. Use one of those three days as a weather or transport buffer only if you are willing to drop an anchor. Five nights can split two nights in Sarlat and three in Les Eyzies only when timed visits and the luggage transfer justify moving; otherwise keep one room and book the last mile.

02 · Transfer

Solve the complete last mile.

TER line D33/L33 links Bordeaux, Bergerac and Sarlat; TER line L34 connects Périgueux, Les Eyzies and Sarlat on its route. Seasonal regional coaches list line 337 Sarlat–Les Eyzies, 338 Dordogne Valley and 339 Vézère Valley; their operating dates and onward links change, and some coaches require advance reservation. No train or coach stop is a promise of a cave shuttle. Check the Nouvelle-Aquitaine timetable search, exact stop, reservation rule and return; prebook a driver or tour where the final leg is absent.

03 · Season

Let current conditions edit the map.

Sarlat’s food market is Wednesday and Saturday morning; the Saturday market is larger, while Thursday’s organic market and winter truffle schedule are seasonal. Regional coach 338 to Castelnaud is shown as a summer service, and the other valley coaches have date-bound calendars. Cave slots, château openings, canoe launches, river levels, heat and harvest traffic also move with season. Check the dated operator calendar, attraction ticket and river notice together before buying a non-refundable room.

04 · Fallback

Keep a real lower-risk day.

If a cave slot, seasonal coach, driver or river operator drops out, keep the day in the base town: Sarlat’s market-and-heritage loop or Les Eyzies’ National Museum of Prehistory is a real alternative. Replace a missed river activity with one open château reached by confirmed transport; do not patch rural gaps with an assumed taxi or an unverified bus connection.

Four-night Périgord plan

Keep one base, then give each valley its own day.

This five-day calendar fits four nights when Day 1 and Day 5 are partial travel days, leaving Days 2–4 for three complete touring days. If arrival or departure removes another day, add a night or drop a valley. Book the cave slot and last-mile transfer before fixing the room; a three-night trip should drop one valley rather than rush through all three.

Day 1 · Arrival and Sarlat orientation

Check into a walkable Sarlat base and keep the arrival evening light. If you reach town early, use Place du Peyrou, Saint-Sacerdos Cathedral, the Lantern of the Dead and the former Sainte-Marie church market hall as a short orientation loop; confirm your return to the lodging before a long dinner.

Day 2 · Give Sarlat a full day

If it is Wednesday or Saturday, start with the food market; otherwise follow Place du Peyrou, Saint-Sacerdos Cathedral, the Lantern of the Dead and the former Sainte-Marie church market hall. Check the station or driver return before a long dinner.

Day 3 · Choose the Vézère anchor

Use TER L34 to Les Eyzies when its date-specific service fits, then visit the National Museum of Prehistory and one pre-booked original site with a confirmed local transfer. Or use Sarlat as the base for a separately booked Montignac/Lascaux IV day. Do not combine both branches without checking their connection.

Day 4 · Dordogne river and one château

Choose Beynac or Castelnaud, not both by default. Use the summer line 338 only if it runs on the exact date and has a workable return; otherwise arrange a car, driver or tour. Pair the hill visit with La Roque-Gageac at river level only when your transfer or named boat departure is confirmed.

Day 5 · Departure and return buffer

Keep your final morning for luggage collection and the station transfer, with a margin. Do not make a rural attraction your last stop before a fixed train unless the full return transfer is booked. If the weather or a missed connection removes a day, drop one planned valley or add a night; do not count the departure day as a full sightseeing day.

Before you choose

Three routes, three different sacrifices.

Market-day limestone clock

Sarlat Market & Old Town

Access: Sarlat station is south of the historic core. Check TER D33/L33 or the SNCF itinerary for your date, then walk into town with luggage kept out of the market lanes; drivers should use signed public parking outside restricted streets. Do not rely on a taxi appearing for the return.

Trade-off: The food market is a morning commitment, while the old-town walk and lift need their own time. Adding Lascaux or a castle creates a second transport contract and usually turns the day into a rushed transfer rather than a Sarlat visit.

Time: Allow five to seven hours for the market, old-town walk and meal; reserve a separate day for caves or castles. A non-market landmark loop needs about three to four hours, plus the station transfer.

Timed-cave evidence ledger

Vézère Valley Prehistory

Access: TER line L34 serves Les Eyzies on its Périgueux–Sarlat route; regional coach 337 is listed seasonally between Sarlat and Les Eyzies, while 339 serves the Vézère Valley. Neither is a guaranteed cave shuttle. Lascaux IV is in Montignac, reached by a separate road transfer; reserve a driver, operator or car and confirm both directions before buying a timed ticket.

Trade-off: Les Eyzies and Montignac are separate day plans: choose the National Museum of Prehistory plus one officially booked original site around Les Eyzies, or Lascaux IV and its interpretation in Montignac. The official Lascaux visit averages 1–1.5 hours, with 2–3 hours recommended for the whole center; leave other timed visits for another day.

Time: Allow a full day for one major cave experience and one contextual site. Two towns or several timed caves need another day.

River-bank castle selector

Dordogne Valley Castles & Cliff Villages

Access: Regional coach 338 is shown by Castelnaud as a summer Sarlat–Castelnaud-la-Chapelle link; verify the date, stop and return in the Nouvelle-Aquitaine schedule search before using it. At Beynac, use the designated village parking: the lower parking-to-château walk takes about 15 minutes uphill. Gabares Norbert sails from and returns to Port de la Malartrie in La Roque-Gageac; check its current departures and river notices.

Trade-off: Beynac and Castelnaud both involve steep historic access; combining both plus a village and boat usually turns a scenic day into a transfer race. Pick one château, then either the La Roque-Gageac boat or a lower riverside walk. A canoe route has separate launch, endpoint and shuttle logistics.

Time: Allow a full day for one castle, one village and a river activity. A castle-and-village route without paddling needs five to seven hours.

Regional answers

Dordogne & Périgord Noir FAQ

How many nights make the Dordogne workable?

Four nights allow one Sarlat or Les Eyzies base and three complete days: the town, one Vézère day and one Dordogne château day. With three nights, choose only two full-day themes; five nights allow a split base if the timed cave and luggage transfer support it.

Can I tour the valleys without a car?

Some rail and seasonal coach links serve Sarlat and Les Eyzies, including routes 337, 338 and 339 on date-dependent calendars. They do not guarantee a link to every cave, château or return. Confirm exact stops and both directions, reserve on-demand coaches where required, or book a driver/tour.

When should I visit Sarlat’s market?

The city lists food markets on Wednesday and Saturday mornings, with Saturday also hosting the wider market. Thursday organic and winter truffle markets follow separate seasonal calendars; confirm the current hours and location before building the day.
