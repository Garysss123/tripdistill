Vézelay, Fontenay & Abbey Country

Vézelay from an Avallon base

Use Mobigo coach 119 from Dijon toward Avallon only when the dated service fits, then book the separate road transfer to Vézelay and back. Walk the rising village street to the basilica; do not mistake Avallon for the monument entrance.

02

Fontenay from Montbard

Take TER to Montbard TGV station, then pre-book a taxi or arrange the official visitor-office bicycle option for the 6 km last mile. Give the abbey, cloister and valley the day’s main block.

A two-abbey day is a long road itinerary, not a public-transport combination. Use a confirmed driver or guided route, compare each site’s current visit window and keep time for the return.

Access first

Know where the day actually begins.

The two access plans are different. Mobigo lists coach 119 between Dijon and Avallon, but Avallon is not the Vézelay Basilica: book the onward road transfer and return before departure. Fontenay’s official FAQ says Montbard TGV station is 6 km away and identifies taxi or bicycle options. TER or coach arrival alone does not solve either monument’s last mile.

Trade-off

Choose what the route leaves out.

Choosing one abbey protects its landscape walk and leaves room for a meal and the confirmed return. A two-site comparison trades away both slow visits and depends on a driver; there is no verified public-transport pairing in this plan.

Time envelope

Give either abbey most of a day once the rail or coach trunk, reserved last mile, visit and return are counted. Fontenay can be a half-day on site, but Montbard’s transfer still sets the day. A two-site road plan needs a separate full-day check.

Combine only when it helps

Choose Vézelay or Fontenay for one rural day. Combine both only with a pre-arranged driver or guide who confirms each site’s access window and a return to the overnight base.

Pilgrimage hill and Cistercian valley

The two abbeys ask for different routes and different pace.

Vézelay’s basilica rises above a village on a steep hill. Fontenay sits in a quiet valley, where the monastic buildings, gardens and waterworks make the approach part of the visit.

Vézelay and the basilica

UNESCO describes the basilica, village and surrounding hill as one heritage landscape. Walk the rising village street at a pace that suits your mobility and leave time to descend before the booked return.

Fontenay’s Cistercian plan

The abbey’s cloister, church, chapter room, forge and valley setting reward a slow visit. The official FAQ says a half day can include a walk in the valley; the grounds are not a picnic site.

Two different gateways

Mobigo coach 119 is listed between Dijon and Avallon; Fontenay’s official visitor FAQ places Montbard TGV station 6 km from the abbey. Neither connection takes you directly to both sites.

  1. 01Arrive

    Choose the abbey and gateway

    For Vézelay, check Mobigo coach 119 to Avallon and book the separate transfer. For Fontenay, find a TER to Montbard and confirm the taxi or bicycle before buying the outward trip.

  2. 02Read the place

    Arrive at the actual entrance route

    At Vézelay, allow for the uphill village street and cobbles. At Fontenay, follow the booked transfer to the abbey entrance and use the valley approach to understand its seclusion.

  3. 03Commit

    Give one site a full reading

    At Vézelay, attend to the Romanesque portal and basilica as an active sacred place. At Fontenay, follow the cloister, church, chapter room and forge; add the valley walk only if current conditions and mobility allow.

  4. 04Return

    Return to the named pickup

    Confirm the driver, stop and exact return before entering. Reach the meeting point early enough for the TER or coach you selected; if no reliable return is confirmed, do not leave the base.

Keep the day useful when the headline plan disappears.

Three failure points

Change the plan before the problem compounds.

01

Avallon is not the Vézelay hill

Coach 119 reaches the Avallon corridor, not the basilica door. Confirm the onward transfer and same-day return before treating this as car-free.

02

Fontenay needs a separate last mile

The abbey’s current FAQ places Montbard TGV 6 km away. Book the taxi or check the visitor-office bicycle arrangement; do not rely on an unplanned walk.

03

Season and group rules matter

Fontenay’s current FAQ says individual advance booking is not required, while group visits must be booked; summer attendance is higher. Recheck before travel, and respect worship, conservation and any temporary closure.

Planning answers

Vézelay, Fontenay & Abbey Country FAQ

Can I visit Vézelay without a car?

Mobigo lists coach 119 between Dijon and Avallon, but Avallon is not the basilica. Confirm the date-specific onward transfer to the hill and its return before leaving; otherwise use a booked car or guided trip.

How do I reach Fontenay Abbey by train?

Use TER to Montbard TGV station, then pre-book a taxi or arrange the visitor-office bicycle option. Fontenay’s official FAQ places the station 6 km from the abbey, so the final leg must be part of the plan.

Can I combine both abbeys or book Fontenay as a group?

Only with a confirmed private vehicle or guided route. Their transfers start from different gateways, and the comparison day sacrifices time at each site. Fontenay’s current FAQ says individual visitors do not need advance booking while groups do; recheck the live visit notice and picnic rules before travel.
