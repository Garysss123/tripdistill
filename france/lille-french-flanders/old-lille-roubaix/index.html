Old Lille, La Piscine & Roubaix

Walk Lille’s civic and Flemish-influenced core as one compact loop, then use the Métro or tram for a deliberate Roubaix collection block only when its opening and return fit.

Access first

Know where the day actually begins.

For regional trains, Lille-Flandres is the central arrival; international and high-speed tickets may use Lille-Europe. They are distinct stations, so check the exact terminal before walking to your hotel or onward platform. Ilévia publishes a Lille–Roubaix route; plan the current Métro line 2 journey to Roubaix–Grand-Place, then follow La Piscine’s official approach on foot. Recheck disruptions and the museum calendar for your date rather than assuming a particular departure or fare.

Trade-off

Choose what the route leaves out.

A full Old Lille loop plus La Piscine is a deliberate split: allow the museum its own substantial block and give up a second large collection, the Citadel circuit or a long market meal. If the museum’s opening day or current line routing does not fit, make Roubaix its own half-day or stay in Lille; do not compress the galleries to keep an arbitrary stop count.

Time envelope

Plan four to six hours for Lille’s centre alone and six to eight hours for Lille plus La Piscine, including the Métro journey, meal and return to the correct rail terminal. A dedicated Roubaix museum visit works as a separate half-day. Add two nights in Lille for a city day and one museum/excursion day; a third night removes pressure from a late return.

Combine only when it helps

Combine Lille with Roubaix because the urban network supports it. Keep Arras memorial ground and the Opal Coast as separate days with their own last-mile plans.

Regional operating model

Fit this route into Lille, Arras & the Opal Coast, not into an isolated checklist.

Lille is a rail gateway, not a single-base answer to every northern day. Keep Lille’s Flemish-influenced streets and Roubaix collections in the urban system; treat Arras and Vimy as a remembrance day with a separately arranged rural last mile; and give the exposed Deux-Caps coast its own base, wind check and return plan. These are three distinct route contracts, so choose one excursion for a short stay or add nights before linking them.

Base strategy

For Lille and Roubaix only, book two nights near Lille-Flandres, Lille-Europe or a useful Métro interchange; three nights allow a full city day plus one excursion. To include both Vimy and the headlands, plan at least five nights and split them deliberately—Lille 2–3, Arras 1–2, then Boulogne-sur-Mer or Calais 2—only after checking dated rail and local connections. Do not count arrival and transfer days as full sightseeing days.

Transfer system

Lille-Flandres and Lille-Europe are separate stations: read the terminal on each ticket and allow a ground transfer between them. For Roubaix, check Ilévia’s current Métro line 2 route to Roubaix–Grand-Place and the walk to La Piscine. For Arras, search TER Hauts-de-France from Lille-Flandres to Arras, then arrange Vimy transport independently; no ordinary urban transit to the memorial is assumed. For the coast, choose Boulogne-Ville or Calais-Ville first, then confirm the dated local bus, guided transfer, bicycle or car leg and the return before leaving the station.

Season gate

Wind and rain can make the Deux-Caps paths unpleasant even when Lille is calm; check the coastal forecast and signed closures on the day. Summer brings longer light but does not guarantee a frequent coastal bus, and shoulder-season services can thin further. In spring or autumn, keep a lower-exposure museum or town day available. Vimy visitor-centre hours, ceremonies and access to preserved ground are date-specific; check the official notice before setting an early departure.

Regional fallback

If wind, visibility or a path closure removes the headland, stay in Boulogne for the old town and Nausicaá only if admission is available, or return to a verified Calais town plan. If the Vimy driver or tour cancels, do not improvise a rural walk: keep Arras’s squares and the Wellington Quarry visitor route where open. If a city museum is closed, remain in Lille’s walkable centre and switch to another confirmed interior.

Four-stage operating line

Arrive, read the place, commit, then protect the return.

On the actual travel date, check Ilévia’s Lille–Roubaix route and service notices, La Piscine’s opening and ticket information, the other museum’s calendar, the Lille-Flandres/Lille-Europe terminal printed on each rail ticket, and TER engineering notices. No fare or departure time is assumed here.

  1. 01Arrive

    Orient between the two Lille stations

    Confirm which terminal you arrived at, then move to Grand Place and mark the return platform before entering the old-town lanes.

  2. 02Read the place

    Walk the civic core

    Connect Grand Place, Vieille Bourse, the cathedral area and Vieux-Lille in one loop. Choose a market or museum only if it supports the day’s theme.

  3. 03Commit

    Commit to Lille or Roubaix

    Stay for a Lille collection and Citadel edge, or take the verified transit to La Piscine with enough time for its building and galleries.

  4. 04Return

    Return without a terminal mistake

    Come back to the correct Lille station or continue from Roubaix only on a checked service. Leave a food stop before the last comfortable train.

Practical route plan

Use the named entrance, then protect the way back.

Entry and return

Start from the terminal printed on the ticket. For Roubaix, use Ilévia’s live Lille–Roubaix route information to confirm Métro line 2 to Roubaix–Grand-Place, then walk to La Piscine by its official approach. Save the same-day route back to the correct Lille station before beginning the museum visit.

Booking and access limits

Base and nights: sleep in central Lille for two nights if you want one full city day and one Roubaix day; three nights add a lower-pressure arrival or alternate museum day. Do not move hotels to Roubaix for a single museum unless another commitment makes the suburb useful. Check La Piscine and Lille museum opening days before choosing the dates.

Weather plan

Rain is a reason to extend the museum block, not to dash between collections. In hot weather, shorten the exposed Citadel walk and keep water and shade breaks. If the museum is closed or line service changes, use a verified Lille interior and keep the city loop compact.

Lower-risk fallback

Keep the day useful when the headline plan disappears.

If line 2 is disrupted or La Piscine is closed on the chosen date, stay in Lille and use the Palais des Beaux-Arts or Hospice Comtesse only after checking its own hours and admission. In steady rain, shorten the outdoor old-town loop and move the confirmed interior earlier; in summer heat, use shaded streets and a longer museum block.

Three failure points

Change the plan before the problem compounds.

01

Two main stations cause missed trains

Lille Flandres and Lille Europe are walkably close but not the same building. Read the ticket before the final meal.

02

Museum days differ

La Piscine and Lille museums may close on different weekdays or change galleries. Check the exact date.

03

Private courtyards remain private

Old Lille rewards looking upward, but residential doors and courtyards are not invitations. Use signed public passages.
