Access first

Know where the day actually begins.

Metro A and C serve the civic center and Croix-Rousse, while many slopes are steep. Decide whether to ride uphill and walk down, and verify any guided traboule or workshop meeting point before arrival.

Trade-off

Choose what the route leaves out.

A full art museum, long food hall visit and deep Croix-Rousse walk exceed one day. The route keeps one central collection or meal and one hill narrative.

Time envelope

Allow six to eight hours for Presqu’île plus Croix-Rousse and one interior. Either district alone supports four to five hours.

Combine only when it helps

Combine the central spine with one Croix-Rousse route. Keep Fourvière, Confluence and Beaujolais for their own days.

Regional operating model

Fit this route into Lyon & the Rhône Gateway, not into an isolated checklist.

Lyon’s planning logic comes from the Saône, Rhône and two hills. Vieux Lyon and Fourvière form a vertical historic route; Presqu’île and Croix-Rousse connect commerce to silk-worker history; Beaujolais and Pérouges require separate regional last miles. Choose the hill before the meal reservation so the final descent, not fatigue, decides where the evening ends.

Base strategy

Three nights allow two city days and one excursion. Base near a useful Métro or tram connection rather than assuming the old town is easiest for every station, hill and dinner.

Transfer system

Part-Dieu and Perrache are distinct main stations. Metro, tram and funicular lines solve different axes; regional excursions require an exact station, bus or tour rather than a generic Lyon departure.

Season gate

Summer heat makes hills and exposed squares harder at midday; winter and rain favor traboules only where publicly signed and museums where open. Festival and major-event periods alter crowds and transit.

Regional fallback

Use the city’s museums, covered food hall or flatter Presqu’île route when heat, funicular disruption or rural weather changes the plan. Keep the substitute on the same transport axis.

Four-stage operating line

Arrive, read the place, commit, then protect the return.

Check TCL service, museum and market hours, workshop reservation and public-passage guidance before choosing the slope.

  1. 01Arrive

    Start between the rivers

    Use Bellecour or Cordeliers to orient Presqu’île, then move north through civic squares without zig-zagging to both riverbanks.

  2. 02Read the place

    Choose the central anchor

    Visit the Musée des Beaux-Arts, market or another selected site, keeping the Croix-Rousse reservation time protected.

  3. 03Commit

    Ride or climb to silk history

    Use Métro C or a deliberate slope, then join the official workshop, Maison des Canuts or public passage route.

  4. 04Return

    Descend toward useful transit

    Walk one signed slope or use the metro, finishing near Hôtel de Ville or a river crossing rather than returning to the plateau.

Lower-risk fallback

Keep the day useful when the headline plan disappears.

In heat or rain, stay on Presqu’île with museums and covered food options, or ride directly to a booked silk interior. If a guided passage cancels, use the public streets and Maison des Canuts where open.

Three failure points

Change the plan before the problem compounds.

01

Slopes are the route, not decoration

Cobble, stairs and gradients affect footwear and mobility. Ride uphill when needed.

02

Workshops need reservations

Living silk businesses are not open-display attractions. Confirm language, time and purchase expectations.

03

Food halls have trading rhythms

Check current opening and vendor hours; a building may be open while the intended stalls are not.
