Access first

Know where the day actually begins.

Start on Métro A at Bellecour or Cordeliers, change at Hôtel de Ville for Métro C to Croix-Rousse, then return by the same interchange. The steep slopes are optional; confirm any guided visit meeting point and language before arrival.

Trade-off

Choose what the route leaves out.

A full art museum, long food hall visit and deep Croix-Rousse walk exceed one day. The route keeps one central collection or meal and one hill narrative.

Time envelope

Allow six to eight hours for Presqu’île plus Croix-Rousse and one interior. Either district alone supports four to five hours.

Combine only when it helps

Combine the central spine with one Croix-Rousse route. Keep Fourvière, Confluence and Beaujolais for their own days.

Regional operating model

Fit this route into Lyon & the Rhône Gateway, not into an isolated checklist.

Lyon’s planning logic comes from the Saône, Rhône and two hills. Vieux Lyon and Fourvière form a vertical historic route; Presqu’île and Croix-Rousse connect commerce to silk-worker history; Beaujolais and Pérouges require separate regional last miles. Choose the hill before the meal reservation so the final descent, not fatigue, decides where the evening ends.

Base strategy

Use four nights for two full Lyon days and one full excursion, sleeping near Bellecour or Cordeliers in Presqu’île for Métro A/D and walkable river routes. Part-Dieu suits an early TER start, but still needs a city-transit ride for the hills. Three nights works only when arrival or departure supplies one usable day segment; otherwise drop the excursion rather than race.

Transfer system

Part-Dieu and Perrache are different stations. For Pérouges, take TER from Lyon Part-Dieu to Meximieux-Pérouges, walk about 20 minutes uphill from the station to the medieval town, then reverse the same route. For Beaujolais, use TER to Villefranche-sur-Saône and continue only with a booked local pickup; TCL 217 serves named villages and stops, but its school-term, holiday, Saturday and Sunday patterns differ.

Season gate

Summer heat makes Fourvière stairs and exposed squares harder at midday; winter rain makes Pérouges cobbles slick and can complicate the rural last mile. Traboules are private residential passages except those currently signed open. Festival dates and major events can alter access and transit.

Regional fallback

If hill weather or the F2 funicular fails, keep the day on flat Presqu’île with a booked museum. If the rural return or Beaujolais pickup cannot be confirmed, replace the excursion with Villefranche’s Paul-Dini museum or another Lyon indoor visit; do not improvise a vineyard taxi after arrival.

Four-stage operating line

Arrive, read the place, commit, then protect the return.

    Start between the rivers

    Use Bellecour or Cordeliers on Métro A, then follow the Presqu’île spine north toward Hôtel de Ville without zig-zagging back across the Rhône.

  2. 02Read the place

    Choose the central anchor

    Visit the Musée des Beaux-Arts, market or another selected site, keeping the Croix-Rousse reservation time protected.

  3. 03Commit

    Ride to silk history

    At Hôtel de Ville, transfer from Métro A to Métro C for Croix-Rousse; join a booked Maison des Canuts or workshop visit.

  4. 04Return

    Return through Hôtel de Ville

    Take Métro C back to Hôtel de Ville and Métro A to Bellecour or Cordeliers. Walk down only if the steep streets suit your group.

Practical route plan

Use the named entrance, then protect the way back.

Entry and return

Start at Bellecour or Cordeliers. Ride Métro A north to Hôtel de Ville, transfer to Métro C for Croix-Rousse, and walk between Maison des Canuts and the plateau sights. Return on Métro C to Hôtel de Ville and Métro A to the central base. A downhill walk to Terreaux is an option only for people comfortable with steep, stepped streets.

Booking and access limits

Maison des Canuts lists Métro C, Croix-Rousse, as its stop and accepts online reservations for its program. Its current practical page lists museum opening Tuesday–Saturday; check the selected visit and language before buying, especially for a guided traboule walk. The plateau is a working residential district, not an open-door attraction.

Weather plan

For heavy rain or heat, keep the route on Presqu’île: choose the Musée des Beaux-Arts or another confirmed indoor stop and skip the slopes. If the museum or silk visit is closed, do not replace it with an unbooked private passage.

Three failure points

Change the plan before the problem compounds.

01

Slopes are the route, not decoration

Cobble, stairs and gradients affect footwear and mobility. Ride uphill when needed.

02

Workshops need reservations

Living silk businesses are not open-display attractions. Confirm language, time and purchase expectations.

03

Food halls have trading rhythms

Check current opening and vendor hours; a building may be open while the intended stalls are not.
