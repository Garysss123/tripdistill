Access first

Know where the day actually begins.

Enter from Métro D Vieux Lyon–Cathédrale Saint-Jean. TCL funicular F2 runs between Vieux Lyon and Fourvière; check its live status before using it as the hill connection. Only passages currently signed open to visitors are public; private residential courtyards remain off-limits.

Trade-off

Choose what the route leaves out.

Adding Croix-Rousse creates a second hill and weakens both. The route gives up Presqu’île shopping and distant food stops so Vieux Lyon and Fourvière form one legible vertical story.

Time envelope

Allow five to seven hours for both levels with one museum. A compact old-town and viewpoint route needs three to four hours.

Combine only when it helps

Combine Vieux Lyon with Fourvière because they share one vertical axis. Keep Croix-Rousse, Parc de la Tête d’Or and Beaujolais for separate routes.

Regional operating model

Fit this route into Lyon & the Rhône Gateway, not into an isolated checklist.

Lyon’s planning logic comes from the Saône, Rhône and two hills. Vieux Lyon and Fourvière form a vertical historic route; Presqu’île and Croix-Rousse connect commerce to silk-worker history; Beaujolais and Pérouges require separate regional last miles. Choose the hill before the meal reservation so the final descent, not fatigue, decides where the evening ends.

Base strategy

Use four nights for two full Lyon days and one full excursion, sleeping near Bellecour or Cordeliers in Presqu’île for Métro A/D and walkable river routes. Part-Dieu suits an early TER start, but still needs a city-transit ride for the hills. Three nights works only when arrival or departure supplies one usable day segment; otherwise drop the excursion rather than race.

Transfer system

Part-Dieu and Perrache are different stations. For Pérouges, take TER from Lyon Part-Dieu to Meximieux-Pérouges, walk about 20 minutes uphill from the station to the medieval town, then reverse the same route. For Beaujolais, use TER to Villefranche-sur-Saône and continue only with a booked local pickup; TCL 217 serves named villages and stops, but its school-term, holiday, Saturday and Sunday patterns differ.

Season gate

Summer heat makes Fourvière stairs and exposed squares harder at midday; winter rain makes Pérouges cobbles slick and can complicate the rural last mile. Traboules are private residential passages except those currently signed open. Festival dates and major events can alter access and transit.

Regional fallback

If hill weather or the F2 funicular fails, keep the day on flat Presqu’île with a booked museum. If the rural return or Beaujolais pickup cannot be confirmed, replace the excursion with Villefranche’s Paul-Dini museum or another Lyon indoor visit; do not improvise a vineyard taxi after arrival.

Four-stage operating line

Arrive, read the place, commit, then protect the return.

  2. 02Read the place

    Read the Renaissance ground

    Connect cathedral, courtyards and one museum or interpretation site, keeping resident access clear and watching the hill above for weather.

  3. 03Commit

    Ascend once

    Take TCL F2 from Vieux Lyon to Fourvière when it is operating, then choose the basilica and viewpoint or Lugdunum and the Roman theatres as the main upper layer.

  4. 04Return

    Protect the return

    Return on F2 to Vieux Lyon and Métro D. Use a signed walking descent only when the official transit status, weather, mobility and daylight support it.

Practical route plan

Use the named entrance, then protect the way back.

Entry and return

Start at Métro D Vieux Lyon–Cathédrale Saint-Jean. Walk the Saône-side streets and only the traboules currently signed open to visitors. Take TCL F2 from Vieux Lyon to Fourvière; return on F2 to the same station, then Métro D toward the central base. If F2 is disrupted, use a signed public street route only when weather, mobility and daylight make the climb reasonable.

Booking and access limits

The basilica is an active place of worship: check its current access and service notices, and confirm Lugdunum or museum opening before choosing the upper-hill anchor. Do not enter private courtyards or assume every traboule is open.

Weather plan

In heavy rain or heat, skip the exposed ascent and keep Vieux Lyon at street level with a booked indoor museum, or move to the flatter Presqu’île. Wet stone steps are a reason to cancel the hill, not to hurry down them.

Three failure points

Change the plan before the problem compounds.

01

Traboules are not all public

Enter only signed passages during allowed hours and keep noise low in residential courtyards.

02

One ascent is enough

Fourvière stairs and slopes add quickly. Use transit for one direction when mobility or heat is a concern.

03

Religious and event access changes

Basilica services and Roman-site events can redirect circulation. Check current notices.
