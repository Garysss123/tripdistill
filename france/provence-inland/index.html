Avignon, Aix & the Luberon

Give Avignon and Aix their own city days; add the Luberon only after solving its last mile.

Avignon is a walled papal city and rail base; Aix is a separate fountain-and-market city with its own distant TGV transfer. Choose which urban core deserves your full day before adding a Luberon village branch that the regional bus network does not turn into a simple loop.

01 · Base

Sleep where the useful departures are.

Two nights buy one full day after arrival and departure: choose Avignon for the papal quarter and the western rail corridor, or Aix for its market streets, museums and Cézanne sites. Three nights let one city breathe for two full days. Four nights can split two and two, but the station and hotel move is a transfer day. With five nights, add a Luberon excursion only if a named operator, driver or car is already arranged; six nights allow a slower rural base, but only when the luggage and return plan work. In Avignon, sleep inside or just outside the walls near Avignon Centre if you want to walk home; in Aix, the Rotonde/Cours Mirabeau or bus-station edge gives a more useful city-to-transit balance. Rural lodging trades rail convenience for a last-mile contract.

02 · Transfer

Solve the complete last mile.

Avignon Centre is beside the historic core; Avignon TGV is 6 km away. Avignon Tourisme lists TER or Orizo buses 10 and 20 between the TGV station and centre. Aix Centre and Aix-en-Provence TGV are also separate: the TGV station is about 15 km from the city, and La Métropole Mobilité calls the connecting Aix Centre–TGV–airport shuttle A2 (renumbered from line 40 in 2025). Use the actual station names in TER/SNCF and A2 searches. The updated Luberon guide explains why the ZOU! Apt–Avignon corridor is a gateway, not village or abbey access.

03 · Season

Let current conditions edit the map.

Markets are a morning plan, not an all-day guarantee: Avignon Tourisme lists Les Halles Tuesday–Sunday; Aix’s tourist office lists Place Richelme produce stalls daily 08:00–13:00 except 1 January, and Place Comtales on Tuesday, Thursday and Saturday. Check the exact calendar before lodging near a market square. Avignon Festival dates bring early hotel demand and venue/security routing. The Palace’s 2026–27 visitor-route transition and the Lauves studio’s 2026 seasonal opening are dated examples, not future-year promises. Put exposed walks early in hot or mistral weather, check the live fire-access map for a rural trail, and treat lavender timing as variable agriculture.

04 · Fallback

Keep a real lower-risk day.

If heat, wind, fire controls or a missed rural connection removes the countryside day, stay in one city: use a currently open museum, a covered market or a shorter wall-and-square walk. If a ticketed room, studio or festival venue is unavailable, do not replace it with a cross-region taxi chain; select a nearby city alternative and protect the booked return.

Four nights for two city cores; keep the fifth flexible

One station move separates the walled city from the market city.

This selective outline uses two nights in Avignon and two in Aix. Arrival and departure are partial days; the city-to-city transfer is not another sightseeing day. On a shorter stay, pick one base. Use the fifth day only for a reserved excursion or an extra day in the city you prefer.

Day 1 · Avignon arrival

Sleep inside or just outside the walls near Avignon Centre. If your train reaches Avignon TGV, use the checked TER connection or Orizo 10/20 before planning a walk; the station is 6 km from the centre. Keep the first loop to the gate, Les Halles or Place de l’Horloge.

Day 2 · Papal quarter and Rhône

Book the Palace first. Its 2026 visitor pages describe a route transition and changing rooms; reserve time for the current circuit, then add the gardens, Petit Palais, Rocher des Doms or Pont Saint-Bénézet as energy and separate access allow.

Day 3 · Transfer to Aix

Use the live TER itinerary between Avignon Centre and Aix-en-Provence, and leave time to check out and move luggage. If a high-speed ticket ends at Aix-en-Provence TGV, the A2 shuttle reaches the Aix bus station; do not confuse the TGV stop with the city station.

Day 4 · Aix old town

Put the official market that matches your date first, then walk one line between Place Richelme, the old-city squares and Cours Mirabeau. Add the Atelier des Lauves only with its dated booking and current bus 5/12 access; otherwise choose one open city collection.

Optional Day 5 · Luberon or a slower city

Stay a fifth night for a booked rural excursion with a confirmed pickup and return, or spend another full day in Avignon or Aix. A city-to-Apt bus is not the last mile to Gordes, Roussillon or Sénanque; use the existing Luberon guide for its separate branch and access decision.

Before you choose

Three routes, three different sacrifices.

Wall-gate papal itinerary

Avignon Papal City & Rhône Edge

Access: Avignon Centre is the walkable station; Avignon TGV is 6 km away, linked by TER or Orizo 10/20. Keep the walled city on foot and check event-day gate access.

Trade-off: The Palace and garden already take a substantial block; adding the separately visited bridge means giving up another museum or the rural excursion.

Time: One full city day after an arrival night; allow about two hours for Palace plus gardens, then add walking, meal and any separate site only if the ticket and pace fit.

Fountain-market studio walk

Aix Markets & Cézanne Ground

Access: Aix Centre and the bus station serve the city; Aix TGV is 15 km out and connects by A2. Atelier des Lauves lists buses 5/12 to Atelier Cézanne.

Trade-off: A market morning pairs with either the booked studio or a city museum. Adding the mountain or multiple Cézanne sites needs another day and its own transit plan.

Time: One full day for the old centre, market and one art anchor; count the TGV shuttle and a dated studio reservation separately.

Village-cluster heat map

Luberon Villages & Sénanque

Access: Regional buses serve some villages on limited schedules, but a tour, bicycle or car is often required. Choose the cluster from the live timetable, road and parking rules; never assume taxis can bridge missed rural buses.

Trade-off: The route gives up “all the Luberon.” Two villages or one village plus the abbey already consume a full day after parking, heat and meals are counted.

Time: Allow a full day for one two-stop cluster. One village alone merits three to five hours with a market or meal; adding a third usually creates road tourism.

Regional answers

Avignon, Aix & the Luberon FAQ

How many nights should I book in inland Provence?

Two or three nights work best in one city. Four nights can support two nights in Avignon and two in Aix, with the move counted as travel time; this is two city visits, not a Luberon circuit. Add a countryside day only when its named transport and return are already arranged.

Which city is the better base?

Choose Avignon for the walled papal core and its central rail position; choose Aix for the old-city market, museum and Cézanne focus. In each city, stay near the centre and the actual city station or bus station. Do not book an Avignon TGV or Aix TGV hotel as if it were inside either historic core.

Can I reach the Luberon without a car?

Some ZOU! services reach larger gateway towns, but the scheduled corridor is not a village-to-abbey loop. Use the Luberon guide to match its current stop list to a date and prearrange a tour, driver or other last mile before committing.

What should I reserve first?

Reserve the Palace or the Atelier des Lauves if either is the reason for the day, then fit markets and secondary museums around its confirmed entry. For a festival performance or rural vehicle, confirm the exact date, venue/pickup and return before choosing the hotel.

What changes in summer?

Festival streets and hotel demand, heat, mistral and the daily Vaucluse fire-access level can change a route. Move walking to a cooler block, use a city interior as the backup, and do not treat lavender bloom as a date guarantee.
