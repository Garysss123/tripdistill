Read the place

See the tower groups as one sacred valley.

From the 4th to 13th centuries, My Son served as a religious and political center of Champa. Its tower-temples use fired brick, stone pillars and sandstone reliefs; their forms record Cham religious and political life, including a strong Hindu tradition. Conflict damaged many structures, and conservation continues in a humid valley ringed by hills at the source of the Thu Bon River.

Orientation note

This is an archaeological landscape, not a single temple. Use current visitor transport if operating, then follow the marked route between standing groups and interpreted ruins. Compare brick joints and sandstone reliefs; damaged areas and barriers are part of its history and conservation.

Four-stage route

Arrive early; walk the signed sequence.

Leave early enough to reach the exposed valley before peak heat. Read the entrance interpretation, use any operating visitor shuttle, then walk the official sequence steadily. Do not cross barriers or leave signed paths: UNESCO notes unresolved UXO risk in parts of the buffer, as well as humidity and flood exposure.

01Arrive

Confirm the return

Arrange a car or regulated excursion with clear pickup and return. Check the management notice, weather and current visitor transport before departure.

02Read

Place the valley in context

Read official interpretation before entering the tower groups. The hills and Thu Bon headwaters explain why this was a sacred and strategic landscape.

03Deepen

Compare brick and relief

Follow the marked circuit and look at fired-brick construction, stone supports and sandstone figures. Damaged towers and conservation boundaries show what survives after conflict and restoration.

04Exit

Keep the road leg open

Use the confirmed pickup and allow time for heat, rain or a slower walk. If the site closes or severe weather arrives, shift to indoor Cham collections in Da Nang or heritage interiors in Hoi An.

Before the inland drive

Heat, transport and marked ground.

The coast-to-valley road and open-air circuit need a return buffer, especially in wet weather.

01

Weather in the valley

My Son is humid and exposed; UNESCO notes flooding and climatic stress. Start in the morning. For heavy rain or unsafe paths, use a museum or old-town interior instead.

02

Visitor transport

The management site lists electric-car and audio-guide services, but a listing does not confirm same-day operation. Ask directly and plan to walk if transport is unavailable.

03

No path shortcuts

UXO risk remains unresolved in parts of the buffer. Stay on designated routes, respect barriers and never enter forest or closed archaeological ground.

Visiting the sanctuary

Cham history in an upland setting

How much time should My Son Sanctuary & Cham Heritage receive?

From Hoi An, plan a morning half-day with several hours among the monument groups and a return buffer. From Da Nang, reserve most of the day for road time and the exposed walk. Mobility, heat, rain or a slower interpretation visit can extend the schedule.

Can I combine My Son Sanctuary & Cham Heritage with another major chapter?

Do not attach another long drive to the return. If heavy rain or heat makes the valley a poor fit, visit Da Nang’s Cham Museum of Sculpture or return to Hoi An for ticketed timber houses and assembly halls; each gives related history in a different setting.

What should I verify before leaving?

Check My Son’s visitor-notice page for entry, road and transport arrangements; ask whether listed electric cars or audio guides are operating. Review weather and closure notices, and keep the visit on designated paths.
