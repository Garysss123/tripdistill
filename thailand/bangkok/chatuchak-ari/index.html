Chatuchak& Ari

Chatuchak is a covered maze of numbered sections and narrow sois: choose a category from the market map, browse one part of the full market, then leave by rail. Ari is a smaller neighborhood chapter for a meal or short lane walk.

Choose by market day

The full browse and the weekday sessions are different visits

The market operator lists plants on Wednesday and Thursday, wholesale on Friday, and the full market on Saturday and Sunday. Use its current schedule and map together: an open market day does not mean every section is trading.

BEFORE YOU GO

Check the session

Use the live schedule to decide whether you want plants, wholesale buying or the broad weekend browse. Check for current notices before crossing Bangkok.

AT THE MAP

Choose one section

The numbered sections and sois make this a network of covered lanes, not one shopping hall. Pick one category and mark the lane where you will enter.

WHEN YOU NEED A BREAK

Step into Chatuchak Park

Leave the stalls for shaded paths, a literary garden, herb gardens and the fish-pond bridge. Use the park as a distinct rest stop, not another errand.

AFTER THE MARKET

Make Ari optional

If you still want a neighborhood meal, take the BTS to Ari and choose one compact cluster around Phahonyothin Soi 7.

Chatuchak: follow the section map

Clothing, homewares, crafts, art, food, plants and second-hand finds sit across numbered sections. Choose the kind of stall you came for, then use the map's section and soi labels to reach it. The narrow covered aisles are easier to navigate when you browse one area at a time.

Ari: a change of scale

Ari is a separate BTS stop with small restaurants, cafes and neighborhood lanes around Phahonyothin Soi 7. It works as a slower meal or short walk after the market; there is no need to chase venues across multiple sois.

Match rail to the next stop

Three stations serve three different edges

Chatuchak sits between two MRT approaches and the BTS. Choose your first entrance and return point from the map before entering the covered lanes.

MRT KAMPHAENG PHET

Market-lane approach

Use this station when your first destination is a market section. Confirm the signed exit that brings you closest to that part of the map.

BTS MO CHIT / MRT CHATUCHAK PARK

Park-side approach

These stops serve Chatuchak Park and other market edges. They also make a practical exit when you want to leave the stalls for green space.

BTS ARI

Neighborhood meal

Take the BTS one stop east from Mo Chit to Ari, then keep your walk around one chosen lane cluster.

Choose and check

  • Pick the category and section before you arrive.
  • Compare materials, condition and dimensions before paying.
  • Ask for the final price and confirm what is included.
  • Check current customs and airline rules for plants, antiques or other restricted goods.

Keep the route easy

  • Save a section, soi and station exit on the operator map.
  • Put purchases away before moving into crowded lanes.
  • Use the park's paths and shaded gardens for a sit-down break.
  • Leave the market when your bags make it harder to move comfortably.

Quick answers

Chatuchak and Ari FAQ

Which day should I choose for Chatuchak Market?

The operator lists Wednesday and Thursday for plants, Friday for wholesale, and Saturday and Sunday for the full market. Check its current schedule and map before travelling because the open sections differ.

Which station is closest to the market?

MRT Kamphaeng Phet is a useful approach for the market lanes. BTS Mo Chit and MRT Chatuchak Park serve other park-side edges; use the exit nearest your first section.

How do I keep the market manageable?

Choose a category and a numbered section from the operator map, save the nearest exit, then stop at Chatuchak Park when you are ready for a quieter break.

What does Ari add to the day?

Ari is a separate neighborhood stop for a meal or a short walk around Phahonyothin Soi 7 and nearby lanes. Take the BTS from Mo Chit to Ari instead of treating it as another market section.
