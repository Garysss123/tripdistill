Andaman chapter 05 · island spine

Ko Lanta cross once, then choose

Most stays use Ko Lanta Yai: cross the mainland car ferry to Ko Lanta Noi, continue over Siri Lanta Bridge, then choose a base along the west coast. Lanta Old Town and Thung Yee Peng sit on the east side; the national-park lighthouse is at the far south. Group those places into separate road days.

Read the island north to south

A single road links five different stay decisions

TAT describes Ko Lanta Yai as a long island with a paved north–south road. Distances look modest on a map; each cross-island turn or southbound excursion still uses the same spine.

NORTH / ARRIVAL

Saladan & Klong Dao

Saladan is the service and arrival town; nearby Klong Dao makes the first night easier after the mainland crossing. Choose this end for provisions and onward transport.

MID-NORTH WEST

Long Beach / Phra Ae

A practical base when you want a west-coast beach and food nearby without starting at the island's far end. Confirm your exact lane and evening walk to dinner.

MIDDLE WEST

Klong Khong & Klong Nin

These are separate beach communities farther south; Klong Khong has a rocky foreshore at low tide, while Klong Nin sits farther down the road. Pick by the nightly radius, not a shared “mid-island” label.

SOUTH / WEST

Kantiang & Tanod Cape

Kantiang is a longer southbound base; Tanod Cape and the park lighthouse sit farther south still. Keep the park visit as its own road chapter.

EAST / HISTORY

Lanta Old Town

Baan Sriraya's old wooden shop-houses face the east-coast pier. Come for the settlement and harbor history, then cross back to the west coast for a sunset beach.

The right beach is a daily radius

Choose the place you will not need to leave at dinner

A walkable cluster of food, shade and the right beach can be more valuable than a remote room that requires two road trips every day.

Inspect the exact property

  • Safe walking route after dark and distance to food.
  • Direct, public or hotel-controlled beach access.
  • Current swimming flags, rocks and tide exposure.
  • Transfer time from the arrival pier and to the departure operator.
Work with the island spine

Group the long turns into separate days

Choose one west-coast base for beach evenings, then give the east settlements and the southern headland their own road outings.

NORTH

Saladan to Klong Dao

Keep the arrival, ferry-side errands and first beach close together after the car-ferry crossing.

EAST

Thung Yee Peng to Baan Sriraya

Cross to the mangrove village and continue south to Lanta Old Town’s wooden harbor-front houses.

SOUTH

Kantiang to Tanod Cape

Continue to the park gate and lighthouse as one out-and-back road day; avoid adding the east-coast return at lunch.

LOCAL MOVES

Choose a driver or ride

A local driver works well for a long multi-stop day. Use a scooter only if the licence, insurance and road experience fit the actual route.

SOUTHERN ROAD DAY

Make Tanod Cape the farthest stop

The national-park headland and lighthouse mark Ko Lanta Yai's south tip. Go on a day when the full return to your west-coast base fits daylight.

THE LAST KILOMETRES

Turn south from your base

Pass the beach settlements toward Kantiang, then continue to the park gate and Tanod Cape rather than doubling back for an east-coast lunch.

AT THE CAPE

Read the headland

The lighthouse overlooks rocky shore and sea rather than a developed beach strip. Check the park's open facilities and walking access before setting out.

RETURN

Keep the sunset flexible

Stop for food on the way north only if the road and weather leave a comfortable ride back to your chosen lodging.

WEST COAST

Use a local beach day

Long Beach, Klong Khong and Klong Nin are road-linked stays, not one continuous swimming condition. Check the beach at the tide you plan to use.

EAST COAST

Pair mangrove and harbor

Thung Yee Peng's community mangroves and Lanta Old Town are a distinct east-side outing; go by a local driver or an experienced road plan instead of a boat-hopping promise.

OFFSHORE

Check park season first

TAT notes that Ko Rok, Ko Haa and nearby park islands are commonly closed from May to October. Verify current DNP notices and the actual operator departure before reserving a sea day.

DEPARTURE

Return to the mainland

The car ferry and bridge still leave a road journey after the island. Stay near your next transport hub before a separate flight.

Four days, four different bearings

Move down the spine once and give the east side its own day

DAY 01 · NORTH

Cross to Saladan

Use the mainland car ferry to Ko Lanta Noi, cross Siri Lanta Bridge to Ko Lanta Yai and sleep near Saladan or Klong Dao after arrival.

DAY 02 · WEST

Settle into the beach base

Choose Long Beach for a central west-coast day, or continue to Klong Khong or Klong Nin if that is where you will spend the evening. Avoid returning north just for dinner.

DAY 03 · EAST

Read the harbor and mangroves

Cross to Thung Yee Peng for a community-led mangrove walk or boat, then continue south to Baan Sriraya in Lanta Old Town and its wooden harbor-front houses.

DAY 04 · SOUTH

Finish at Tanod Cape

Take the west-coast road through Kantiang to the national-park gate and lighthouse. Return north before dark; leave a weather-flexible day instead if an offshore park is open.

Quick answers

Ko Lanta planning FAQ

How do I reach Ko Lanta Yai from the mainland?

The route crosses by car ferry to Ko Lanta Noi and continues across Siri Lanta Bridge to Ko Lanta Yai. Check current ferry operation, road transfer and timing for your arrival date.

Where should I stay on the island?

Saladan and Klong Dao suit an easy arrival; Long Beach is a useful central west-coast base; Klong Khong, Klong Nin and Kantiang move progressively farther south. Lanta Old Town is an east-coast culture stay rather than a west-beach base.

Can I visit Old Town and the mangroves in one day?

Yes as an east-side road day: visit Thung Yee Peng's community mangrove area, then continue south to Baan Sriraya in Lanta Old Town. Use a local transfer and leave the far southern park road for another day.

Do Ko Rok and Ko Haa boats run year-round?

No fixed year-round service is a safe assumption. TAT notes common May–October closures for outlying park islands; check the current DNP notice and named operator before booking.
