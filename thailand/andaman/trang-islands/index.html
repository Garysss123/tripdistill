Trang Islands purpose before hopping

Ko Muk, Ko Kradan and Ko Libong appear close, but they answer different holidays and connect through different mainland piers and boats. Build one bright center; treat every extra island as a transfer to prove.

Three islands, three reasons

Pick the landscape before the boat

Trang’s islands are not one shared beach strip, and the nearest pier changes with your choice.

Ko Muk

Farang Beach and a lived-in village sit on the same island; the west-coast Morakot Cave enters through an approximately 80-metre dark water passage into an enclosed sandy chamber.

Ko Kradan

Choose a beach-and-reef stay or a long day from Pak Meng. The Hat Chao Mai National Park protection unit is on the island; accommodation commonly arranges the closer Kuan Tung Ku crossing.

Ko Libong

Trang’s largest island has fishing settlements, mangroves and extensive seagrass. The Leekpai Bridge dugong-viewing tower looks over habitat; wildlife sightings are never guaranteed.

Trang town & Kantang

Put a mainland day around the former governor’s house museum, the historic Kantang rail terminus and the first rubber tree TAT identifies on the road south.

PIER ISLAND MATCH

Use the right mainland landing

Trang Airport and station connect by road; the final pier is a separate decision.

KUAN TUNG KU

Ko Muk and Ko Kradan

For Ko Muk, arrange the crossing to your lodging or cave operator. For Ko Kradan, TAT says many stays arrange the boat from this nearer pier.

PAK MENG

Day-trip boats

TAT lists charter boats from Pak Meng to Ko Kradan at about an hour. Treat a day tour as a packaged excursion, not proof of a daily public ferry onward.

HAT YAO

Ko Libong

TAT lists a short local motorboat crossing to the island. Confirm the current departure window, landing and return with the pier or your host.

Ko Muk’s enclosed lagoon

Morakot Cave is a passage through the island

On Ko Muk’s west side, visitors float as a group through roughly 80 metres of darkness before the limestone opens into a high chamber and white-sand beach. It is a cave visit with a water approach, not a walk-in beach.

Check this specific excursion

  • Ask the local operator whether the cave and entry route are open on your date.
  • Confirm the tide, sea conditions, guide and flotation plan before boarding.
  • If darkness, enclosed space or swimming is uncomfortable, stay on Ko Muk’s beaches or choose the Trang mainland route instead.

TAT reported Hat Chao Mai’s annual Ko Muk and Ko Kradan closure as 1 June–30 September in its 21 February 2025 island guide; check the current DNP order each year.

Use the place for what it is

Village, reef and seagrass are different days

Choose one island’s character; an extra crossing only helps when it opens a genuinely different route.

KO MUK

Farang Beach to village

Give the cave and the island’s Muslim fishing community separate time. TAT describes residents working in fishing, rubber and tourism; make local arrangements rather than treating the village as scenery.

KO KRADAN

Beach and reef

The island’s clear coast and coral make a compact sea-and-shore stay. Confirm which beach or boat landing your accommodation uses and whether it is operating for the season.

KO LIBONG

Leekpai Bridge viewpoint

Use the tower at the bridge’s end or Khao Batu Putei to look for dugongs over seagrass. The reserve is a lived island with fishing villages, not a wildlife show.

MAINLAND

Trang town to Kantang

Make the former governor’s residence and the terminal railway station a separate cultural day, then use the correct southern pier for the island you selected.

DATED PARK NOTICE

Ko Muk and Ko Kradan

TAT’s 21 February 2025 route guide reports a 1 June–30 September Hat Chao Mai closure for both islands. Check DNP’s current notice for the travel year and exact area.

LIBONG ROUTE

Separate crossing

Ko Libong uses Hat Yao and its own local boat service. Do not assume its pier, schedule or access follows the Muk/Kradan park route.

MONSOON COAST

Ask about the boat

Seasonal seas can change departures and island accommodation. Confirm the named operator and the return leg directly before fixing a flight.

RETURN

Sleep on land

Use Trang or another road-linked base before an early TST flight or a long-distance train.

Choose one five-night route

Do not turn separate piers into a forced circuit

NIGHT 01 · TRANG

Arrive on land

Stay in town, then check the road transfer and exact boat pier for Ko Muk, Ko Kradan or Ko Libong.

NIGHTS 02–04 · MUK

Village and cave

Take the Kuan Tung Ku route to Ko Muk, walk Farang Beach and the village, then book Morakot Cave only when the park and operator confirm access.

ALTERNATE · KRADAN

Beach and reef

Choose Ko Kradan instead of Muk for a slower beach stay; ask the accommodation about its Kuan Tung Ku transfer or plan a Pak Meng charter day.

ALTERNATE · LIBONG

Community and seagrass

Use Hat Yao for Ko Libong, visit the Leekpai Bridge viewpoint and let the mainland return follow the crossing time your host confirms.

Quick answers

Trang islands FAQ

Which island should I choose?

Ko Muk for village life plus cave access, Ko Kradan for beach focus, or Ko Libong for community and seagrass landscape. Choose one primary purpose.

Can I visit all three?

Possibly when live services align, but every move costs a packing and boat day. One island plus a mainland buffer is often stronger.

Is Morakot Cave easy?

Not for everyone. Darkness, water, enclosed space, tide and group control matter. Use a competent operator and opt out without pressure.

How do I arrive?

Trang Airport or railway station connects by road to a specific mainland pier, then boat. Confirm each leg and avoid landing after the final safe service.
