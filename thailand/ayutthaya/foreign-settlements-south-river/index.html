Ayutthaya chapter 07 / south-bank field guide

Foreign settlements three histories, two banks

Ayutthaya's overseas story is not a row of matching national villages. Follow the Japanese memorial grounds on the east bank, Portuguese church archaeology opposite, and the Dutch VOC's separate trading-office site. The river explains their position; today's road crossings decide the visit.

READ THE RIVER MAP

A capital connected to the sea

UNESCO describes Ayutthaya as a major center of diplomacy and commerce, with foreign trader and missionary enclaves downstream from the royal palace.

EAST BANK

Japanese settlement

The modern Japanese Village is a memorial and interpretation site on the Chao Phraya's east bank, south of the island city.

OPPOSITE BANK

Portuguese quarter

The local tourism portal places the Portuguese Village on the west side of the river; St Peter's church remains an archaeological trace.

SEPARATE SITE

Dutch VOC office

Baan Hollanda interprets a Dutch trading-office location near Wat Phanan Choeng. Its current building is an information center, not the seventeenth-century lodge.

East bank / Ko Rian

A memorial landscape on the old settlement ground

The Japanese Village operator places the historic settlement along roughly a kilometre of the Chao Phraya's east bank, opposite the Portuguese quarter. The present visit is a layered memorial: the Thai-Japanese Association acquired land in 1935; a study-center annex and garden followed, and a Yamada Nagamasa and Thao Thong Kip Ma exhibition opened in 2015. Those buildings and garden interpret the past; they are not an intact seventeenth-century town.

START INSIDE

Read the paired biographies

Yamada Nagamasa is presented alongside Thao Thong Kip Ma. Keep both within Ayutthaya's court and trading networks rather than making either one person stand for the whole settlement.

THEN OUTSIDE

Follow the river edge

Use the bank to picture boat access south of the capital. The present flag poles, garden and pavilion belong to the memorial grounds, not the original settlement fabric.

BEFORE YOU GO

Ask about the exhibition

The operator's history page records later exhibition and garden projects. Confirm which rooms and displays are open through its official contact page before crossing to Ko Rian.

West bank / across the Chao Phraya

St Peter's ruins and a layered cemetery record

The provincial tourism portal's 2 April 2022 history dates the Portuguese arrival to 1511, during Ramathibodi II, and describes merchants and mercenaries. It names three Catholic communities: Dominican St Peter, Jesuit St Paul and a Franciscan church. The remaining St Peter church ruins and excavated burial ground are evidence of one community, not a full reconstruction of all three.

Read the excavation carefully

The official provincial article, published 28 February 2022, describes the St Peter complex in three parts: cemetery at the front, worship and priest burials in the middle, and residence behind and beside it. It reports 254 excavated skeletons and objects including coins, ceramics, glass bangles, pipes and devotional items. The small display building is an archaeological interpretation; check current access before planning an interior visit.

The local visitor page places this site opposite Japanese Village across the river and describes a road approach over a bridge. Put both exact pins in a driver's map; do not count on a direct foot ferry between them.

A different river community

Baan Hollanda interprets the VOC trading ground

The Baan Hollanda Foundation says Dutch merchants arrived in Ayutthaya in 1604 and received permission to establish a trading post in 1608. Its current information center stands at the historic office location and explains cargo, diplomacy and the Dutch East India Company. Excavated foundations and trade objects are distinct from the modern riverside building and exhibition.

WHY THE SITE MATTERS

Trade was organized

The VOC was a chartered company with commercial and political power. Baan Hollanda's account describes hides, tin, rice and imported goods moving through its Siam trade.

WHAT YOU SEE

Modern center, older ground

Use the exhibition's maps and document facsimiles to understand the settlement; do not label the present Dutch-style building a surviving VOC lodge.

HOW TO ADD IT

Make a separate road stop

The center is near Wat Phanan Choeng, rather than a third doorway in the Japanese-Portuguese pair. Confirm current exhibition operation and entrance details with the foundation.

A road-and-river half day

Choose two sites, then decide on the Dutch extension

Reserve about 3.5-5 hours for the Japanese Village and Portuguese site together as a planning estimate, plus road transfers and any wait for access. For these two riverbank sites, arrange one driver for the stop-to-stop road journey and agree the wait or pickup point at each site before departure. A bicycle suits only riders comfortable with local traffic and the weather.

1 / EAST BANK

Japanese Village

Start with the exhibition, then walk the garden and river edge. Allow roughly 60-90 minutes as an estimate; check the operator's current rooms, programs and hours.

2 / ROAD CROSSING

Portuguese site

Have the driver take you from Japanese Village to Chedi Wat Sam Pluem roundabout, then across the river by the official road approach to Portuguese Village (St Peter's ruins). Share both site pins and agree the pickup point and time at the exact entrance. Allow around 45-60 minutes for the church remains and interpretation if open; the cemetery display is not a guaranteed visit.

3 / OPTIONAL

Baan Hollanda

Add the Dutch center near Wat Phanan Choeng only if its current exhibition is operating and you have a separate road transfer. Give it its own block rather than rushing the first two sites.

RETURN

Agree the pickup

Before the first stop, book the driver to wait or collect at the agreed pickup point after both visits. If a site is closed, use the same driver for the agreed road return or continue to Baan Hollanda only with a separately confirmed transfer; do not improvise a river crossing.

Quick answers

Foreign settlements FAQ

Were the Japanese and Portuguese villages on the same bank?

No. The official provincial visitor page places them on opposite banks. Arrange one driver from Japanese Village via Chedi Wat Sam Pluem roundabout to Portuguese Village (St Peter's ruins), share both site pins, and agree the waiting or pickup point; do not assume a ferry.

Is today's Japanese Village an intact old town?

No. It is a modern memorial and interpretation landscape built on part of the historic settlement ground. The exhibition and garden explain the history; they are not surviving seventeenth-century streets.

What remains at the Portuguese site?

The local provincial history identifies St Peter's church ruins, archaeological finds and a cemetery display. It also records other churches, but they should not be assumed to survive as a complete visitable group.

Can I add the Dutch center to the same half day?

Possibly, if Baan Hollanda's exhibition is currently open and a road transfer fits. Its location near Wat Phanan Choeng is a separate stop, so leave it out when the two-bank visit is already tight.
