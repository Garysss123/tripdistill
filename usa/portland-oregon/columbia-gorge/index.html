Columbia Gorge / A transport choice before a waterfall list

Choose the corridor.
Then choose the falls.

A Gorge Express stop and a Historic Highway viewpoint are different access plans. Choose a limited route and let the return remain as clear as the outward journey.

The Multnomah permit is specific to an access point and season

ODOT’s Multnomah Falls permit page states that the 2026 summer timed-use permit season for the I-84 Exit 31 parking lot has ended, as checked on 11 September 2026. It says information for 2027 will be posted there and on Recreation.gov. Use the current year’s requirements for your travel date rather than applying an old summer booking rule throughout the year.

ODOT: Multnomah Falls Exit 31 permit season

That permit information does not establish parking availability, access to every Gorge trailhead or permission to stop on a road shoulder. Decide whether you are using a private vehicle, the Gorge Express or a confirmed tour before planning the sequence. Different access methods reach different places, and a ticket for one does not automatically cover another.

Drive for a selected Historic Highway route

Choose this for stops such as Crown Point that are not simply part of the Gorge Express’s listed stops. Check open roads and legal parking before adding them, and keep the number of walks modest.

Use the Gorge Express for its actual corridor

Choose this when its Portland, Multnomah Falls, Cascade Locks or Hood River service matches the visit. Read CAT’s own timetable and alerts; a map of the whole Gorge is not the route of this one bus.

Choose one main waterfall visit

A focused Multnomah visit can be the day’s principal purpose. A lower viewing area and a longer uphill trail are different activities. Decide which you actually want before adding several more waterfalls.

A road day with one constrained stop and a flexible remainder

  1. Portland → Crown Point when the road works

    Use the current road and Vista House information before choosing the Historic Highway approach. The viewpoint and building visit have different weather and operating considerations. Strong wind can make an exposed stop unappealing even when the drive itself is possible.

  2. Vista House · Understand the wider river setting

    Use the view and available interpretation to orient yourself along the Gorge instead of treating the building as merely a place to turn the car around. Keep the actual parking and public access clear. If the building is closed, describe the stop as an exterior viewpoint and do not assume an indoor shelter is available.

  3. Multnomah Falls · Use the correct access arrangement

    Reach the falls through the permitted route for your transport and season, then choose the open viewing area or a separately assessed trail. If parking or access fails, use the alternative already in your plan rather than stopping in a traffic lane or improvising a roadside drop-off.

  4. Return or continue deliberately

    Return toward Portland for a focused day, or give an onward Cascade Locks / Hood River visit a real block and account for the additional journey. Do not call the farther town a quick final stop after a long trail. The evening return should still work if lunch or traffic takes longer than expected.

The Gorge Express is useful, but not an all-viewpoint shuttle

CAT’s current page lists service between Portland, Troutdale, Multnomah Falls, Cascade Locks and Hood River. Its timetable identifies Gateway Transit Center as the Portland connection. Choose the outward and return services from the operator’s own table, including whether your particular trip serves the stop you want. Do not assume every departure stops at every listed place.

CAT also warns that construction can cause delays and that severe congestion can rarely force a Multnomah stop bypass. Read the current rider alert before leaving and keep the return flexible enough to accommodate a disruption. A bus day should not end with a flight or another critical appointment that depends on one exact pickup at the falls.

Crown Point is not an assumed bus stop

The Vista House version above is a different route from the Gorge Express’s listed corridor. If you want both, verify a separate operator or connection that actually serves the viewpoint. Do not leave a scheduled bus expecting a convenient walk along an unsuitable road to make the plans equivalent.

Choose the walk separately from the waterfall photograph

A roadside or lower viewing-area visit and an uphill hike have different needs for time, footwear, weather and ability. Check the land manager’s current trail information before committing to a bridge, upper viewpoint or longer loop. Rockfall, ice, fire damage and work can affect familiar paths even when an attraction remains visible from below.

Keep to open routes and use the return you selected. If the main visit has already used the group’s energy, omit another trail rather than treating a nearby waterfall name as a compulsory next step. For reduced walking, identify the specific usable viewing area and facilities, not a general label that the whole Gorge is accessible.

Arrange food and drinking water around the actual day. A scenic viewpoint is not a guaranteed meal stop, and leaving a booked transport window to search for lunch can unravel the return. A limited river-and-waterfall day is complete without a cross-river loop or a farther mountain added at the end.

Before adding another destination

Does a permit guarantee a parking space?

Read the current operator’s terms rather than assuming that permission to use an access point guarantees immediate parking. The season, access method and actual capacity are separate questions.

Can I use an old summer timetable or permit screenshot?

No. ODOT and CAT publish current information, and both seasons and operations can change. Save the correct information for your date, with enough flexibility for current alerts.
