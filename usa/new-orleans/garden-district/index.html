Washington Avenue → a public-street loop → Magazine Street

  1. St. Charles to Washington Avenue

    Begin with the actual stop and a manageable public sidewalk connection. Orient the group before walking farther into the district. This is an editorial starting area, not a surveyed accessible route; check the specific surface and curb crossings for your needs.

  2. Washington and Prytania surroundings

    Use public streets to observe the relationship between houses, gardens, setbacks and street trees. Keep entrances clear and remain outside private grounds. A distinctive facade can be appreciated without approaching a window or turning a resident’s gate into a meeting point.

  3. Choose a short street loop, not every named house

    Select a manageable portion toward streets such as Coliseum or Camp using the current local map, then return toward the chosen food area. Compare details between buildings rather than collecting a list of celebrity addresses. Omit another block when uneven paving, heat or the group’s pace makes it unhelpful.

  4. Magazine Street · Stop in one chosen stretch

    Choose the actual meal or shop area and give it time. Magazine Street extends well beyond this walk; do not treat its full length as the final short section of a Garden District visit. After the pause, use the return connection already selected.

Lafayette Cemetery No. 1 is closed to walk-in visits; book its sole official tour

The City still labels Lafayette Cemetery No. 1 closed to the public, so do not enter on your own or expect an ordinary neighborhood tour to open the gate. A new official exception is now available: after the City selected PRC’s Save Our Cemeteries as the sole tour operator, PRC began scheduled public visits in fall 2026.

City of New Orleans: current cemetery access

PRC Save Our Cemeteries: sole official Lafayette No. 1 tour and booking

PRC lists the 90-minute Lafayette No. 1 tour at 1400 Washington Avenue for $30 adults, $15 ages 6–12, and free under age 6. Check the live date and ticket availability before shaping this walking day around it; those listed prices were checked 12 October 2026. A reservation is for this named site only, not for other city cemeteries.

If a tour is unavailable, keep the outside-only plan

Use public sidewalks for the Washington–Prytania–Coliseum–Magazine sequence and respect the closed gate. Do not substitute a ticket for another cemetery or operator. The district remains a complete architecture and streetcar visit without interior access.

The tour uses about 90 minutes before you add the streetcar, street loop, meal or transfer. If that makes the day crowded, keep the tour and shorten the walk; if the published times do not fit, omit the tour rather than rushing between it and a meal reservation.

Make the meal a destination, not another long search

Choose a limited Magazine Street stretch that connects sensibly with the walk. Compare current menus, opening times and seating needs before arriving hungry. A cafe pause, a longer restaurant meal and browsing independent shops are different uses of the remaining afternoon.

If a specific restaurant is important, let its location and booking shape the end of the walk. Otherwise choose a suitable nearby option rather than crossing to a distant part of the city for another food name. Ask about ingredients directly and leave room for a real seated break when needed.

Shopping should follow the stores that are actually open and interesting to the group. A district’s reputation does not mean every storefront has the same merchandise or hours. One thoughtful stop can be a better ending than walking the full street in search of a generalized image of the neighborhood.

Decide afterward whether to return or take a separate short streetcar ride. Do not make an additional Uptown attraction compulsory because the line continues there. The visit already has a coherent beginning, walk and finish; anything more should earn its travel time.

Keep a shorter route that still has a purpose

For limited walking, choose a small public-street portion near a confirmed transport stop and a meal with suitable access. Check the actual sidewalk and entrance, not only a neighborhood-wide accessibility label. A slower pace should be part of the plan rather than something to recover by rushing the final blocks.

For rain or unsuitable outdoor conditions, change to a verified indoor visit elsewhere in the city and account for that transfer. The Historic New Orleans Collection can be a separate museum option when available, not a shelter assumed to be next door to the Garden District. A restaurant reservation alone does not make the connecting outdoor route suitable.

Before leaving the base

Can a Garden District tour enter Lafayette Cemetery No. 1 despite the closure?

Not on a walk-in basis. The City still lists it as closed to the public, but PRC Save Our Cemeteries is now the sole official tour operator and offers bookable scheduled visits. Check the City notice and PRC’s live ticket page; a general neighborhood tour does not grant entry.

Does the whole length of Magazine Street fit after the walk?

Choose one stretch around the actual meal or shops you want. The street is larger than this local itinerary and does not need to be walked end to end.
