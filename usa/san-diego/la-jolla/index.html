Three nearby names do not mean one beach

La Jolla Cove

Use the current lifeguard and city information for the public approach you plan to take. A viewpoint visit and entering the water are different activities. Check the exact path, tide and posted warnings before leaving the village.

Point La Jolla / Boomer Beach

The city identifies this rocky area as sea-lion habitat and states that it is closed year-round. Observe from a permitted public viewpoint. A visible stair, worn track or nearby beach does not create an authorized way down.

Children’s Pool

This is a separate harbor-seal location. The city lists seasonal beach-access closure during pupping season, 15 December through 15 May each year. Outside that window, follow the current signs and wildlife distance rules; the place name does not guarantee swimming access.

For a two-night visit, make one section the outing instead of trying to cover the Cove, the closed habitat and every village stop.

Keep the year-round and seasonal closures separate

The City of San Diego says Point La Jolla and Boomer Beach are closed year-round. Its Children’s Pool notice lists beach closure during harbor seal pupping season, 15 December–15 May. These rules apply to different sites. The seasonal window is not permission to enter Point La Jolla or Boomer Beach at another time.

Follow signs, barriers and lifeguard directions. Give seals and sea lions distance; do not approach, touch, feed, or try to move them for a photograph. If the permitted viewpoint is crowded, wildlife is absent or a path is restricted, accept that result and choose another open section or the village. Never use a closed stair, rock shelf or private access as a workaround.

A sighting is not part of the itinerary

Choose a lawful viewpoint and let the animals determine whether they are visible. If they are absent, the coast and village still make a complete land visit. A photograph is not a reason to cross a closure line.

Trace the transit chain before leaving Old Town

  1. Airport to the regional connection

    From SAN, use the published Flyer shuttle to Old Town Transit Center or Route 992 toward downtown, then confirm the onward MTS connection. The airport terminal itself has no Trolley station.

  2. Old Town or UTC to La Jolla Village

    MTS Route 30 runs between Old Town and UTC/VA Medical Center via Pacific Beach, La Jolla and UCSD. Its 6 September 2026 route sheet includes La Jolla Village stops around Silverado Street and Herschel Avenue. Check the live stop and direction for your date.

  3. Check the last mile to the actual viewpoint

    Route 30 does not promise a door-to-Cove transfer. Map the walk or a confirmed local connection from your chosen stop to the permitted public approach and back. If the route, slope or return does not suit your group, use a separately confirmed ride or stay in the village.

  4. Keep a turnaround and meal

    Choose one coastal section, then return to the village for a rest or meal. Continue only on a public, open path and if the group has time and energy. Do not add an uncertain beach access to fill a gap.

Use the forecast and lifeguard notice for the exact coast

Fog, wind, high surf, rain and strong currents can change a coastal day. The National Weather Service San Diego page gives current forecasts and alerts; San Diego Lifeguards publish beach information and direct visitors to local signs. Both are live check points, not a guarantee for a future visit. A comfortable air temperature does not establish safe water conditions.

For rain, high surf or a closure, make the day shorter and stay on a permitted public path, or move to a confirmed indoor visit and meal in the village. Do not assume a nearby cove is a safe substitute for a hazardous one. Any kayak, swim or guided activity needs its own operator, suitability, cancellation and sea-condition check; if it is cancelled, do not attempt the same trip independently.

If the planned coast is unavailable, return by the verified route and move the visit to another day. Balboa Park or a downtown museum can be a separate indoor alternative only after checking its opening and ticket availability. The plan should be able to change without entering habitat, using private access or making an unsafe last connection.

Before adding more to the day

Are Point La Jolla and Children’s Pool closed on the same schedule?

No. Point La Jolla/Boomer Beach is described as closed year-round; Children’s Pool has its own seasonal beach restriction. Check the exact place and current signs.

Does this page recommend swimming at the Cove?

No. It plans a land-based visit. A water activity needs a separate suitability and current-condition check.

Can I assume Route 30 ends at the coast?

No. It serves La Jolla/UTC corridors; map the final leg from the actual stop to an open public viewpoint and the return.
