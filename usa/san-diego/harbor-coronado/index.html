Three reasons to visit the harbor, three different scales

USS Midway Museum

Choose the ship when its history and interpretation are the main interest. Check the current ticket, opening time and accessibility details; a visit across the ship takes a real block. Keep the adjacent waterfront or meal as an optional finish, not a promise that a full crossing also fits.

Mainland waterfront and a short ferry

Choose a measured waterfront walk, meal and crossing when seeing the bay and another shore is the point. The ferry is transport between two terminals, not a harbor-sightseeing cruise and not an ocean-beach transfer.

Coronado-centered day

Choose this when the far-side town or ocean beach is the main purpose. The Coronado Ferry Landing is on the bay side. Add the onward connection and return before deciding whether the ferry works for your schedule.

For a one- or two-night downtown stop, choose either the museum or the crossing as the anchor. If staying in Coronado, compare the hotel’s actual location with the Ferry Landing and oceanfront, plus the last service back to the mainland. A lower room rate can cost more in repeated transfers; a split stay adds check-in and luggage time.

Give the ship its time before adding another shore

  1. Reach the actual museum entrance

    From a downtown hotel, check the waterfront walking route or current transit stop for the USS Midway address. Allow time for ticketing and security, and confirm which decks or spaces suit your group. A nearby pier is not the museum entrance.

  2. Choose a few exhibits to follow

    Use the museum’s interpretation to understand how the vessel worked and how people lived aboard. Spend time with a smaller number of spaces instead of trying to turn every room and aircraft into a quick photograph.

  3. Pause on the mainland

    Have a meal or a short public waterfront walk, then decide honestly whether a ferry still fits. If the museum held your attention, ending nearby is a complete day.

  4. If crossing, use the named terminal

    Flagship lists separate departures from Broadway Pier and Fifth Avenue Landing. Match the route table to the pier you can actually reach, and keep the return from Coronado in view before boarding.

Do not combine a full museum day, both ferry terminals and an ocean-beach visit into a short afternoon. When the crossing is the main purpose, start at the correct pier and leave the museum for another day. When a sailing is cancelled or the timetable no longer fits, remain on the mainland or choose a checked alternative; do not improvise a crossing.

Broadway Pier and Fifth Avenue Landing use different tables

Flagship publishes a Broadway Pier–Coronado Ferry Landing route and a separate Fifth Avenue Landing route. Confirm your departure address, direction, ticket and return sailing on the operator’s current table. Do not use a time from the other pier or assume a boat leaves at the interval shown in a past snapshot.

Flagship: departure addresses and directions

On 13 October 2026, the Broadway route page showed a 15-minute crossing and a $9 one-way adult fare in its current schedule snapshot. Those figures are time-sensitive; schedules can vary with date, capacity and operating conditions. Verify the exact day, fare, accessibility and bicycle terms before relying on them. A harbor cruise ticket is not the transport ferry ticket.

The ferry landing is not the ocean beach

The Coronado Ferry Landing is on San Diego Bay. Decide on the onward trip before leaving it. If the connection or return is not confirmed, enjoy the bay-side landing or keep the day on the mainland.

Choose the bay-side pause or plan the ocean connection

Stay near the Ferry Landing

Use the bay-side public area for a modest walk, meal or view back to downtown. This works when time is limited or the crossing itself is the outing. Check the return ferry before settling into a long break.

Continue toward Hotel del or the ocean

MTS Route 904’s route sheet effective 7 June 2026 shows the Ferry Landing, Orange Avenue and Hotel del/Strand-area stops. Confirm that Route 904 operates on your travel date, its direction, hours and return. If it does not fit, use a separately confirmed ride or stop at the bay; do not assume a walk across the bridge or an unverified last mile.

Give Coronado a separate stay

A longer stay can separate the ferry arrival from a slower town or ocean visit. Compare the hotel’s exact address, beach access, baggage plan and first mainland connection on departure day. The island name alone does not tell you how far it is from either shore.

City of Coronado: beach access and local rules

The ferry, local bus and beach are three separate legs. The Route 904 PDF is a dated route sheet, not proof of service on every future day. If any leg is unavailable, keep the outing at the bay landing, return on the checked ferry, or reschedule the ocean visit. Never treat the Coronado Bridge as an improvised pedestrian return.

Fog, wind or rough water can affect the crossing and shore plan. Check the operator’s same-day notices, the National Weather Service San Diego forecast, City of Coronado beach information and lifeguard guidance before leaving. A cancelled boat or hazardous beach is a reason to stay mainland-side or choose an indoor museum after checking its hours—not to bypass a closure or take an unsafe route.

Before adding more to the day

Does the ferry arrive at Coronado’s ocean beach?

No. It arrives at the bay-side Ferry Landing. Plan a separate confirmed onward connection or keep the visit near the landing.

Can I use either mainland pier for the same departure time?

No. Broadway Pier and Fifth Avenue Landing are separate terminals with separate route tables.

Will Route 904 be running when I visit?

The route PDF checked here is effective 7 June 2026. Confirm the live date-specific timetable and return before building the day around it.
