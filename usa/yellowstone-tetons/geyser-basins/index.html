Yellowstone & Grand Teton

Yellowstone Geyser Basins

The geyser basins are fragile thermal landscapes, not ordinary walking terrain. Boardwalks provide access to dramatic features, and a predicted eruption should be treated as an estimate rather than a guaranteed appointment.

A practical visit plan

Choose one basin area for the morning and walk only open boardwalks and paths. Use current ranger information for eruption predictions. Add another nearby feature only if road time and daylight allow, leaving room for slow traffic.

02

What to check before leaving

Stay on designated surfaces and keep children close. Thermal water and thin crust can cause severe injury. Check closures and parking conditions, and never enter restricted ground to improve a photograph.

Fit this visit into your trip

Yellowstone and Grand Teton combine long park roads with geothermal landscapes and wildlife. Entrance selection and overnight bases matter; a single day cannot do justice to both parks.

More ways to explore Yellowstone & Grand Teton

Yellowstone Canyon & Wildlife Valleys

The canyon and northern valleys offer different scenery from the geyser basins. Wildlife viewing is uncertain and should be based on patient observation from legal pullouts, not on approaching animals or chasing sightings.

Grand Teton & Jackson

Grand Teton offers mountain views, lakes and substantial hiking, with Jackson as one possible gateway. A scenic roadside visit can stand alone; a boat crossing or mountain trail adds separate schedules and preparation.
