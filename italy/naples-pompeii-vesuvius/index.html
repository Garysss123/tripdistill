Naples, Pompeii & Vesuvius

Give Naples, Pompeii, Herculaneum and Vesuvius their own clear place in the trip.

Naples is a living city with Greek, Roman, Bourbon and modern layers. Pompeii preserves a broad commercial town; Herculaneum offers a smaller residential grid with unusually rich organic remains; Vesuvius is a separate park and access decision. Treat them as connected histories with different entrances, transport and days.

Park maps, opening lists, museum collection plans and rail services can change. Keep a complete Naples or MANN day available when a site route changes.

COMPARE BEFORE COMMITTING

Every route gains something by leaving something else out.

Farnese-to-decumani day plan

Naples Centre, MANN & the Waterfront

Gateway: Museo and Dante stations serve different edges of the old centre; Toledo and Municipio serve the western civic line. Check ANM’s current map and service before setting the first stop. Regional EAV trains use separate platforms at Garibaldi and do not leave from the metro entrance marked Centrale.

Sacrifice: A focused MANN visit can absorb the attention needed for the old centre. A historic-street day gives up a long gallery block; a westward walk trades archaeological context for civic architecture and the bay. Choose one main direction, then leave a real meal and a straightforward return.

Time: Editorial estimate: allow five to seven hours for one MANN collection block, a nearby old-centre walk and a meal. A full western civic-and-waterfront route works better as its own day.

Gate-to-rail route map

Pompeii Archaeological City

Gateway: The Park pairs Porta Marina and Piazza Esedra with the EAV Napoli–Sorrento stop Pompei Villa dei Misteri; Piazza Anfiteatro is approached from Pompei Santuario on the Napoli–Poggiomarino line. Trenitalia’s Pompei station is separate and has its own onward connection. Match operator, stop, gate and intended exit before boarding.

Sacrifice: Pompeii’s 44-hectare excavated city cannot be read well as a rapid sweep. A civic route leaves distant houses for another visit; a domestic route gives up more public buildings; the accessible itinerary covers a different, carefully planned line. Keep water, shade and the chosen exit ahead of the checklist.

Time: Editorial estimate: plan four to six hours inside the site for one themed route, one confirmed building and rest breaks, plus the train and entrance approach. A wider suburban-villa ticket is a separate, longer day.

Shoreline-to-crater decision map

Herculaneum or Vesuvius

Gateway: For Herculaneum, the Park directs rail visitors from Napoli Centrale to the EAV Circumvesuviana Napoli–Sorrento service and Ercolano Scavi; its directions estimate about six minutes on foot from the station to Corso Resina 187. Ask about the blue Ercolano per tutti route if you need its accessible itinerary. Vesuvius access is separately controlled by the National Park.

Sacrifice: Herculaneum is not a small version of Pompeii: its visible domestic and commercial blocks, surviving organic material and partly buried public zone reward slow close reading. The crater is an exposed, status-dependent walk. Combining both major sites compresses their different histories and leaves little margin for the uphill return to Ercolano Scavi.

Time: Editorial estimate: allow four to six hours for Herculaneum with travel and a slow site visit. Give a Vesuvius crater window its own day, with transport and safety margin.
