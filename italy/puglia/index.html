Southern Italy

Puglia

Choose one rail spine or one coast before the heel widens around you.

Plan Puglia as two rail-and-street systems with a deliberate handover: Bari and the Adriatic main line in the north, then Lecce and one chosen Salento coast in the south. Alberobello and the Itria Valley sit on a different branch network; Castel del Monte and many beaches add a last mile that a map pin cannot solve. Choose the base first, then verify the exact operator, station and return for each day.

PLAN YOUR STAY

Plan where to stay, how to get around and what to do if plans change.

BASE

Choose a place to stay that suits your plans.

For four to six nights, use two bases rather than moving every night: stay around Bari Centrale or the Murat grid for rail arrivals and the Bari Vecchia walk, then move to Lecce’s historic centre for its evening streets and one coast day. With three nights, choose the north (Bari, Trani and one Itria day) or the south (Lecce and one coast). An Alberobello or Locorotondo overnight earns its luggage move only when an early or late town visit matters; a countryside masseria needs a confirmed car or transfer plan.

TRANSFER

Check how you will reach the starting point.

Trenitalia is the first check for the Adriatic corridor and Trani; FS Sud Est serves branch routes toward Alberobello and the Salento, including Otranto and Gallipoli. Treat operator, station, replacement bus and ticket as separate checks. Castel del Monte is an inland last-mile commitment, while coastal beaches may sit beyond the rail stop: confirm the onward link and return before reserving a timed visit or leaving the base.

SEASON

Check conditions for the time of year.

In July and August, put the exposed stone-town walk in the cooler part of the day, keep an indoor or shaded pause for midday and expect the coast to attract heavier visitor demand. Outside the main beach season, do not assume a bus, beach facility or attraction keeps its summer pattern. Weekday, Sunday, holiday and works notices can change the useful branch connection in any month; build the day from the dated operator journey planner.

FALLBACK

Keep a nearby alternative in mind.

Keep a complete walkable day in the current base. From Bari, use the old town, castle area and waterfront; from Lecce, link the central squares and Baroque streets. If a rural bus or branch return does not work, drop the excursion instead of inventing a taxi connection or crossing to the other coast.

COMPARE BEFORE COMMITTING

Every route gains something by leaving something else out.

Port-to-fortress hinge

Bari, Trani & Castel del Monte

Gateway: Use Bari Centrale for the city or the exact Trenitalia calling point for Trani; save the return before starting the walk. Castel del Monte is inland and not beside a rail platform. Identify the date-valid rail or bus gateway, who operates the onward leg, where it leaves you and how it returns before treating the monument as reachable.

Sacrifice: Bari and Trani reward a walk joined by their own station and street grids. Castel del Monte changes the day into a timed rural transfer and a site visit. Adding it to both cities would trade the cathedral, harbour and meal for connection risk, so select one operating system.

Time: Guide estimates: keep a full day for Bari, Trani or Castel del Monte. Bari and Trani can fill one long day only if the date-specific trains leave time for the cathedral and harbour walk; do not add the fortress to that pair. Treat these as planning estimates, not measured visits.

Trulli bus-radius grid

Alberobello & the Itria Valley

Gateway: Alberobello, Locorotondo and Martina Franca use the FS Sud Est network; Ostuni’s Trenitalia station is outside its hilltop centre. Match the operator, exact station, bus stop or replacement service, and final return for the date. In Alberobello, the viewpoint, two trulli districts and museum are a town walk rather than three independent transfers.

Sacrifice: A rail-and-bus day keeps the driver out of the picture but limits how many towns fit and in what order. A car can connect the rural stops only if the driver also plans parking, any ZTL approach and the old-town walk; neither choice turns the whole valley into one effortless loop.

Time: Guide estimate: allow most of a day for Alberobello’s two districts, interpretation space and meal. A second town can turn it into a long day once the actual train or bus gaps are counted; do not plan a fixed number of hours from a generic route search.

Salento dual-coast clock

Lecce, Otranto & Gallipoli

Gateway: Lecce rail and bus departures serve different operators and are not one interchangeable stop. Otranto and Gallipoli sit on separate FS Sud Est branches; check the exact departure point, any transfer or replacement coach, the walk from the destination stop into the old town and the final return to Lecce.

Sacrifice: Lecce itself gives a dense walk between Roman, Baroque and civic landmarks. Otranto and Gallipoli each add a coastal old town, but on opposite sides of the peninsula. Visiting both turns the day into a connection test; choose one coast or overnight there.

Time: Guide estimates: reserve five to seven hours for Lecce’s core on foot with an indoor visit and meal. Give a coast its own day; combining it with a full city itinerary depends on the published service and can leave little time beyond the old-town walk.
