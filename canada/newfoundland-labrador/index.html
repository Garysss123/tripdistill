Newfoundland & Labrador

choose the coast before counting stops.

Treat Newfoundland as three different stays: St. John’s for the Avalon, Bonavista Peninsula for Trinity and Bonavista, and Gros Morne on the west coast. The road links are the trip, so do not turn these into day trips from one base. Labrador is on the mainland and needs its own confirmed road, ferry or flight connection.

Useful stay

For all three Newfoundland areas, plan about 10–12 nights: 3 in St. John’s, 2 on the Bonavista Peninsula, 4 near Gros Morne and 1–3 nights for long transfers or weather slack. With only a week, choose the east (St. John’s plus Bonavista) or west (Gros Morne) instead of trying to cross the island.

Transfer logic

A rental car gives the most control for the dispersed coastal and park stops; confirm pickup, insurance and seasonal road suitability before booking. St. John’s has an urban bus operator, but do not assume it reaches headlands or replaces a regional transfer. Flights and Marine Atlantic ferries reach Newfoundland; Labrador is a separate mainland itinerary with different road and ferry legs. Check official live status rather than relying on a remembered timetable.

Season gate

Checked 12 October 2026: Parks Canada listed Signal Hill and Ryan Premises visitor seasons as June 1–October 9, while Signal Hill grounds and Gros Morne National Park are open year-round. Gros Morne is in its low season from October to May; services, tours, daylight and trail conditions change. Fog, wind and rain can affect exposed coast at any time, and wildlife sightings are never guaranteed.

Fallback

Keep one lower-risk day ready.

When fog or wind removes a headland or boat outing, keep the day in its current base: use St. John’s museums and steep-street neighborhoods, Trinity or Bonavista heritage interiors when open, or a lower-elevation Gros Morne trail and community stop. Move the exposed activity to the clearest available day; do not make up lost stops after dark.

Respect

Community guidance outranks a map pin.
