Downtown, Stanley Park & Granville Island

Link Canada Place and Coal Harbour to one measured Stanley Park walk, then cross False Creek to a former industrial island that now combines its Public Market, artist studios and working waterfront.

Plan this route

Make the park loop earn its place

This route joins the city’s harbour edge, a major urban rainforest and a repurposed industrial waterfront. The park’s loop is the long block; the market and Net Loft are a slower second neighborhood, not a quick transit interchange.

Getting there

From downtown, walk the harbour edge into Stanley Park. The City measures the seawall around the park at 9 km and 2–3 hours on foot; Granville Island is across False Creek, reached by a TransLink bus, a small passenger ferry or a longer walk. Pick the ferry pier or bus stop from your hotel side before heading south.

Time to allow

Plan roughly 6–8 hours including meals and transfers. The City’s 9 km seawall loop is 2–3 hours on foot by itself; a shorter Coal Harbour–Brockton Point out-and-back leaves room for the market and a relaxed lunch.

How to extend the day

Keep the North Shore and Whistler for separate days. This day already has two distinct walking districts and a cross-creek transfer; add a museum only if it replaces part of the loop.

Route sequence

Coal Harbour to Brockton Point, then choose your seawall length

Use the City park map and seawall notices for closures, especially winter rock-slope work between Prospect Point and Third Beach. Check the Public Market hours, TransLink connection and the selected False Creek ferry operator on your date.

01Arrive

Start at the water

Begin at Canada Place or the Olympic Cauldron in Coal Harbour. The convention-centre harbour edge gives you the cruise terminal, seaplanes and mountain horizon in one view; continue west toward the park rather than walking back through downtown.

02Move

Choose Stanley Park depth

At Brockton Point, read the names and explanations beside the First Nations totem poles before following the seawall. Construction began in 1917 and the loop was completed in 1980; a plaque to master mason James Cunningham is above the path at Siwash Rock. The City’s loop is 9 km, so choose a partial section if the aquarium, beaches or a long meal are also on your list.

03Read

Cross to a working waterfront

Choose a bus or passenger ferry across False Creek to Granville Island. The Public Market and Net Loft reward a slow indoor/outdoor browse; the island’s 1970s industrial-to-public transformation is also visible in its studios, cultural venues and active marine edge.

04Return

Return from the correct pier

Check which ferry landing or bus stop is closest to your next neighborhood, then finish around Yaletown, Olympic Village or your hotel. The False Creek boats are separate operators from TransLink, so do not assume a transit timetable or fare covers them.

If the day changes

Use the waterfront in shorter, more sheltered pieces

Plan B

Rain or wind: make Brockton Point and one sheltered park interior the outdoor section, then spend the longer block in the Public Market or an open gallery. Do not book a fixed-time ferry as your only way back; check current ferry and bus service.

What can change the route

Three checks for this place and this date.

01

Winter seawall work

City maintenance can close the Prospect Point–Third Beach segment for about three weeks in winter. Follow the posted detour instead of assuming the waterfront remains a continuous loop.

02

Two path lanes

The seawall separates people walking from wheeled traffic. Keep to the signed walking side and allow extra room on sunny weekends, which the City flags as busy periods.

03

Trying to see the whole park

A full loop already uses 2–3 walking hours. Add only one major interior attraction before Granville Island, or shorten the park segment instead of rushing the market and last connection.

Questions for this route

Downtown, Stanley Park & Granville Island FAQ

How long is the Stanley Park seawall walk?

The City lists the loop around the park as 9 km and estimates 2–3 hours on foot, before photo stops, beaches, food or an interior attraction. Its wider Seaside Greenway is 28 km, so check which route you are following.

What is Granville Island beyond the Public Market?

The Public Market is one anchor within a mixed waterfront of studios, the Net Loft, cultural venues, eateries and marine uses. CMHC dates its change from industrial land to a public destination to the 1970s.

Should I walk or cycle the seawall?

The City marks separate paths: the pedestrian lane is nearest the water and the inner lane is for cycling and inline skating. Pick one mode and keep to its signs, especially on busy sunny days.
