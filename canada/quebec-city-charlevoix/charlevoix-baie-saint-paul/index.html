Charlevoix & Baie-Saint-Paul

Use Baie-Saint-Paul as the town base, then choose either a cultural day among its galleries and compact main streets or a mountain-park day in Grands-Jardins. The high trail looks across the Charlevoix impact crater, a landscape story that the riverside town and the trailhead reveal in very different ways.

Arrival and base

A car or prebooked regional operator is needed for the Grands-Jardins trailhead; the seasonal Train de Charlevoix reaches a town stop, not the park. The 2026 Québec train schedule gave visitors about four and a half hours in Baie-Saint-Paul before the return, enough for a town visit but not a mountain hike.

What this day gives you

Baie-Saint-Paul is a walkable arts-and-service base near the river; Grands-Jardins is a quiet high-elevation park with exposed trail and fragile Arctic-alpine plants. The Mont-du-Lac-des-Cygnes trail is not a casual scenic pull-off: it climbs 480 metres over 8.6 kilometres and is rated difficult.

Weather or closure fallback

If cloud hides the summit or trail conditions are unsuitable, stay in Baie-Saint-Paul for its galleries, cafés and town walk. For a shorter park outing, Sépaq lists La Chouenne as a 4.6-kilometre, two-hour intermediate trail, but check its current opening and conditions too.

01Arrive

Arrive in Baie-Saint-Paul

Walk the compact centre before driving farther. Use the galleries and main street to experience the town’s contemporary art identity, then check the next day’s mountain forecast and park notices.

02Move

Choose a park objective

For a summit day, drive to the Mont-du-Lac-des-Cygnes visitor centre on Route 381 at kilometre 21. The 8.6-kilometre round trip is rated difficult, takes about four hours and gains 480 metres.

03Read

Stay on the marked route

The summit looks across the Charlevoix crater and contains rare, fragile Arctic-alpine vegetation. Remain on the trail edge and do not treat open ground as a viewpoint shortcut. If that climb is too much, compare the shorter La Chouenne route on Sépaq’s current trail page.

04Return

Keep the return local

Return to Baie-Saint-Paul for a meal and a second night, or make the next regional transfer after rest. If using the train, use the posted town timetable rather than assuming it stops at the park.

Failure points

Three reasons to change the plan.

01

Trail date and condition

The 2026 seasonal summit-trail window shown by Sépaq ends 12 October. Snow, wind or a closure can remove the route even while the town remains accessible.

02

Summit cloud

The crater view is the point of the difficult ascent. Check a mountain-level forecast and keep Baie-Saint-Paul as a complete low-visibility alternative.

03

Fragile summit plants

The Arctic-alpine vegetation is rare and delicate. Stay on marked ground even when an informal shortcut looks clear.
