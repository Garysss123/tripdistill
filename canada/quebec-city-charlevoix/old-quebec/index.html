Old Québec & the Fortified City

Read the UNESCO district as a vertical city, not a single postcard square: the Upper Town’s surviving wall and cliff-top institutions above the Lower Town’s Place Royale and former harbour. Walk one level at a time, then use the descent to understand how the two halves fit.

Four-stage route

A sequence that protects the return.

Check Parks Canada site and guided-tour hours, current access to the ramparts, RTC service, funicular operation and winter stair conditions. On 7 October 2026 Parks Canada listed daily guided tours from 19 June to 30 August, with availability at the Frontenac kiosk from 31 August to 12 October; confirm at the kiosk because tours depend on staff availability and weather.

01Arrive

Read the Upper Town edge

Begin at Dufferin Terrace, where the cliff and river make the defensive position legible. Follow an open section of the ramparts and note how gates and bastions close the old city around the heights.

02Move

Look below the terrace

When open, visit the Saint-Louis Forts and Châteaux archaeological site. The excavated remains beneath Dufferin Terrace explain the governors’ seat that held French and British power for more than two centuries.

03Read

Descend once to Place Royale

Take the chosen stair or funicular down toward Petit-Champlain and Place Royale. The Lower Town grew around the square and harbour; look for the tighter street fabric and trade-facing scale rather than treating it as only a shopping lane.

04Return

Return by the route that fits

Use a gradual uphill street, the funicular or a stair that matches your ability and current surface. If the lower streets are crowded, finish along the waterfront and return by RTC rather than retracing every alley.

Failure points

Three reasons to change the plan.

01

Vertical effort

The route includes a real climb even when the map distance is short. Ice, wet stone and crowded stair landings can change the safe direction.

02

Seasonal interpretation

The archaeological site and guided programs have published opening windows; the walls and the indoor interpretation do not share one schedule.

03

Crowd bottlenecks

Cruise arrivals and narrow Petit-Champlain lanes can slow the lower-town segment. Start early or reverse the timing after checking port activity.
