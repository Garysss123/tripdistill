Whitehorse & Kluane

Choose the base before the road.

Choose Yukon by where you can spend the night: Whitehorse for its riverfront and services, or Haines Junction for Kluane’s road-accessible edge. Parks Canada places the park 160 km west of Whitehorse; its interior generally requires flightseeing or a serious mountaineering or ski expedition.

Movement

Season

Use the local condition, not a national average.

Summer daylight supports a city walk and a full park day, but can hide fatigue. Shoulder-season and winter snow, ice, shorter light and reduced services narrow the choices. Visitor-centre hours are seasonal; dark skies make aurora possible but never guaranteed.

Fallback

Keep one lower-risk day ready.

If a high trail, road or flightseeing plan fails, switch to an open lower route such as the 0.5-km Kokanee boardwalk at Kathleen Lake, or use the Haines Junction visitor centre only when it is operating. On 12 October 2026, Parks Canada listed the Kathleen Lake campground and oTENTik as closed until May 2027. Whitehorse’s river and cultural route is a separate weather-buffer day; recheck every site and road before leaving.

Respect

Community guidance outranks a map pin.

Follow current park, community and Indigenous tourism guidance for access, photography, wildlife and cultural places. Proximity never implies permission.

First-visit plan

Two bases, one moveable mountain day

Keep the town day, road transfer and park choice in separate blocks. This is an itinerary frame; live services, trail notices and weather decide what can actually run.

01

Day 1 · Whitehorse

Stay in town, check the City transit schedule, then choose one open cultural stop and a Yukon River walk. Do not add Miles Canyon if it turns the arrival day into a long hike before an aurora night.

02

Day 2 · Move west

Drive the 160-km Alaska Highway leg to Haines Junction only after checking Yukon 511, the forecast and the fuel or lodging services you will rely on. Confirm those stops directly; do not infer that every map pin is open.

03

Day 3 · Kluane choice

Choose one trail by footing and ability: the accessible 0.5-km Kokanee boardwalk, Parks Canada’s 7.6-km St. Elias Lake day hike listed at 2–4 hours, or a more demanding route only when current notices and conditions support it. Keep another night nearby if weather can move the outing.

Planning answers

Whitehorse & Kluane FAQ

How many nights should I split between Whitehorse and Kluane?

For a first road-led visit, use about two Whitehorse nights and two or three in Haines Junction. The second base prevents a 160-km-each-way park drive from consuming the hiking day and gives weather a chance to move the outing.

Can I use Whitehorse city transit to reach Kluane trailheads?

No. Whitehorse Transit is for the city; Parks Canada says there is no public transportation to Kluane trailheads. Arrange your own vehicle or a confirmed operator, and check the highway before departure.

Can I drive into the interior of Kluane?

Not on an ordinary park road. Parks Canada says the interior is generally reached by flightseeing or a serious mountaineering or ski-tour expedition. A roadside visit and a flightseeing trip are different plans.

Are visitor centres and camping open all year?

No fixed year-round opening should be assumed. The current Parks Canada page lists seasonal visitor-centre dates, and its 12 October 2026 Kathleen Lake listing says the campground and oTENTik are closed until May 2027. Recheck the exact season before lodging or trail planning.
