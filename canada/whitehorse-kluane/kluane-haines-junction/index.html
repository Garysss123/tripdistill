Kluane & Haines Junction

Use Haines Junction as the overnight gateway, then choose one Kluane road-accessible sector and a trail that matches ability, footing and daylight. Parks Canada places the park 160 km west of Whitehorse, says trailheads lack public transit and notes that the interior is generally accessed by flightseeing or a serious expedition.

Arrival and base

Drive the Alaska Highway west from Whitehorse to Haines Junction; Parks Canada gives the distance as 160 km. The trailheads are dispersed and have no public transport. Check Yukon 511, weather, verified services and Parks Canada closures before the road leg.

What this day gives you

Two or three Haines Junction nights cost more lodging moves than a day trip but give the Kluane outing a local start and a weather-move option. Whitehorse is a services base, not a close trailhead.

Weather or closure fallback

When cloud, wind, smoke, snow or a trail closure removes the high route or flightseeing, choose the currently open short option such as the Kokanee boardwalk or a lower road-side walk. If those are closed or icy, use an operating visitor centre or stay in town; do not replace a closed trail with an unmarked route.

Access

Drive the Alaska Highway west from Whitehorse to Haines Junction; Parks Canada gives the distance as 160 km. The trailheads are dispersed and have no public transport. Check Yukon 511, weather, verified services and Parks Canada closures before the road leg.

Combine carefully

Sleep in Haines Junction before and after the park day. Do not drive 160 km from Whitehorse, take a long hike or flightseeing trip, then plan to drive the same distance back late.

Choose the day’s anchor

Choose the Kluane day by effort and access

All three choices start from a road-access plan. Confirm the exact trail notice and weather first; the listed distances and times come from Parks Canada and are not promises about your pace.

Short and lower walking

Kathleen Lake · Kokanee boardwalk

Parks Canada describes a 0.5-km wheelchair-accessible boardwalk from the Kathleen Lake day-use area. It suits a scenic, lower-effort visit when the day-use area and route are open; it is not a substitute for an accessible journey from Whitehorse.

Moderate day-hike choice

St. Elias Lake

The Parks Canada trail listing gives 7.6 km return and 2–4 hours on easy terrain. Check the current trail, bridges, footing and daylight, and leave room for a slower return.

Longer mountain effort

Auriol or King’s Throne

Parks Canada lists Auriol as a 15-km loop taking 4–6 hours at moderate difficulty; King’s Throne is a steep 10-km return, 4–6 hours and difficult. Pick one only if the group, weather and return margin fit.

01Transfer

Make the road a separate trip leg

Choose a departure after checking Yukon 511 and the local forecast. Confirm fuel, food and lodging services directly; the site does not publish a guaranteed service timetable for this corridor.

02Check

Check the visitor and trail notices

Use the Parks Canada Kluane page for seasonal visitor-centre hours and important bulletins. As checked on 12 October 2026, its two listed visitor-centre periods had ended; a closed desk means you need the online trail and safety information before leaving town.

03Choose

Choose one effort level

Use Kokanee for the short lake-side boardwalk, St. Elias Lake for a longer easy-terrain day, or a strenuous trail only when ability, forecast, trail status and daylight agree. Carry the gear and bear-safety plan appropriate to the route.

04Return

Return to Haines Junction

Finish close to your lodging and reassess the next day. Move a weather-sensitive flight or hike within your extra night if possible; do not add the long drive back to Whitehorse after a full outing.

Failure points

Three reasons to change the plan.

01

Trailhead access

Parks Canada says trailheads can be difficult to reach without a vehicle and that no public transportation serves them. Confirm your actual ride before selecting a trail.

02

Mountain weather and visibility

Kluane conditions can differ from a Whitehorse city forecast. Use the local forecast, park bulletins and operator call before committing to an exposed route or scenic flight.

03

Seasonal services

Visitor centres and Kathleen Lake overnight facilities publish seasonal dates. A listing from another month or year does not establish that a facility is open for your trip.

Planning answers

Kluane & Haines Junction FAQ

Is Kluane a Whitehorse day trip?

Whitehorse is 160 km from Kluane according to Parks Canada, and the dispersed trailheads have no public transport. A park hike as a same-day there-and-back leaves a long road day around the activity; base in Haines Junction for a focused route and weather margin.

Which trail is the easiest starting point?

Parks Canada lists the 0.5-km Kokanee boardwalk at Kathleen Lake as wheelchair accessible, and St. Elias Lake as a 7.6-km return, 2–4-hour route on easy terrain. Check current access and choose based on your actual needs and the day’s footing.

Can I take a bus to a trailhead?

Parks Canada says there is no public transportation to Kluane trailheads. Arrange a vehicle or confirm a specific operator and pickup before lodging or trail bookings.

Will flightseeing always run if booked?

No. Parks Canada describes the remote interior as generally accessed by flightseeing or a serious mountaineering or ski expedition. Weather can change flight operations; read the operator’s cancellation terms and use the road-accessible trails as a separate fallback.

Is the Kathleen Lake campground open in October?

The Parks Canada page checked on 12 October 2026 listed the campground and oTENTik as closed until May 2027. Recheck the page for a later season, and do not make those facilities the fallback for a shoulder-season trip.
