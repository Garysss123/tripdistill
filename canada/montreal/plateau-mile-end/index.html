Plateau & Mile End

Walk from a Plateau commercial avenue into Mile End, pairing outdoor staircases and green alleys with former garment blocks, a church landmark and one meal.

Trace the routeCheck route details
Plan this visit

Walk north once; do not retrace the hill

The Plateau is the larger borough; its busier southern avenues and parks give way northward to Mile End’s lower residential blocks, former factories and Saint-Laurent/Saint-Viateur storefronts. The area’s history includes Jewish garment workers, postwar Greek settlement near Park Avenue and Portuguese businesses farther south. These are examples from across the wider Plateau, not a complete account of Mile End or a label for today’s residents.

Getting there

Laurier or Mont-Royal on the Orange line work for a southern start. Rosemont on the Orange line or Outremont and Acadie on the Blue line may fit a northern finish; choose the exact endpoint before walking so the route stays one-way.

Time to allow

Set aside 4–6 hours for a one-way walk, a meal or bakery stop and a pause at Parc Lahaie. This is an editorial range, not a shop timetable; queues, weather and the chosen endpoint change the pace.

How to extend the day

A Mount Royal visit works only if you finish near the chosen park approach and still have daylight and energy. Otherwise let this be the whole walking day; the summit is a separate uphill outing.

Route sequence

From the Plateau avenues into Mile End

01Arrive

Choose one Plateau avenue

Start near Laurier or Mont-Royal station. Walk Avenue du Mont-Royal or Duluth, not both end to end. Look at the iron staircases and balconies from the sidewalk: these narrow triplex homes became common as the district grew, and exterior stairs kept interior space for residents.

02Walk

Follow the seam north

Use Saint-Laurent Boulevard as the broad dividing street, then take Saint-Viateur toward Waverly. The 1914–15 St. Michael and St. Anthony Church rises above the blocks beside Café Olimpico; view its dome and narrow tower from the sidewalk, and check the parish schedule before entering because it is an active worship space.

03Explore

Pause at Parc Lahaie

The small square marks an older village centre. Nearby Saint-Laurent and Saint-Viateur mix storefronts, cafés and former industrial buildings. Pointe-à-Callière’s neighborhood history documents Jewish garment work, a postwar Greek community near Park Avenue and 1950s Portuguese businesses farther south—historical examples across the Plateau, not a complete Mile End timeline.

04Finish

Finish on your chosen line

Choose a bakery, meal or cultural stop whose hours you have checked, then end near Rosemont, Outremont or Acadie according to the actual service you need. Do not photograph private stairs or linger at home windows; the appeal of the route is a lived-in neighborhood.

If the day changes

Keep the visit worthwhile with a shorter outdoor section.

Plan B

In rain, choose one avenue, a meal and a short indoor stop; use a park only when paths are clear. In winter, plan two warm stops around one short outdoor walk, and do not depend on a particular bakery or shop without checking its current hours.

Before you go

Check the exact entrance and opening status.

Before leaving, check the STM map and service/elevator alerts, then confirm hours for the specific bakery, parish or cultural stop you want to enter. Street closures and independent business schedules vary by block and date.

What can change the route

Three practical checks for this day.

01

One-way distance

Plateau and Mile End are broad areas rather than adjoining plazas. Set your end station first and shorten the route if weather or tired legs make the northbound walk too long.

02

Homes and active worship

Outdoor stairs, balconies and the church are part of residents’ daily lives. Stay on public sidewalks, keep entrances clear and follow parish signs if visiting inside.

03

Shop hours

Bakery counters and independent stores may open or close at different times. Check the one stop you care about; the walk remains worthwhile without a famous queue.

Questions for this route

Plateau & Mile End FAQ

How are the Plateau and Mile End different on foot?

The Plateau’s southern avenues—especially Avenue du Mont-Royal and Duluth—are busy commercial streets with parks nearby. Farther north, Mile End shifts to smaller residential blocks, former industrial buildings and Saint-Laurent/Saint-Viateur storefronts. The neighborhood names overlap in casual use, but the walk is not one compact plaza.

What should I look for besides cafés and bagels?

Look up at the iron outdoor staircases and balconies on the Plateau’s narrow triplexes, then notice how Mile End’s early-20th-century garment and factory blocks meet ordinary homes. At Saint-Viateur and Saint-Urbain, St. Michael and St. Anthony’s dome and tower are a street landmark; the parish remains an active place of worship.

Where should this walk end?

For a short Orange-line return, finish toward Rosemont; Outremont or Acadie suits a Blue-line endpoint farther west. Check the actual route before setting out and choose one finish rather than doubling back to the southern start.
