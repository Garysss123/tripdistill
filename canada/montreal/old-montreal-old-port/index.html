Old Montreal & Old Port

Walk from Place-d’Armes down through Place Royale to the Old Port, with one interior visit chosen for your interest: the Fort Ville-Marie excavation or Notre-Dame Basilica’s Gothic Revival interior.

Trace the routeCheck route details
Plan this visit

Choose one interior before walking downhill

Choose Pointe-à-Callière for buried city layers, including Fort Ville-Marie and the collector sewer; choose Notre-Dame Basilica for architecture and art. Seeing both interiors and walking the waterfront makes a long day, so keep one as the main visit and let Place Royale and Rue Saint-Paul supply the outdoor history.

Getting there

Place-d’Armes, Champ-de-Mars and Square-Victoria–OACI are Orange-line approaches to different edges of the old centre. Place-d’Armes is the most direct start for the downhill street sequence; Champ-de-Mars suits the eastern edge. Check STM elevator status if stairs or sloping streets affect your route.

Time to allow

Allow about 5–7 hours for one substantial interior, a slow walk through Place Royale and Rue Saint-Paul, and a waterfront pause. This is an editorial pacing range; entry schedules, queues and event fencing change the day. For a half day, choose the museum and nearby square or the riverfront walk, not both.

How to extend the day

Finish with dinner downtown if you have energy. Keep Plateau/Mile End and Mount Royal for another day: each adds a different uphill or cross-town leg after the historic centre’s stone streets.

Route sequence

From Place-d’Armes to the riverfront

01Arrive

Begin at Place-d’Armes

Face Notre-Dame Basilica from the square. The church’s two towers and neo-Gothic façade frame the older civic core; if you go inside, notice the gold-leafed vaults, Montreal-history stained glass and carved pulpit rather than treating it as a generic church stop.

02Walk

Visit the excavation

At Pointe-à-Callière, the Fort Ville-Marie pavilion presents the remains of the 1642 settlement and the governor’s later residence. The museum also opens a 110-metre section of the 1830s collector sewer: Memory Collector projects light onto its stone walls and uses a sound environment. Choose this interior when archaeology and infrastructure interest you.

03Explore

Walk the old commercial streets

Follow Rue Saint-Paul downhill to Place Royale. Pause at the square and its church-fronted streetscape, then continue through the narrow historic grid; keep the basilica, museum and extra church interiors selective so the route still reaches the water.

04Finish

Finish at the St Lawrence

Use the Old Port promenade as the open-air finish. Its marina, paths and seasonal programming are separate experiences with changing event layouts; choose a short waterfront section, then return from the station or bus stop that matches the day’s endpoint.

If the day changes

Keep the visit worthwhile with a shorter outdoor section.

Plan B

On a wet day, put the chosen interior first and shorten the cobbled walk to the clearest stretch between Place-d’Armes and Place Royale. The Old Port is still open air; save its longer promenade for a dry or less icy window.

Before you go

Check the exact entrance and opening status.

Before departure, check Pointe-à-Callière’s open areas and ticket information, Notre-Dame’s sightseeing schedule and worship notices if it is your chosen interior, STM elevator/service alerts, and the Old Port’s seasonal event map.

What can change the route

Three practical checks for this day.

01

Stone underfoot

Cobbles and sloping sidewalks can be slick in rain, packed snow or freeze-thaw. If footing is poor, shorten the walk to Place Royale and use the smoother waterfront paths only when the connecting route is clear.

02

Two interior schedules

Pointe-à-Callière’s exhibition areas and Notre-Dame’s visitor access are operated separately. Pick one main interior and check its current hours, tickets and worship or event notices.

03

Waterfront events

Summer festivals and temporary installations can redirect the Old Port promenade. Follow current signs and leave the riverfront from the nearest practical transit stop rather than retracing the full route.

Questions for this route

Old Montreal & Old Port FAQ

What does the Fort Ville-Marie visit show?

Pointe-à-Callière presents excavated remains of the 1642 settlement and the later residence of Governor Louis-Hector de Callière. Its Memory Collector experience lets visitors walk 110 metres through the 1832–1838 collector sewer, with projected light on the stone walls and a designed sound environment. Check the museum’s current hours and open areas.

What is distinctive about Notre-Dame Basilica inside?

The Basilica is Canada’s first Gothic Revival church. Visitors can follow 24 self-guided points through the nave and aisles, see stained glass recounting Montreal history, gilded vault decoration, the carved Pulpit of Truth and a 7,000-pipe Casavant organ. The operator estimates about an hour for the daytime visit; worship has its own schedule.

Is the Old Port an indoor attraction?

No. It is a public riverfront promenade with seasonal activities and temporary event layouts. Keep it as the outdoor finish, and check event notices if you need a particular path or waterfront attraction.
