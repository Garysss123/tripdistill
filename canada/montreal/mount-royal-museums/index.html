Mount Royal & Museum Mile

Make Mount Royal a park visit, not a quick lookout add-on: choose Kondiaronk terrace for the skyline or Lac aux Castors for a waterside pause, then add one museum only if the descent fits.

Trace the routeCheck route details
Plan this visit

Choose a lookout or a lakeside walk

Kondiaronk gives the broad city view and the Chalet; Lac aux Castors offers a lake and park pause. The Olmsted trail is 8.3 kilometres, so do not confuse the designed summit route with a short stroll to the terrace. If you want an indoor second stop, the Montreal Museum of Fine Arts at 1380 Sherbrooke West has Quebec/Canadian, international, decorative-arts/design and contemporary collections; select one focus rather than attempting the entire museum.

Getting there

Kondiaronk terrace sits at the Mount Royal welcome centre at 1196 Voie Camillien-Houde; the City lists an access ramp to the lookout, not step-free access across every park trail. Plan the exact entrance and return with STM service, trail notices and your mobility needs in mind.

Time to allow

Allow roughly 2–3 hours for one focused park objective and another 2–3 for one museum. These are planning ranges, not measured walk times; the 8.3-kilometre Olmsted trail, trail detours, museum hours and your chosen entrance all change the day.

How to extend the day

The Museum of Fine Arts is a practical indoor complement at the southern edge of this day; check its current exhibitions and choose one collection focus. Do not add Old Montreal’s waterfront after the climb unless you are deliberately extending the outing.

Route sequence

One park entrance, one outdoor goal, one collection

01Arrive

Choose the mountain day

Check cloud, smoke, heat and footing before deciding. The City park page lists trail and facility notices separately; an open park does not mean every path is clear.

02Walk

Go to one outdoor landmark

At Kondiaronk, the terrace and Chalet frame a broad city view; the City lists a ramp at the lookout. At Lac aux Castors, choose a shorter waterside pause. Do not promise yourself both if you are also considering the full 8.3-kilometre Olmsted route.

03Explore

Stay on the signed route

On 7 October 2026, the City’s resurfacing work rerouted Chemin Olmsted near Lac aux Castors through 10 October. Follow temporary signs and use only a trail segment the live notice lists as open; revisit the trail page after the dated work ends.

04Finish

Descend to one collection

If conditions and energy allow, continue to the Montreal Museum of Fine Arts on Sherbrooke Street West. Its collection spans Quebec and Canadian art, international art, decorative arts/design and contemporary art; choose one area, since the museum’s full collection is much larger than one afternoon.

If the day changes

Keep the visit worthwhile with a shorter outdoor section.

Plan B

Cloud, smoke, heat or ice can make the summit or descent a poor choice. Move the Montreal Museum of Fine Arts to the main block, or keep only a lower maintained park segment when the City reports it open; a closed trail section can coexist with an open park.

Before you go

Check the exact entrance and opening status.

Checked 7 October 2026: Ville de Montréal says Chemin Olmsted near Lac aux Castors is being resurfaced and rerouted from 30 September through 10 October; follow on-site signs during the work. Recheck the City trail notice after that date, Kondiaronk access, STM elevator/service alerts, visibility and the chosen museum’s hours.

What can change the route

Three practical checks for this day.

01

Surface and slope

The mountain adds real elevation and the Olmsted route is 8.3 kilometres. Wet leaves, packed snow or ice can make the return harder than the ascent; choose the trail and endpoint before starting.

02

Access is landmark-specific

The ramp listed at Kondiaronk serves the lookout approach. It does not make the whole summit route or every park trail step-free; confirm the exact path, transit stop and return.

03

Visibility and live closures

Low cloud and smoke can erase the skyline, while resurfacing or seasonal work can reroute one trail. Use the park page and signs on site, and keep a museum-only day as a real alternative.

Questions for this route

Mount Royal & Museum Mile FAQ

Kondiaronk or Lac aux Castors: which should I choose?

Choose Kondiaronk for the Chalet-side skyline terrace; the City lists a ramp to the lookout. Choose Lac aux Castors for a waterside park stop and seasonal facilities. They are different objectives, and the connecting walk is still part of the day.

Is the Olmsted route a short path to the viewpoint?

No. The City lists the Chemin Olmsted route at 8.3 kilometres. Treat it as a substantial park walk with elevation, weather and trail detours, not as the required approach to the Kondiaronk terrace. Check the current map and closure notice before choosing it.

Can I add a museum after the park?

Yes, if you pick one and keep enough time for the descent. The Montreal Museum of Fine Arts is on Sherbrooke Street West and has several distinct collection areas; choose one focus and check the current gallery and visitor information rather than trying to see the whole collection.
