Saguenay Fjord Hiking

Choose a named sector and one signed objective before driving: Baie-Éternité, Baie-Sainte-Marguerite and Baie-de-Tadoussac are separate access decisions around a long fjord. A land day reveals cliffs and forest at walking pace; it is not an easy add-on to a boat day or a through-drive.

Arrival and base

Sépaq’s trailheads sit in distinct sectors, so select the entrance, trail and lodging base together. A car or a confirmed operator transfer is usually needed for rural access; check the exact date’s parking, trail and service notices rather than assuming Saguenay’s urban bus serves a trailhead.

What this day gives you

Baie-Éternité is a strong choice when the goal is a major fjord-side walk; Baie-Sainte-Marguerite offers a land-based beluga observation approach when the relevant trail is open; Tadoussac offers a shorter wharf-side marine walk. The goals differ, and a trail label or distance alone does not describe the climb, exposure or return.

Weather or closure fallback

On rain, fog, high wind, an early closing or a full parking area, shorten the objective or move to a currently open lower walk and interpretation stop. If no safe trail fits, use a town or indoor museum day; do not drive to a second distant sector on the assumption it will have space.

Four-stage route

A sequence that protects the return.

Before departure check Sépaq’s chosen-sector page, the named trail’s distance/difficulty and seasonal status, parking or shuttle arrangement, weather and daylight. On 12 October 2026, Sépaq listed several short walks through 18 October and the 3 km Halte du Béluga through 12 October; these dates can change by year and are not a future opening guarantee.

01Choose

Choose one sector by the day’s goal

For a fjord hike, select a Baie-Éternité trail and read its climbing profile; for land-based beluga interpretation, check the Baie-Sainte-Marguerite trail and seasonal listing; for a shorter shore walk, check Pointe-de-l’Islet in Tadoussac. These are alternatives, not consecutive trail stops.

02Check

Verify the exact entrance and date

Open the official Sépaq listing for the named trail, its difficulty, seasonal dates and parking. A current page may distinguish trails that appear close on a regional map. Save the access point and current road or shuttle information before leaving the base.

03Walk

Set the turnaround before walking

Check forecast, wind, rain and remaining daylight. Choose a route that fits the least experienced walker, carry water and layers, and use only signed trails. Turn back before the exposed section if visibility, footing, fatigue or closure removes a comfortable return margin.

04Return

Return through the same confirmed plan

Account for the full walk out, not only the viewpoint. Confirm parking exit, any shuttle return and dinner or fuel before the park day. Move the marine excursion to another day instead of stacking it after a hard hike.

Failure points

Three reasons to change the plan.

01

Sector names hide different access

The three sectors are separated around the fjord. Reserve lodging and transport for the chosen entrance; do not trust a short straight-line map distance.

02

Season dates belong to individual trails

The 2026 Sépaq page showed different end dates for different walks. Check the selected trail’s own current listing; a park can remain accessible while one route or service closes.

03

Fjord weather changes the return

Fog, rain and exposed wind can reduce visibility and footing. A lower or indoor fallback is better than pushing toward a viewpoint after the safe turnaround.
