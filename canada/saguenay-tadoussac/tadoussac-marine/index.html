Tadoussac & Marine Park

Tadoussac is a place to choose between a specific marine departure and a shore-based day, not a promise of seeing whales. Start with the dock, forecast and return; if a boat is unsuitable, the wharf-side Pointe-de-l’Islet path and marine interpretation give the visit a complete land-based shape.

Arrival and base

Name the departure town and dock before booking: boats in the wider marine park do not all leave from Tadoussac. If your road plan uses Highway 138 across the Saguenay, confirm the STQ crossing from Baie-Sainte-Catherine. The ferry is not required by every approach, and urban transit should not be treated as a dock shuttle unless a current trip planner confirms it.

What this day gives you

A boat covers more of the estuary but adds cold exposure, motion, a fixed check-in and a weather-dependent return. A shore visit is easier to shorten and pairs with Tadoussac’s signed Pointe-de-l’Islet walk, but it cannot promise an animal sighting either. Choose the experience for the day you want, not a species checklist.

Weather or closure fallback

When wind, fog, operator cancellation or motion sensitivity changes the plan, use the public shoreline and a marine-park interpretation stop that is open that day. Sépaq lists the Pointe-de-l’Islet path as a short, wharf-adjacent walk with a seasonal window; confirm current access, daylight and any local closure before relying on it.

Four-stage route

A sequence that protects the return.

Check the selected operator’s dock, check-in, cancellation and return policy; the marine-park visitor guidance; weather; Route 138 and STQ notices if relevant; and the current DFO viewing rules. DFO lists a 400 m distance for endangered or threatened cetaceans, including beluga, in the Saguenay–St Lawrence Marine Park, and 100 m for cetaceans in other Saguenay River areas. Follow the applicable posted guidance.

01Select

Choose the experience before the operator

Decide whether you want a boat or a flexible shoreline visit. Compare the exact departure town, dock, check-in and cancellation terms; “Saguenay–St Lawrence” is a large marine park, not one departure pier.

02Confirm

Make the approach leg explicit

If driving via Highway 138, put the Baie-Sainte-Catherine–Tadoussac ferry in the itinerary and use STQ’s current schedule and service notice. For another approach, map that route separately. Leave a road and check-in buffer rather than assuming the crossing or boarding will be immediate.

03Observe

Let wildlife set the distance

Bring warm layers for cold water and follow the guide, park rules and DFO distances. In the marine park the 400 m rule applies to endangered or threatened cetaceans such as beluga; DFO gives 100 m for cetaceans in other Saguenay River areas. Never pursue or touch wildlife.

04Return

Use a shore plan if the boat changes

Walk the signed Pointe-de-l’Islet route only if its current seasonal access and conditions allow it. Check any interpretation centre’s hours. Finish in Tadoussac and keep the next long road leg for a rested, daylight start.

Failure points

Three reasons to change the plan.

01

Sighting is uncertain

A responsible operator cannot schedule a wild animal. Fog, behavior and sea state may change the route or remove a departure; a shore walk is a valid full alternative.

02

Distance rules differ by place

The 400 m endangered-cetacean rule in the marine park and 100 m rule in other Saguenay River areas do not describe every viewing context. Follow the current official rule for the exact water and activity.

03

Crossing and dock are separate legs

The STQ ferry serves one Route 138 crossing. It does not connect every boat dock or every approach; verify the route you actually intend to drive.
