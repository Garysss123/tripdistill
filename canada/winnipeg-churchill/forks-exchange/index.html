The Forks & Exchange District

Follow one coherent central Winnipeg day: read the Red and Assiniboine river meeting place at The Forks, choose either the nearby Peace Meeting site or a museum stop, then continue to the Exchange District’s historic streets. The Forks describes the confluence as a gathering place for more than 6,000 years; approach Indigenous histories as living stories, not a quick attraction label.

Failure points

02

Planning answers

The Forks & Exchange District FAQ

Is The Forks only a market stop?

No. The Forks places the site at the Red and Assiniboine confluence and describes a long history of Indigenous gathering. Use the Peace Meeting site or Oodena interpretation as context, and treat Indigenous presence as living rather than only historic.

Should I take a guided Exchange District walk?

Choose the BIZ tour when its current calendar fits and you want a curated architectural route. Otherwise, keep to a self-directed public-street visit and verify which businesses or interiors are actually open.

Can I walk between The Forks and the Exchange District?

The areas are both central, but weather, mobility, path conditions and your chosen route matter. Check TransitTools for a current bus alternative; this guide does not assume a specific crossing, distance or walking time.

What is the best bad-weather fallback?

Keep one indoor anchor at or near The Forks, such as the Human Rights Museum if its hours and tickets suit. Shorten the Exchange walk and check transit rather than using river ice or an exposed path as a shortcut.
