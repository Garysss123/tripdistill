Combine carefully

Make the drive the day’s main event. Points East can use a selected section around St. Peter’s Bay, Souris or East Point; North Cape can use a west-coast community and one north-end stop. Confirm exact attractions and return transport before picking a route.

Choose the day’s anchor

Choose the coast by the stop you most want

The route names describe broad regions, not a promise to visit every attraction. Decide whether east-coast beaches and harbours or western Acadian and oyster-coast communities fit the day.

East

Points East Coastal Drive

Select a manageable section such as St. Peter’s Bay–Greenwich or continue toward Souris and East Point only if the extra road time and opening hours fit. Tourism PEI describes the full drive as 475 km.

West

North Cape Coastal Drive

Choose a community on the western route and one north-end viewpoint or heritage stop. Check season-specific openings; the regional visitor centre and attractions do not share one calendar.

Two-coast stay

Split east and west across two days

Move the base or add a night before exploring the other end. This protects daylight, weather flexibility and an honest return; do not pair two long coast drives with a timed mainland crossing.

01Choose

Choose east or west the night before

Use one corridor, not an island circuit. Points East spans 475 km according to Tourism PEI; North Cape is the distinct western coastal region. Pick the nearest base and your single priority stop.

02Confirm

Confirm the stop is operating

Check the exact lighthouse, museum, food business or visitor centre for its own hours and access. Seasonal listings do not share a common opening date; save a town or public-road alternative if the interior is closed.

03Explore

Explore one community and one shore

Allow time to walk the chosen harbour or public viewpoint instead of collecting distant pins. Slow road speeds, weather and unscheduled stops expand the day beyond the drive-time estimate.

04Return

Return without a crossing deadline

Check the road and visibility again before heading back. If your itinerary also includes the Wood Islands ferry or a bridge departure, shorten the coast plan and protect a transfer buffer.

Failure points

Three reasons to change the plan.

01

The map hides stop time

A regional drive includes small roads, viewpoint walks, meals and seasonal hours. Choose one coast and cap the stop list before leaving.

02

Coastal weather can erase visibility

Fog and wind can make an exposed lookout or lighthouse approach unrewarding. Switch to a verified town stop or return safely; do not walk near an unseen cliff edge.

03

Attractions close on different dates

Tourism PEI lists each operator separately. Reconfirm current hours, last admission, parking and public access rather than assuming the whole regional route is open.

Planning answers

PEI East & West Coastal Drives FAQ

Can I drive all of Points East in one day?

Tourism PEI describes the Points East Coastal Drive as 475 km. From Charlottetown, choose a segment and a few stops; a full regional itinerary is not a quick lighthouse loop. Stay overnight if you want to explore the full corridor.

Which coast should I choose?

Choose Points East for eastern beaches, harbours and communities such as St. Peter’s Bay or Souris; choose North Cape for western communities and the island’s northwest coast. Let a specific open stop and your base determine the choice.

Are lighthouse sites open every day?

No single schedule covers the island’s lighthouses. Some offer seasonal interiors or museums; access to an exterior viewpoint is also site-specific. Check the exact operator or Tourism PEI listing and weather before driving to a named site.

Can I combine a coast day with the ferry or bridge?

Avoid making a full scenic day depend on a fixed departure. The Wood Islands–Caribou ferry has its own seasonal schedule, while the Confederation Bridge has separate advisories. Check the live operator notices and leave a generous transfer margin.
