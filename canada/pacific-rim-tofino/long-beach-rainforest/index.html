Four-stage route

A sequence that protects the return.

Check Parks Canada advisories, parking or pass requirements, surf and tide conditions, wildlife notices and current trail status.

01Arrive

Check the coast

Read the marine forecast, tide and park advisories before deciding whether the beach or forest comes first.

02Move

Long Beach window

Use the broad shoreline during the safer and more comfortable weather period rather than assigning a fixed clock time days in advance.

03Read

Rainforest contrast

Move to one signed forest trail with footwear suited to wet boardwalks and roots.

04Return

Exit before dark road fatigue

Return to Tofino or Ucluelet with enough light and energy for the highway and wildlife-aware drive.

Fallback logic

The day still needs a useful shape when conditions change.

Plan B

If surf or wind makes the exposed beach unpleasant, shorten the sand time and use a sheltered rainforest trail or town stop.

Recheck before leaving: Check Parks Canada advisories, parking or pass requirements, surf and tide conditions, wildlife notices and current trail status.
Failure points

Three reasons to change the plan.

01

Cold Pacific water

Surf culture does not make the ocean benign; use local safety guidance and do not treat a scenic beach as a swimming beach.

02

Wildlife food rules

Store food and waste correctly and follow current bear, wolf and cougar guidance.

03

Parking pressure

Popular lots can fill in peak periods; have a second legal stop instead of improvising roadside parking.
