Combine carefully

Combine with the Ucluelet Lighthouse Loop during a four-night stay, but give it a separate day if you want a full beach walk. Do not append the long Highway 4 crossing back toward Nanaimo or Victoria after a long shoreline day.

01Arrive

Check pass, tide and trail notices

Before leaving Tofino or Ucluelet, open Parks Canada’s dated Long Beach Unit trail table, read the current surf and tide guidance, and choose an open legal trailhead. Keep Combers and Schooner Cove out of a default plan until their live notices permit them.

02Move

Walk one forest loop

Use Rainforest Trail A for its 1 km loop if you are comfortable with many stairs and uneven boardwalk, or choose the 800 m Shorepine Bog loop for a no-stair option with uneven boardwalk. Stay on the signed path and do not assume an RV can use the small Rainforest Trail parking lots.

03Read

Use one Long Beach access

Walk the beach at the pace and length conditions allow. Watch the water and incoming tide, stay away from logs and creek crossings when notices warn of hazards, and do not turn a scenic shoreline into a swim recommendation.

04Return

Return along Highway 4

Choose the legal lot for your next stop, then leave enough daylight and driver attention for the return to your lodging. If a lot or trail is full or closed, move on rather than parking on the shoulder.

Failure points

Three reasons to change the plan.

01

Water and tide

The Park warns that creeks can become impossible to cross at high tide or after heavy rain. Read current water-hazard guidance and turn back early; tide pools and surf do not justify crossing moving water.

02

Dated closures

The 12 October 2026 trail table listed Schooner Cove closed, Combers in caution and wolf warnings. Recheck the live notice before each walk and obey temporary dog or access restrictions.

03

Footing and parking

Rainforest A has many steps; Shorepine Bog has an uneven boardwalk; both require slower footing when wet. Use signed lots only and do not assume every trailhead accommodates an RV.

Planning answers

Long Beach & Rainforest Trails FAQ

How long is the Rainforest Trail A loop?

Parks Canada lists Rainforest Trail A as a 1 km moderate loop with boardwalk and gravel, many steps and handrails. It is a short distance with stair effort; check current trail status before going.

Is Long Beach safe for swimming or creek crossings?

Do not infer swimming safety from a calm-looking beach. Parks Canada warns about water hazards, changing tides, strong surf and creeks that may be impossible to cross at high tide or after heavy rain. Follow current notices and turn around early.

Which trails are open right now?

The Parks Canada table checked 12 October 2026 listed Rainforest A and B as open, Schooner Cove as closed until further notice, Combers as caution with a damaged boardwalk, and several other trails with restrictions. That is a dated snapshot, not a future promise; recheck before leaving.
