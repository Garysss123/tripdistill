Downtown & Waterfront

Start with Union Station’s 1927 Beaux-Arts Great Hall, then follow the southbound rail-to-waterfront corridor to Harbourfront. The Toronto Music Garden gives the walk a destination; a timed tower visit is an optional extra, not another compulsory stop.

Plan this route

Union Station to the waterfront, with one chosen anchor

This route is for the city’s railway gateway, public waterfront and one view or cultural stop. Union Station is a working terminal; adding a timed tower visit changes the pace, and the west-side market and Islands each need their own day.

Getting there

UP Express, GO and VIA arrive at Union Station; TTC Line 1 serves the same hub. The 509 Harbourfront streetcar is the direct transit link along Queens Quay, while the shoreline can be walked in sections. Check live service and the station exit you need.

Time to allow

Allow about four to six hours for Union Station, one shoreline segment and the Music Garden, plus a meal. Add a separate block for a booked tower or event; these are editorial planning estimates, not operator timings.

Pair it with

A nearby evening event can fit after this route. Do not add the Islands or a full museum visit: each requires its own departure or admission block.

Route sequence

Read the old rail gateway, then follow the shoreline

01Orient

Union Station’s Great Hall

Begin on Front Street. The City dates this Beaux-Arts terminal’s opening to 1927 and notes the city names carved on its interior walls; look up, then notice that this is still a live passenger station with platforms and transfer corridors behind the public hall.

02Follow

From rail gateway to lake edge

Leave south toward Queens Quay rather than looping north into the Financial District. The City’s rail history starts with a passenger train from a Front Street shed in 1853 and traces the station’s later sites. This walk links the rail corridor to today’s shoreline.

03Choose

Music Garden or one timed view

At the Toronto Music Garden, the beds and wrought-iron details interpret Bach’s Suite No. 1, a design developed with cellist Yo-Yo Ma. Pause for the garden if you want a public landscape walk; substitute one reserved tower or event only if a skyline view matters more.

04Return

Return on the 509 or finish on foot

Use the 509 Harbourfront streetcar toward Union when your legs or weather call for it. If you walked only to the Garden, return the same way; do not assume a waterfront detour is faster during construction or event closures.

If the day changes

Keep the day indoors when the lake edge is exposed

Plan B

In strong wind or rain, shorten the exposed Queens Quay section and spend longer inside Union’s Great Hall or at one reserved indoor attraction. The Music Garden is the part to move, not a reason to cross town in bad weather.

Before you go

Check the rail hall and streetcar route before you start

Check Union Station visitor access, TTC 509 service, current waterfront construction and any timed-entry requirement for the one attraction you add.

What can change the route

Three checks specific to this Toronto day.

01

Station is an operating terminal

Passenger flows, construction and event crowds can change which exit is easiest. Keep clear of platform gates and follow station signs rather than crossing restricted areas for a photograph.

02

Shoreline exposure

Lake wind and rain hit the open Queens Quay stretch; carry a warmer layer and shorten the walk if conditions shift.

03

One paid anchor is enough

A tower visit, game or show has its own timed entry and queue. Reserve its block before adding another attraction.

Questions for this route

Downtown & Waterfront FAQ

What is worth seeing inside Union Station?

The public Great Hall is the Beaux-Arts centerpiece: its scale, barrel-vaulted windows, carved city names and connection to the active concourses make it more than a transfer hall. The City dates the station’s opening to 1927; remain in public areas and follow passenger signs.

Is the Music Garden just a flower garden?

Its layout interprets Bach’s Suite No. 1 through planted spaces and wrought-iron details, with the design developed with cellist Yo-Yo Ma. It works as a distinct shoreline stop when you want a quieter, free-form outdoor pause instead of another ticket queue.

Can I walk from Union Station to the lake?

Yes. Use the southbound street grid toward Queens Quay; if you would rather save the walk, TTC’s 509 Harbourfront links the Union hub with the waterfront. Check service alerts for construction changes.
