Toronto Museums, the Annex & Kensington

ROM and AGO are different museum days, not adjacent checklist stops: ROM’s broad art, culture and nature collections pair naturally with the Bloor-side Annex; AGO’s Canadian-art focus links more directly to Grange Park and Kensington’s historic market streets.

Plan this route

One collection, one street-level neighborhood

Choose ROM for a broad mix of natural history, world cultures and art, including dinosaur galleries; choose AGO for art, with Group of Seven works on Level 1 and in the Thomson Collection of Canadian Art on Level 2. Pair the choice with the nearby neighborhood instead of collecting both museums.

Getting there

ROM is beside Museum Station on TTC Line 1; AGO is near St Patrick Station on Line 1. Kensington is a short street-grid walk from the AGO side, while the Annex follows Bloor west of the ROM. Check TTC alerts and accessible entrances for the exact museum and day.

Time to allow

Set aside about five to seven hours for one museum, one neighborhood walk and a meal. Two to three hours inside is a selective-gallery estimate; a special exhibition or deep collection visit takes longer.

Pair it with

Use ROM plus the Annex when the Bloor museum corridor is the priority. Use AGO plus Grange Park, Chinatown or Kensington when Canadian art and the market district are the draw. Trying to link both large museums with both neighborhoods turns the day into cross-town transit.

Route sequence

Choose a museum, then follow its closest city layer

01Choose

Choose one collection

ROM’s building and galleries combine art, culture and nature; the Michael Lee-Chin Crystal opened in 2007 as part of the museum expansion. At AGO, the Group of Seven has been central since the museum’s 1920 inaugural exhibition, with signature work on Levels 1 and 2. Pick the collection that matches your interest before the day begins.

02Pair

Follow the nearby neighborhood

From ROM, walk west along Bloor if the Annex’s residential streets and university edge are your interest. From AGO, use Grange Park and the Spadina-side street grid to reach Chinatown or Kensington. These are two different pairings; the route does not require both.

03Read

Read Kensington as a living market

Kensington is a National Historic Site whose late-Victorian houses and storefronts carry traces of successive immigrant communities. Browse the public streets around Kensington Avenue, Augusta and Baldwin at shop pace; this is also a residential neighborhood, so keep entrances clear and ask before photographing people.

04Exit

Leave from the side you chose

For a ROM-and-Annex day, return to a Bloor Line 1 station. For AGO and Kensington, use the current TTC map for the nearest station or streetcar after your final stop; do not retrace to the museum automatically.

If the day changes

Protect the collection block and shorten the outdoor leg

Plan B

In rain, keep the museum you chose and shorten the outside section. If Kensington’s shopfront browsing is the purpose, confirm the specific business hours before leaving the AGO; do not replace a closed shop with an unplanned second museum.

Before you go

Check the museum calendar and the current TTC route

Check ROM and AGO hours, admission and current exhibition access separately; confirm the TTC station or streetcar status and any event affecting the market streets.

What can change the route

Three checks specific to this Toronto day.

01

Museum schedules differ

ROM and AGO set separate hours, admission and exhibition access. Check the specific museum’s calendar instead of assuming one ticket or closure pattern covers both.

02

Market streets are narrow and lived in

Shop displays, residents and visitors share the sidewalks. Pause out of doorways and do not treat a home or storefront as a photo prop.

03

Surface transit changes by block

Streetcar stops and diversions can shift during construction. Check the TTC route and station accessibility for the route you selected, especially if a transfer is time-sensitive.

Questions for this route

Toronto Museums, the Annex & Kensington FAQ

Should I choose ROM or AGO?

ROM suits a day built around natural history, world cultures and art; its Michael Lee-Chin Crystal adds a striking 2007 architectural layer. AGO suits a day focused on art, with the Group of Seven and Thomson Collection of Canadian Art as useful starting points. Read both current calendars before booking.

Which neighborhood pairs with each museum?

The Annex is the Bloor-side extension after ROM. Kensington and nearby Chinatown work more naturally after AGO and Grange Park. Choose one pairing; the other can be a separate half-day.

Is Kensington Market an indoor market?

No. It is a historic neighborhood of public streets, homes and independent storefronts. Its open-air displays and businesses operate on their own schedules, so confirm a specific shop and leave room for the street walk itself.
