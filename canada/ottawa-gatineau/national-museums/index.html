Ottawa–Gatineau National Museums

Choose a collection for its subject, then keep the day on that museum’s riverbank. The History Museum is directly opposite Parliament in Gatineau; the War Museum and National Gallery give distinct Ottawa-side alternatives.

Choose the operating day

Choose the collection before choosing a crossing

The Canadian Museum of History suits a day focused on Canadian history and its large galleries; the Canadian War Museum is the military-history choice; the National Gallery is the art choice. These are separate institutions, not a single campus. One major collection can fill the day, while a second only helps when its subject is a deliberate contrast.

Base and arrival

Use downtown Ottawa or central Hull/Gatineau as the base that fits your chosen museum. The History Museum lists access by foot, bicycle and public transit from Ottawa, plus a seasonal water taxi from the Ottawa Locks; use its current route and closure notice with the separate OC Transpo or STO trip planner. For Ottawa-side museums, verify the exact entrance and current service before selecting the stop.

Time to allow

Allow a half day or more for one major museum, plus the selected trip to and from its entrance. A timed ticket, special exhibition, family visit or deep collection plan can take longer; the museum’s own calendar and ticket page set the actual entry window.

What fits after

Pair one museum with a nearby river view, meal or short neighborhood walk on the same bank. Keep Parliament and the canal for another day. Do not plan the History Museum, War Museum and National Gallery as a same-day checklist across two transit systems.

01Select

Name the collection question

Choose the History Museum for a Canadian-history day, the War Museum for military history or the National Gallery for art. Read the current exhibition and visit pages before booking so the route follows the galleries you actually want to see, not the museum count.

02Connect

Plan the side of the river first

For the History Museum, use its official getting-here page: it identifies the Gatineau address and lists walking, cycling, public transit and a seasonal water taxi from the Ottawa Locks. It warns that construction and special events can close nearby roads and bridges. For an Ottawa-side museum, use that institution’s own visitor route.

03Stay

Give the chosen collection the main block

At the History Museum, enter on the timed ticket and choose a small number of galleries or the current Canadian History Hall/First Peoples Hall visit that matches your interests; the Children’s Museum is a separate family-facing choice. At the War Museum or National Gallery, use the current floor and gallery guide to select a focus. Do not treat a broad institution as a one-hour stop.

04Return

Eat and return on the same side

Finish with a meal or brief riverfront walk near the museum you chose. Check the last practical return leg and any temporary closure before crossing again. If the return is disrupted, follow the operator’s live trip planner rather than assuming the morning route still works.

Access questions

Ottawa–Gatineau National Museums FAQ

Which museum should I choose?

Choose the Canadian Museum of History for Canadian history and its large gallery sequence, the War Museum for military history, or the National Gallery for art. Use current exhibition and visitor pages to decide which specific galleries justify your day.

Can I walk from Ottawa to the Canadian Museum of History?

The Museum lists walking and cycling among its access options, along with public transit and a seasonal water taxi. Nearby roads and bridges can close for work or events, so check its current getting-here notice and an alternate transit return before setting out.

Are timed tickets required?

The History Museum’s page checked on 12 October 2026 says every visitor needs a timed ticket. That is a dated snapshot; check its live ticket page and each other museum’s own rules for your visit date.
