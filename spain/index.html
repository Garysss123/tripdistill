Spain

Plan a country of different rail corridors, languages, seasons and island transfers. This guide links Madrid, Catalonia, Andalusia, the Basque coast, Valencia and three island systems with route choices that start at the station, port or trailhead—not at a generic map pin.

01Central Spain · 3 focused routes

Madrid & Castile approaches

Madrid is a rail hub and a full city stay, not merely a transfer between AVE trains. Use the capital for its own walkable museum, park and palace districts; add a Castilian day trip only when the named station, return train and local onward leg all fit.

02Northeast Spain · 3 focused routes

Catalonia & Barcelona

Barcelona rewards a neighbourhood-led stay; Girona and the coast are separate rail and last-mile decisions. AVE access from France or Madrid lands on a city network, while beach, hillside and monument visits still have their own doors and local services.

03Southern Spain · 3 focused routes

Andalusia: Seville, Córdoba & Granada

Andalusia is not one city itinerary: Seville, Córdoba and Granada each have a rail station, a different local walk and an independent landmark-entry system. Link them by AVE or conventional rail only after checking the named operator, terminal and transfer; summer heat can change how much of each day belongs outdoors.

04Northern Spain · 3 focused routes

Basque Country: Bilbao, Donostia & the Coast

Bilbao, Donostia-San Sebastián and the smaller coast are linked by a mix of national rail, Euskotren, bus and local streets. The Bay of Biscay brings changing weather and steep coastal paths; choose one city base and treat each rural stop as a separate timetable decision.

05Eastern Spain · 3 focused routes

Valencia & the Albufera

Valencia pairs a compact historic centre with a long former riverbed park, eastern cultural district and a separate lagoon-and-rice coast. Metro, tram, bus and walking cover different edges of the city; Albufera is an excursion whose access, boat operation and return deserve a dedicated check.

06Western Mediterranean islands · 3 focused routes

Balearic Islands: Mallorca, Menorca & Ibiza

Mallorca, Menorca and Ibiza are separate island trips with different settlement patterns and ground transport; Formentera is an additional ferry leg from Ibiza. Choose one island for a short stay, then decide whether local bus, rail, rental car or bicycle actually reaches the place you plan to see.

07Atlantic islands · 3 focused routes

Canary Islands: Tenerife, Gran Canaria & the Eastern Islands

The Canary Islands are several distinct volcanic islands, not one rail-connected destination. Choose a home island by terrain and town mix, then coordinate an inter-island flight or ferry with the port or airport transfer and local road or bus network; weather and elevation can change sharply within one day.

08Northwest Spain · 3 focused routes

Galicia & Asturias: Atlantic Cities and Green North

Northwestern Spain trades the interior’s heat for Atlantic weather, green valleys and a more dispersed coast. Santiago de Compostela has a strong rail-and-walk centre, while the Rías Baixas and Asturias’ Picos gateways need a dated bus, road or seasonal access plan beyond the mainline station.

Transport & trip shape

Buy the trunk after the endpoints make sense.

Spain’s long-distance rail is useful when its exact station and journey fit the itinerary. It does not replace city transit, a regional train, a dated island boat or a final bus to a beach or mountain town.

HIGH-SPEED RAIL

AVE and long-distance trains

Use Renfe’s current map and ticket conditions for the named route. Madrid has multiple long-distance terminals; Barcelona Sants, Valencia Joaquín Sorolla, Córdoba and Seville are specific stations. Check the ticket, luggage plan and onward Metro or walk before reserving another leg.

REGIONAL NETWORKS

One station is not the whole region

Cercanías, Media Distancia, Euskotren, Metrovalencia, island buses and intercity coaches are separate networks. Confirm operator, start point, service notices and last practical return for the day.

SUMMER & WEATHER

Move exposed walking into cooler hours

In hot inland and southern cities, schedule plazas, waterfront and hills early or late; choose a confirmed indoor visit during peak heat. Check AEMET warnings for the city or elevation, not only the country forecast.

ISLAND GATEWAYS

Flights and ferries add a transfer day

Balearic and Canary routes use distinct airports and ports. Add the local transfer, check-in buffer, luggage or vehicle rules and island transport. Save both the outward and return crossing.

Trip-length choices

Stay long enough to notice the place you chose.

4–5 days

One strong base

Give Madrid, Barcelona, Seville, Granada, Bilbao, Valencia or Palma a real city stay. Add at most one nearby rail day trip; keep arrival and departure days light.

7–10 days

One corridor, two or three bases

Join a sensible mainland line—such as Madrid with Andalusia—or stay in Catalonia and add Girona or one coast town. Count intercity transfer days honestly.

12–16 days

One mainland region plus an island

Leave enough nights for a separate Balearic or Canary base. Choose one island and book the gateway after confirming local transport and seasonal service.

Car-free or self-drive?

Pick transport after naming the last stop.

City pairs and principal rail hubs usually work better without parking friction. For coves, inland villages and remote trailheads, a car can solve the final gap but adds parking, road and weather constraints.

Car-free works best when

Your base is a major city, the day trip ends at a rail-served town and the station-to-centre walk or bus is clear.

A car earns its place when

The route links dispersed rural stops or a trailhead with no reliable public return. Reserve legal parking and check seasonal road controls.

Choose a slower day when

A train, timed monument and final bus all need to align. Drop one stop or sleep near the coast rather than make an uncertain evening return the hinge.

Planning answers

Frequently asked questions

How many Spanish cities fit into one week?

For seven days, choose one city or a two-city rail corridor with two or three stable nights per base. Madrid–Córdoba–Seville or Barcelona with one nearby rail day trip can work; adding an island or remote coast makes transfer days dominate.

Should I travel by AVE or regional rail?

Use AVE and other high-speed trains for a named long-distance trunk where the station and ticket fit your route. Regional rail, Cercanías, metro, intercity bus and ferry solve different local legs; check the operator and station, then plan the last mile separately.

Is a rental car needed?

Most city centres and major rail corridors work better without a car. A car helps with dispersed villages, rural coast or some island and mountain routes, but restricted centres, parking and seasonal access can make it slower. Choose after naming the exact places.

When is Spain too hot for a walking day?

Heat varies by region and date, but exposed southern and inland cities can become uncomfortable in summer. Put outdoor routes in cooler hours, take an indoor midday break, carry water and follow AEMET warnings for the precise location.

Can I add an island to a mainland itinerary?

Yes, if you budget for an airport or port transfer and a separate flight or ferry. Add the local transfer, check-in buffer and island transport; on a short trip, choose one island rather than several crossings.
