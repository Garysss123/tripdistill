Lake city / eight planning instruments

Hangzhou use West Lake as a compass

West Lake is not a single attraction at the center of a checklist. It is a cultural landscape with distinct shores, wooded hills and deliberate views. Read those edges first, then give the canal, wetland, tea villages and Liangzhu the separate time their scale requires.

Four edges, one protected landscape

Orient by shore before booking by district

The lake is roughly fifteen kilometers around, while major chapters continue well beyond it. A hotel that looks “near West Lake” may still sit on the wrong edge for your first two mornings.

ARRIVAL AND RETURN

Name the terminal and station before the lake

Hangzhou has an international airport and several railway stations. Keep arrival and departure days light; choose the last hotel and final transfer only after matching the actual airport terminal or the station printed on your ticket.

HGH / XIAOSHAN AIRPORT

Metro, taxi or coach

The airport's ground transport center connects multiple modes. Check the live route for your terminal and arrival or departure hour, including the final hotel leg; do not assume every airport train reaches the lakeshore directly.

HANGZHOU EAST

Major high-speed rail hub

Useful for many Shanghai and national services, with a large station complex. Match the booked train, arrival level and hotel route; allow margin for security and the station-to-street transfer.

HANGZHOU / WEST

Different corridors, different city edges

Hangzhou station can favor the historic core; Hangzhou West serves different corridors. Never shorten a station name to “Hangzhou” in a transfer plan, and keep Xixi or Liangzhu off the itinerary just before a flight or train.

Base by first departure

A lake-facing address is not automatically a useful base

Compare the exact metro entrance, road crossings and the morning direction of your first three days.

Hubin and east shore

Strong for first arrivals, restaurants, metro access and evening lake views. Expect the busiest pedestrian edge and longer west-hill transfers.

Wulin and north city

A practical urban base for transport and the Grand Canal direction. Check whether “West Lake” marketing hides a long walk to the water.

Huanglong and northwest

Useful when Lingyin, Longjing or Xixi dominate the plan. Hotel quality matters less than the precise bus, taxi and evening-return chain.

Three full days; fourth by choice

Do not circle the lake every day

Three full sightseeing days usually need four nights when arrival and departure are separate; add a fifth night for a fourth full day. Keep each day geographically coherent and leave visibility, rain and crowd-sensitive choices flexible.

Day 1 / North shore

Begin at Hubin or Broken Bridge, cross Bai Causeway, slow down at Gushan and finish along Beishan. Choose this single north-shore sequence instead of tracing the full lake edge.

Day 2 / West hills

Choose Lingyin and Feilai Peak as the cultural anchor, or choose a Longjing/Nine Creeks tea and trail day. Do not promise yourself both full routes on a wet or low-energy day.

Day 3 / South shore

Keep the south side to one planned cluster: a museum or garden walk, then Leifeng Pagoda or a lake-level sunset view if weather, opening and energy still allow it.

Day 4 / Outer contrast

If staying longer, choose the Grand Canal for urban heritage, Xixi for wetland ecology or Liangzhu for archaeology. Each needs most of a day and its own current entrance and return plan.

Lake movement, weather and payments

Prepare the friction that postcard maps hide

The metro reaches the edges

Metro stations help with the urban shore, airport and rail hubs, but many western-hill and lakeside trips still require a bus, taxi or substantial walk. Save the Chinese destination name and check the current station exit for the exact venue.

Rain changes trail value

The lower Yangtze has a variable early-summer Meiyu pattern, and rain can also arrive outside that window. If showers persist, skip wet hill steps and boardwalks; choose one indoor museum after checking its current campus, opening day and reservation, and treat boats as optional.

Use more than one payment route

Prepare a supported mobile-payment or card option plus RMB cash. Test transport and attraction payments early, and keep passport requirements visible for bookings.

For limited walking: choose one short shore cluster, such as a single north- or south-lake segment, and use a verified current taxi or transit leg between clusters. Check the exact steps, slopes, restrooms, lift status and boat boarding with the relevant venue or operator; neither the whole lake circuit nor every historic entrance should be assumed step-free.

Quick answers

Hangzhou planning FAQ

How many days should I allow?

Three full days cover the north shore, one west-hill choice and the south shore; allow about four nights when you keep arrival and departure separate. A fourth full day for the canal, Xixi or Liangzhu generally means a fifth night.

Should I make Hangzhou a day trip from Shanghai?

A day trip can show one coherent West Lake sequence, but rail station approaches and the last-mile return use part of the day. Stay overnight if you want early causeways, evening light, the western hills or any outer district.

Is the entire West Lake scenic area free?

The open lakeside landscape and many paths do not share one admission, but temples, pagodas, museums, boats and specific attractions may have separate tickets, reservations or closure days. Verify each operator.

Can I combine Lingyin, Longjing and Xixi in one day?

They look close on a regional map but involve different entrances, traffic and walking scales. Choose one major anchor and one compatible secondary stop rather than compressing all three.
