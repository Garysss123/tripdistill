Two anchors, one wildcard

Curate the visitor before the visitor is curated

An art district rewards selection. Choose one institution-scale exhibition, one smaller space and one unscheduled discovery, then stop when attention falls.

The building is evidence

Read the factory fabric between shows

Official descriptions emphasize 798's industrial complex and its conversion into a dense cultural zone. Roof forms, service lanes, brick, signage and reused volumes explain why the district feels different from a conventional museum campus.

Keep four layers separate

  • Industrial shell: look for original scale and circulation without inventing a complete factory history.
  • Adaptive reuse: notice how galleries negotiate large halls, daylight and old infrastructure.
  • Public realm: distinguish shared streets from ticketed interiors and working service areas.
  • Commercial layer: cafés and retail can support the day without becoming proof of artistic value.

Quick answers

798 FAQ

Do I need one ticket for the whole district?

No. The shared zone and individual venues have different admission models. Check each selected exhibition.

What day is best?

Choose a day when the anchor venues are open. There is no useful universal answer because programs and closed days differ.

Can I combine 798 with Summer Palace?

They sit in different directions and both deserve attention. Combining them creates a cross-city transfer day with little slack.

Is the area only for contemporary-art specialists?

No. Industrial architecture, design, public space and a carefully chosen exhibition can make a coherent half day for a curious first-time visitor.
