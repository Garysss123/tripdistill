The hub treats St. John’s/Avalon, the Bonavista Peninsula and Gros Morne as separate overnight bases. Its approximately 10–12-night plan assigns three nights to St. John’s, two to Bonavista, four to Gros Morne, then one to three transfer/weather nights. A one-week visit chooses the east or west side instead of crossing the island. Labrador is identified as a separate mainland road/ferry/flight plan.

St. John’s now gives a two-full-day, three-night plan, fits downtown walking and one cultural anchor before a weather-selected headland, and treats Cape Spear as a separate transport leg. It distinguishes Metrobus city service from unverified headland access; no bus route or fare is invented. Bonavista gives Trinity and the Bonavista harbor separate blocks across at least two peninsula nights, names lodging choices, daylight transfer and an optional coast branch, and does not assume a regional bus. Gros Morne separates Tablelands/Trout River from Rocky Harbour/Norris Point/Western Brook Pond, describes how a base affects cross-park drives, sets a three-night floor and a four- or five-night weather margin, and keeps the boat operator as optional.

All four routes specify weather, access, lodging/night and geographic sequencing choices with viable fallbacks. The comparison required by the README used the same-level Japan source pages [Hokkaido](/japan/hokkaido/) and [Asakusa & Ueno](/japan/tokyo/asakusa-ueno/): regional gateways, distinct transport legs, base nights, a geographically ordered sequence and alternatives were compared at the content-source level. The route plans do not promise unverified fares, exact future departures, tour seats, wildlife sightings or future opening hours.

## Official operating snapshot

These dated facts were checked against the linked official pages on 12 October 2026. They are snapshots, not future guarantees; the guides point readers to current notices before booking or setting out.

- [Parks Canada Signal Hill](https://parks.canada.ca/lhn-nhs/nl/signalhill) listed its 2026 visitor season as 1 June–9 October and the grounds as open year-round. Its [visitor page](https://parks.canada.ca/lhn-nhs/nl/signalhill/visit) says to allow 1–3 hours and lists access by car or on foot; it does not establish a Metrobus connection.
- [Parks Canada Cape Spear](https://parks.canada.ca/lhn-nhs/nl/spear) listed its visitor season through 9 October 2026 and grounds open year-round. Separate building and program hours still need checking.
- [Parks Canada Ryan Premises](https://parks.canada.ca/lhn-nhs/nl/ryan) listed its 2026 visitor season through 9 October. A harbor walk does not mean its buildings are open outside that season.
- [Parks Canada Gros Morne](https://parks.canada.ca/pn-np/nl/grosmorne) says the park is open year-round and identifies October–May as low season. It describes Gros Morne Mountain as a challenging 17 km one-day loop, closed 1 May–27 June and reopening 28 June. The [Tablelands trail page](https://parks.canada.ca/pn-np/nl/grosmorne/activ/experiences/tablelands) lists an easy 4 km return trail and the Route 431 approach to Trout River.
- The provincial [road status page](https://www.gov.nl.ca/ti/roads/home/) directs travelers to 511 for current road, construction, winter and wildlife notices. The provincial [ferry services page](https://www.gov.nl.ca/ti/ferryservices/) directs travelers to live 511 ferry status and says weather can change service without notice.
- The [Metrobus operator site](https://www.metrobus.com/) was checked, but its current route details were not available in the extracted page content, so the guide names no specific route or headland connection. [The Rooms](https://therooms.ca/) has a current visitor calendar/hours page; the guide tells travelers to check the selected date.
