# Greece editorial and visual review — 20 October 2026

## Scope and comparison method

This release adds 33 English route bodies—one country overview, eight region hubs and 24 focused local guides—and their four translated editions, for 165 Greek pages across English, Traditional Chinese, Japanese, Korean and Thai. Each route was read against the actual same-level Japan page named below. The Japan pages serve as editorial depth references, not as geographic analogies: the review asks whether each Greece page makes its own useful base, access, sequence, season and fallback decisions rather than inheriting generic travel copy.

| Greece layer | Greece page | Same-level Japan reference | Comparison focus |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Country | `/greece/` | `/japan/` | Region selection, trip lengths, land/sea split, entry gateways and itinerary trade-offs. Greece separates urban Athens, overland mainland chapters and three distinct island groups instead of presenting the country as one island-hopping loop. |
| Region hub | Every `/greece/<region>/` page | `/japan/kyoto/` | A hub must orient the traveler, distinguish arrival and lodging bases, set a feasible sequence and explain the three linked guides. Greek hubs additionally treat road distances, sparse rural links, local gateways and island transfer risk as part of regional choice. |
| Local guide | Every `/greece/<region>/<guide>/` page | `/japan/kyoto/kyoto-station-south/` | A focused page must describe a useful area/visit, door-to-door access, mobility, time, combinations, date-sensitive checks and a credible alternative. Greek guides retain that planning depth while distinguishing archaeological sites, mountain approaches, ferry legs and road-only coasts. |

The comparison used the authored source data and generated page bodies, including the visible route decisions and linked official authorities/operators. Counts and minimum lengths were treated only as safety checks. The route-to-route notes below record what a reader can decide on each page and the distinction that prevents it from being a renamed template.

## Cross-language and operational copy

Traditional Chinese, Japanese, Korean and Thai translations cover all 33 Greece routes, the four-language navigation/search/sidebar/footer integration and the English terminology used by the route system. Locale copy retains named international transport/operator terms where they are needed to find a live service. Page copy directs readers to dated operator, government or site notices for changeable transport, weather and admission details; it does not publish guessed timetables, prices, opening hours, ferry availability or booking limits.

The Knossos transport copy specifically distinguishes Heraklion’s city bus service from KTEL Heraklion–Lasithi regional coaches, based on the operators’ own itinerary/timetable pages. Other route-level references are listed in the guide data and validated against the allowlisted official authority/operator hosts by `npm run audit:greece`. Ferry disruption instructions point users back to their ticketing carrier and retain an on-island fallback; they do not promise a refund or substitute route.
