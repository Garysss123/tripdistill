# Loire guide photo and depicted-work review

Reviewed 7 October 2026 for the Loire Valley hub and its three existing field guides. This is an attribution and source review, not a legal opinion or formal rights clearance.

| Route image | Photo source-page check | Separate subject-work consideration |
| --- | --- | --- |

The replacement was visually checked against its source and depicts the Loire at Blois, with the distant riverfront town, rather than Chambord. The guide links creator, source and license; resize, display crop and WebP conversion are recorded. | The French Ministry lists the Domaine national de Chambord and says commercial use of images of buildings in national domains generally needs prior authorization, while enumerating information, cultural, teaching and other exemptions ([Ministry guidance](https://www.culture.gouv.fr/thematiques/monuments-sites/monuments-historiques-sites-patrimoniaux/les-monuments-historiques/les-domaines-nationaux); [Heritage Code, Article L621-42](https://www.legifrance.gouv.fr/codes/loda/id/LEGITEXT000006074236/2026-03-14)). We found no manager clearance. Whether a monetized travel-information guide falls within an exemption has not been determined. The Commons photo license applies to the photograph and does not by itself settle separate depicted-estate restrictions; we replaced the façade image conservatively and make no legal conclusion. |

| Tours, Villandry & Azay-le-Rideau | The earlier [Commons garden photo](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Chateau_Villandry_garden,_Loire_Valley,_2004.JPG) was removed after the separate subject-work review below. It depicted the designed landscape, whose authorship extends beyond the historic château. The replacement is the [Commons Loire River photo](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Loire_River,_France_(17376540539).jpg): Larry, CC BY 2.0. The downloaded photo was visually compared with the source and shows the river, a small boat and riverside path. The rendered guide links the creator, source and license and records the WebP/crop adaptation. | Villandry’s estate history says Joachim Carvallo bought the property in 1906 and recreated the Renaissance gardens between 1908 and 1918; it credits painter Lozano and landscape architect Javier de Winthuysen for individual ornamental salons ([estate account](https://www.chateauvillandry.fr/villandry-through-the-ages/the-gardens-of-villandry-are-restored-to-their-renaissance-glory/?lang=en)). This makes the garden photo’s underlying subject a separate rights question from its CC BY-SA 3.0 photographic license. The replacement depicts the river landscape rather than the designed garden or château; no legal clearance conclusion is asserted. |

The in-use photograph records were checked for exact source title, creator, declared photo license and visual match. No freedom-of-panorama notice was found on the Chenonceau source page. The Chambord facade image was removed after the separate national-domain review above. The Villandry garden photo was replaced because the estate documents a 20th-century designed garden and named collaborators. Photo licenses are not treated as licenses to separate depicted works, and this report does not assert formal legal clearance. The site uses source-linked photographs and original editorial text rather than estate-site images or visitor copy.
