Thessaloniki & Northern Greece

Thessaloniki is a walkable waterfront city with a growing metro network; Vergina/Aigai, Halkidiki and the northern hinterland are separate day-trip geographies. Use the city as a real base, then match each excursion to its own coach, airport or road gateway and return.

Regional decision

Keep the waterfront, upper town and museums in the city plan. Add Vergina or a Halkidiki peninsula only when the named coach/road connection leaves an honest visit window.

BASE & NIGHTS

The historic centre and waterfront work for walking and metro access; the station/coach terminal area is practical for a transfer-led arrival but less central to the evening promenade. For Halkidiki, spend nights on the chosen peninsula rather than commuting from the city.

SEASON & EXPOSURE

Summer beach traffic can lengthen road journeys south, while winter rain, wind and mountain access change northern day trips. Follow HNMS and operator notices; keep a city museum or waterfront loop as a same-base alternative.

CAR OR NO CAR

A car helps for multiple Halkidiki beaches or dispersed northern sites. Within Thessaloniki, Metro, bus and walking usually avoid parking friction; for Vergina, compare the dated coach/tour and a planned return before renting a car for one site.

Build the route from the ground up

Give each gateway a realistic day shape.

ROUTE SEQUENCE

Allow two nights for the city itself: use the waterfront, central museums and upper town at walking pace, with the metro as a separate connector rather than the day’s itinerary. Aigai/Vergina is a dedicated archaeological day beyond the urban grid; leave time for the tombs, museum and the exact return to Thessaloniki. Halkidiki is not a short beach shuttle from the promenade. Pick Kassandra or Sithonia, select a served town or a car-ready base, and spend the night there if swimming is the purpose. Do not put a peninsula crossing after a long museum day or connect a remote coach return to a fixed flight without a margin.

WITHOUT A CAR

The urban trip can be car-free: use the current Thessaloniki Metro and OASTH information for airport, waterfront and city movement, checking the airport stop and luggage walk to your accommodation. Intercity rail and KTEL use distinct stations and terminals; name the right one before buying an onward ticket. Aigai needs its own KTEL or organized transfer and site return. For Halkidiki, check KTEL Chalkidiki’s exact terminal, route and beach stop; a coach reaching a resort town does not guarantee access to a remote cove. Choose one served waterfront or peninsula base when no-car access matters more than covering the most coastline.

Thessaloniki Waterfront & Upper Town

Follow the waterfront as a long walk, then choose one upward branch into Ano Poli or the city’s archaeological collections. The Metro and OASTH serve different edges; treat the return from the upper town as a separate bus, taxi or downhill walk decision.

Aigai (Vergina) Royal Tombs & Museum

Aigai’s museum and royal burial landscape lie outside Thessaloniki’s urban transport grid. The day depends on a verified coach, organized transfer or car and the return to the city; a rail journey to Thessaloniki is not the last leg to Vergina.

Halkidiki: Kassandra or Sithonia

Halkidiki’s branching peninsulas make “a beach day” a destination choice, not a short city transfer. Choose Kassandra for a developed resort corridor or Sithonia for a more dispersed coast, then pick a town whose local bus or parking actually reaches the beach you want.
