Cottesloe: rail and final approach

Choose it when the Fremantle Line and either the walk or route 102 fit your group. It works as a stand-alone beach visit; do not assume the station is on the sand or that every bus serves both the station and the promenade.

Scarborough: Stirling plus Surf CAT

Choose it when the Yanchep Line and a direct Surf CAT transfer fit the day. Confirm the current stop, return frequency and any service changes before leaving Stirling Station.

Water or shore only

Choose swimming only when the patrol is active and the flags are set, then follow the lifeguards. Wind, swell, heat or a closure can make a promenade visit or an indoor city alternative the better plan.

Field orientation

Pick the shoreline after you solve its last mile.

Cottesloe and Scarborough are two beach precincts, not adjacent stops in a neat evening loop. Cottesloe has a Fremantle Line station, but the station and bathing beach are separate points. Depending on heat, mobility and the day’s timetable, use a public walk or connect through the current route 102 service to Marine Parade / Grant Street; the timetable also names Cottesloe Station. Scarborough works through a different chain: train to Stirling Station, then the Surf CAT bus to Scarborough Beach. The Public Transport Authority describes the Surf CAT as a daily service running direct from Stirling Station, free and up to every ten minutes during peak periods. Schedules still change. Transperth listed October bus changes effective 11 October 2026, so a February PDF or saved screenshot is only a reference, not the trip plan. Choose the beach that fits your actual arrival, access needs and return departure. Then check the beach and surf conditions. A glowing sunset is not a patrol schedule, and an urban beach is not a promise of calm water.

Access first

For Cottesloe, check the Fremantle Line train in both directions and decide whether the station-to-beach walk suits the group. Route 102 links Claremont Station, Marine Parade / Grant Street and Cottesloe Station in the published network; use Transperth’s date-specific planner to see which stop best serves your beach entry and whether a current bus change affects the connection. For Scarborough, plan the Yanchep Line to Stirling Station and the Surf CAT to the beach precinct, then save the same path in reverse. The Surf CAT is currently described by the Public Transport Authority as free and daily, with up-to-every-10-minute peak frequency; confirm live operations and the last departure rather than relying on that frequency at every hour. Check step-free access and the distance from stop to sand before travelling, especially with a mobility aid or small children. Do not assume the bus remains frequent after a major event or late sunset. Keep enough battery or a written note to identify your return stop.

Sequence

Make the visit a one-beach afternoon or half-day. Arrive before the hottest part of the day when practical, locate the lifesaving patrol and red-yellow flags, then decide whether to swim, sit above the waterline or walk the promenade. Enter the water only within the patrolled area while it is actually being supervised, and follow local lifesaver instructions; never use a sunset photo as evidence that the patrol is still operating. If conditions are rough, windy or the flags are absent, keep the visit land-based. Give the sand, dune vegetation and nearby residents room; use marked access and bins. Before sunset, stop for food or a sheltered promenade rest near the chosen stop and recheck the return service. Leave before the final connection becomes tight. If the swell, wind or transport change makes the selected coast unsuitable, substitute a city museum or another indoor Perth plan rather than attempting to race from Cottesloe to Scarborough.

Failure points

Three reasons to change the plan.

01

Flags, patrol and surf do not follow the sunset

Patrol hours and conditions vary by beach and day. Swim only between flags while lifesavers are supervising, follow their directions and treat no flags as a land-only visit. Check the beach safety page and current surf forecast.

02

Last-mile and service changes

The Cottesloe rail station is not the bathing beach; route 102 and walking are separate options. Scarborough uses Stirling Station and Surf CAT. October 2026 bus changes are already in effect, so refresh both outbound and return directions in Transperth.

03

Heat, wind and exposed sand

The coast can be bright and windy with little shade. Bring drinking water, sun protection and a sheltered break; shorten the walk or leave when weather, fatigue or a beach closure changes the risk.

Country and care boundary

Access is never implied by proximity.

These are working public coastlines on Whadjuk Noongar Country. Follow lifeguards, flags, signs and any beach closure; do not cross dune restoration barriers or enter the water from an unpatrolled stretch because it looks quieter. Keep dogs, boards and beach equipment under the local rules, and give residents, surf users and other visitors space. Use Country-led interpretation where offered; the ocean is a lived place as well as a view.

Planning answers

Cottesloe & Scarborough Coast FAQ

Which beach is easier without a car?

It depends on your starting point and the current service. Cottesloe combines the Fremantle Line with a separate walk or route 102 connection; Scarborough combines the Yanchep Line with the Surf CAT from Stirling. Compare the full return from your accommodation, including the stop-to-sand approach.

Can I swim after sunset?

Do not use sunset as a safety cue. Swim only between red and yellow flags while lifesavers are on patrol and follow their instructions. When flags are not present, make the visit land-based and check current beach advice.

Can I see both beaches in one afternoon?

That usually adds a cross-city transfer without improving the beach visit. Choose one coast, reserve time for the actual last mile and return, and give the other beach a separate day.

What should I do if the water is rough?

Stay out of the water and use a public promenade or a sheltered city alternative. Recheck the local beach notice and marine forecast; conditions can change after you have arrived.

Why not follow a saved timetable PDF?

Transperth’s published bus timetables have effective dates and the network lists planned service changes. A route may be familiar while the stop or departure changes. Refresh the journey planner for your exact day and check it again before leaving the beach.
