Field orientation

Choose the ferry type before choosing the beach.

From Brisbane, the first decision is the Cleveland terminal and whether you need a passenger-only or vehicle ferry. Both bring visitors to Dunwich, but a walk-on day depends on a separate island-bus timetable; carrying a car changes the booking, queue and park-road choices. For a relaxed visit, plan two nights at Dunwich or Point Lookout. A one-day outing is only realistic when the ferry and bus connections leave time for one headland or beach, plus a protected return.

Stay and access

Point Lookout makes sense when a signed headland walk or beach is the main focus and your lodging is linked to the bus route. Dunwich reduces the first and last transfer and suits a slower arrival. Check the exact accommodation address, stop, service date and the operator’s latest return before booking; bus timetables and ferry schedules are separate.

Country and guidance

Minjerribah is Quandamooka Country. Read QYAC and Queensland Parks public guidance, keep to signed visitor routes, and do not infer access to cultural places from a map or online photo. If a community-led cultural experience is publicly offered, follow that provider’s invitation, instructions and booking terms.

One focus per day

A bus-led route can run from Dunwich to Point Lookout, then use one open, signed headland walk and one suitable beach only if the service and conditions allow. The Gorge Walk is an exposed coastal route: carry water, check closures and sea conditions, and choose a shorter lookout or indoor alternative when wind, heat or access makes the full walk unsuitable.

Passenger-ferry day or two-night stay

Dunwich landing, one headland, one booked return.

Build the plan around the particular ferry and island bus on your date. No sample here guarantees a departure, fare or transfer connection.

01Mainland departure

Book Cleveland and the right ferry

Use the operator’s current directions for its Cleveland terminal. Confirm passenger or vehicle space, check-in guidance, the island arrival point and the return sailing. For a walk-on plan, open the island-bus timetable beside the ferry schedule before paying for either leg.

02Island connection

Meet the bus at Dunwich

Check the local operator’s current route, stop and service day from the ferry terminal toward Point Lookout. Carry the return plan with you. If no bus sequence safely fits the ferry window, take a shorter Dunwich-area day or move the visit to an overnight stay.

03Point Lookout

Choose Gorge Walk or beach, not both by default

Follow signed access to the open public headland walk and check Queensland Parks notices before leaving. Add a beach only where access, surf conditions and patrol information suit the group; an exposed lookout can be the complete visit when wind or heat rises.

04Return with margin

Reach Dunwich before the chosen sailing

Allow for the bus ride back, boarding rules and a service disruption. If the last feasible connection is uncertain, stay overnight rather than depending on an unverified taxi, an extra ferry or a tight change across operators.

A two-night stay can place the travel legs on separate days and leave one full island day; a one-night stay is useful only when its booking and ferry dates actually fit.

Failure points

Three reasons to switch to the shorter plan.

01

Ferry and bus do not connect

Ferry and island-bus schedules can differ by date and operator. Check both directions before travel; if the return chain is tight, add a night or keep the visit near the landing.

02

Coastal weather, surf or closure

Use Queensland Parks notices, Bureau of Meteorology warnings and current local beach information. Skip an exposed walk or swimming when wind, surf, heat, rain or a closure changes the risk.

03

Vehicle or public access is restricted

A vehicle ticket is not permission to drive every beach or track. Follow local signs and park rules, stay off dunes, and do not enter a cultural or restricted area because it looks reachable.

Quandamooka Country

Follow the invitation and the boundary.

Recognize Minjerribah as living Quandamooka Country. Use public cultural interpretation and authorized visitor experiences as offered, care for wildlife and beaches, and respect community guidance, residential places and closures.

Planning answers

Minjerribah / North Stradbroke Island FAQ

Is Minjerribah practical as a day trip?

It can work only when the specific passenger ferry, connecting island bus and return ferry line up with enough time for one headland or beach visit. Two nights make the island less dependent on a tight same-day chain.

Where should I stay?

Point Lookout suits a walk-focused visit if your accommodation and bus stop connect to the places you plan to see. Dunwich is closer to the ferry landing and makes a calmer arrival base. Verify the exact address, bus stop and return service before booking.

Do I need a car?

Not necessarily: the local bus links Dunwich with Point Lookout and Amity Point, but it is a separate operator and schedule. A car can widen the plan only if you book the correct vehicle ferry and follow park-road rules.

What should I check before leaving?

Confirm the Cleveland terminal and ferry type, current island-bus timetable, beach patrol and access, Queensland Parks alerts, cultural guidance from Quandamooka organizations, and the marine and weather forecast.
