Field orientation

Choose between the park crossing and the resort ferry.

Mulgumpin is not a casual extension of the Brisbane waterfront. A self-drive park visit needs a booked vehicle ferry, suitable 4WD, required permits, tide and access checks, and enough time for sand roads. A passenger catamaran to Tangalooma Resort is a different landing and holiday product; it does not provide independent park-track access. For a self-drive route, two nights give one island day and a weather or return margin. Without a 4WD setup, book a guided trip or the resort’s own passenger itinerary and follow its limits.

Base and booking

Match a pre-booked campground or accommodation to the chosen ferry landing and one main activity. Park camping is separately reserved; a vehicle ferry ticket alone does not arrange a site or grant permission to drive every beach. A resort room and resort ferry are a separate stay, not an interchangeable park base.

Driving decision

Queensland Parks says 4WD access is essential in the recreation area; check the current access guide for vehicle capability, low-range advice, permits, tides and closures. If you have not driven sand before or your vehicle is not suitable, choose an operator-led trip rather than learning on an exposed beach track.

Stay within one corridor

After landing, use the current park condition report and tide information to select one objective near a route you are allowed to drive. Avoid stitching the wrecks, lighthouse, inland tracks and a resort visit into one circuit; distances on sand, loading windows and soft or wet conditions change the real day.

One objective, with a second night in reserve

Manifest, landing and tide define the route.

Use this sequence for a booked park-oriented 4WD trip. For the passenger-only Tangalooma product, follow the resort itinerary and do not transfer these park-road steps to it.

01Before the mainland

Complete the vehicle and permit plan

Reserve the correct vehicle ferry and campsite or accommodation. Check Queensland Parks driving and camping permits, vehicle suitability and recovery equipment against the official guidance. Carry water, fuel, communications and supplies for a remote island day.

02Cross and orient

Use the exact landing instructions

Follow the ferry operator’s check-in and landing directions; do not assume all services use the same wharf or beach. At arrival, read the current park condition report and signs, confirm the legal route and tide window, and abandon a track that is closed or unsafe.

03Choose one focus

Keep the first outing close to the feasible corridor

Pick one permitted beach or inland objective that fits the day’s tide, vehicle, passenger skill and return requirements. For an overnight park stay, reserve camping separately and follow all fire and waste instructions. A guided tour is the alternative when equipment or experience is missing.

04Close safely

Turn back before tide or time removes the margin

Retain a return buffer for soft sand, traffic, ferry loading and changing weather. No recovery or mobile signal is assured everywhere. If access, vehicle behavior or visibility changes, stop at a safe legal place and use the operator or emergency instructions.

The resort ferry from Holt Street is passenger-only and lands at Tangalooma; it is a separate visit. Confirm resort access and activities directly rather than treating them as park ferry or public road access.

Failure points

Three conditions that cancel a sand-island plan.

01

Vehicle or driver is not ready

Do not take a standard road vehicle onto soft sand. Confirm the exact vehicle guidance and driver capability; if either is missing, change to a guided activity or resort visit.

02

Tide, weather or track changes

Use the current Queensland Parks access report and marine forecast. High tide, rain, wind, fire conditions or a closure can remove a route; never rely on an old map or an assumed return crossing.

03

Camping, fuel or communications fall short

Secure camping permits and bookings before arrival, carry the supplies the route requires, and tell someone the plan. Do not assume water, fuel, medical help or mobile coverage will be available at the next stop.

Quandamooka Country and protected park

Use only designated access.

Mulgumpin is Quandamooka Country. Keep vehicles on permitted routes, respect camping and cultural guidance, protect dunes and wildlife, and leave closed or sensitive areas undisturbed.

Planning answers

Mulgumpin / Moreton Island FAQ

Can I explore Mulgumpin without a 4WD?

A private park-road itinerary requires an appropriate vehicle, permit and driver prepared for sand conditions. Without that setup, choose a booked guided trip or the separate passenger ferry to Tangalooma Resort; do not assume the resort ferry provides park access.

How many nights make the trip worthwhile?

Two nights give a self-drive itinerary room for the crossing, one focused island day and a weather or return margin. A day visit can suit a single operator-led activity when its exact landing, transfers and return are confirmed.

Where should I stay?

Match a prebooked campground or accommodation to the ferry landing and the one activity you intend to do. A resort stay is a different product and location from a park camping or 4WD itinerary.

What must be checked before the crossing?

Verify vehicle suitability and permits, ferry loading and landing instructions, camping reservations, current park access reports, tides, beach and fire conditions, communications and marine weather. Turn back when access or conditions do not match the plan.
