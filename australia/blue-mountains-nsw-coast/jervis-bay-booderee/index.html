Town shore or Booderee

Hyams Beach and the NSW-side park are separate from Booderee. Pick one authority first; a nearby photo does not mean the park gate, road or beach rules are the same.

Vehicle day pass or campsite

Buy the Booderee pass for a vehicle, but reserve Green Patch, Bristol Point or Cave Beach camping separately and early; the pass does not hold a site.

Beach stop or short walk

Choose one currently open beach area or a named signed walk. Every Booderee beach is unsupervised by lifeguards, and water conditions remain a separate decision.

Field orientation

Choose which park and which water authority.

“Jervis Bay” is a region, not one park gate. Hyams Beach sits on the NSW side near the separate Jervis Bay National Park; Booderee National Park is Aboriginal land in Jervis Bay Territory and is managed with the Wreck Bay Aboriginal Community. The hero photograph shows Hyams Beach looking toward Booderee, so it illustrates the neighboring shore rather than an interior park beach. Pick the authority and access system before choosing the day’s beach.

On the public path

Parks Australia says Booderee is Aboriginal land and that it works hand in hand with the Wreck Bay Aboriginal Community. Follow local cultural guidance and current park instructions; the area’s beaches and waters include different land and marine authorities, so do not carry a park pass or rule from one shoreline into another without checking.

Access first

A vehicle visiting Booderee needs a park pass. On 12 October 2026 the official pass page listed a one-day vehicle pass at A$20 (A$10 concession); it also reported the entry station temporarily closed, with online purchase, the Visitor Centre or a mobile van at Jervis Bay Supermarket as alternatives. Recheck the live notice and price before driving. A day pass is not a campsite booking.

Sequence

Base in Huskisson or Vincentia for a town-and-water day, or reserve a Booderee campsite before choosing an overnight inside the park. For a park day, buy the vehicle pass first, read current closure alerts, then choose one open landscape such as Green Patch and its signed walks. Parks Australia’s 2 km Green Patch–Bristol Point loop is a one-hour, grade 2 option; its rocky shore section is best at low tide. Finish with a verified road return before fatigue or a park gate time becomes the constraint.

Failure points

Three reasons to change the plan.

01

Entry and beach closures

As checked 12 October 2026, Parks Australia reported the entry station temporarily closed and Murrays Beach and Iluka day-use access under repair. Recheck official notices and do not plan around either until reopened.

02

Unsupervised water

Parks Australia states Booderee beaches are not supervised by lifeguards. Clear water is not a safety assessment; follow current signs and supervise children directly.

03

Multiple authorities

A Booderee pass does not cover an NSW beach or every marine activity. Confirm the land manager, campsite reservation and marine zoning for each stop.

Country and care boundary

Access is never implied by proximity.

Booderee is Aboriginal land and jointly managed with the Wreck Bay Aboriginal Community. Respect community direction, cultural places, habitat and wildlife; do not feed animals or enter closed sites. Hyams Beach, Booderee beaches and the wider Jervis Bay Marine Park do not share one entry pass or identical water rules.

Planning answers

Jervis Bay & Booderee FAQ

Do I need a pass for Booderee?

A vehicle visiting the park needs a vehicle pass; the official pass page also describes free entry on foot or by bicycle. Check the current pass options and sales notice before travel.

Is the entry station open?

As checked on 12 October 2026, Parks Australia said the entry station was temporarily closed. It listed online purchase, the Visitor Centre and a mobile van at Jervis Bay Supermarket as alternatives; recheck the live closure notice.

Can I camp with a day pass?

No. Camping fees and reservations for Green Patch, Bristol Point and Cave Beach are handled separately. Campsites can fill in peak periods, so book the exact site before building an overnight route around it.

Are Booderee beaches patrolled?

Parks Australia states that none of its beaches are supervised by lifeguards. Check its safety guidance, obey signs, and do not treat calm-looking water as a patrol or safety guarantee.

Is Hyams Beach inside Booderee?

No. Hyams Beach is on the NSW side and is listed by NSW National Parks with Jervis Bay National Park; Booderee is a separate Aboriginal-owned park with its own access rules.
