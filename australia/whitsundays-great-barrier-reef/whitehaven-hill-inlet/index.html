Whitehaven Beach & Hill Inlet tide-window landing.

A boat landing and short walk planned by island end and tide: Whitehaven’s beach and Hill Inlet’s Tongue Point lookout are different stops.

Prioritize the lookout

Choose an itinerary that explicitly names Hill Inlet/Tongue Point and allows the moderate 1.3 km return lookout walk (about 40 minutes). The boat approach depends on a mid-to-high tide access window and park status.

Prioritize beach time

Choose a Whitehaven landing with enough time for your preferred beach stretch; do not assume it includes Hill Inlet or the lookout track.

Choose a gentler day

A high-speed raft can involve a bouncy ride and wet transfer; compare with a larger vessel or a flight-only viewpoint if motion, balance or tender steps are a concern. Disclose mobility needs before booking.

If the landing or sea conditions change

The operator may alter or cancel the landing for wind, swell, tide or safety. Use the confirmed mainland town/lagoon day as the fallback; do not self-charter or cross tidal water to recover a missed viewpoint.

Field orientation

Make the decision before the distance.

Whitehaven Beach stretches along Whitsunday Island; Hill Inlet opens at its northern end, where the Tongue Point track looks across the inlet. Queensland Parks places the lookout track at Tongue Point and says boat access to it is possible from mid to high tide. Do not book a trip described only as “Whitehaven” if the northern lookout is your priority.

Access first

Start from the port stated by your operator—commonly Port of Airlie, Coral Sea Marina North or Hamilton Island Marina—and verify whether the itinerary lands at southern Whitehaven, Tongue Bay/Hill Inlet, or both. Queensland Parks identifies the Hill Inlet lookout as a formed walk at Tongue Point; a boat operator controls landing and any tender transfer. Confirm track time, beach time, tender steps, mobility limits, water and the exact return vessel.

Sequence

For Hill Inlet, reach Tongue Bay only on the booked vessel or an authorized boat, follow the crew’s landing instructions, then allow about 40 minutes for the moderate 1.3 km return walk to the lookout and back before using any beach access. The 500 m return Lookout Beach branch is separate and also has a tide window. If the operator lands at Whitehaven’s southern end instead, keep that as the beach visit and do not assume a cross-island walk to Tongue Point. Return to the same vessel at the stated boarding time.

Four-stage field sequence

A route with an exit built in.

01Orient

Select the actual island end

Compare itinerary names and map the landing: southern Whitehaven beach and northern Hill Inlet are not a single walkable stop.

02Move

Check the tide and park notice

Confirm Tongue Bay access, the lookout track and any alerts close to travel; a track page is not a live operator promise.

03Read

Land, walk, then return

Use crew transfer directions, the formed track and the planned boarding time. Keep the optional beach branch within tide and daylight.

04Return

Accept a changed itinerary

If the master changes the landing or cancels for safety, follow the operator message and choose a mainland day; do not improvise an inlet crossing.

Failure points

Three reasons to change the plan.

01

Tidal inlet

Current can be strong and water level rise quickly; access at the beach track is tide-dependent.

02

Landing logistics

A beach or tender landing may be wet, uneven and exposed. Ask about steps, walking distance and mobility fit before paying.

03

Heat and limited facilities

The island is uninhabited; bring water, sun protection and any personal essentials specified by the operator.

Country and care boundary

Access is never implied by proximity.

Hill Inlet is tidal: Queensland Parks warns currents can be strong and water can rise rapidly. Stay on formed tracks, heed barriers and crew, carry drinking water, and do not wade or swim across the inlet to chase a view. Leave sand, shells, plants and wildlife undisturbed.

Planning answers

Whitehaven Beach & Hill Inlet FAQ

Does every Whitehaven trip visit Hill Inlet?

No. Hill Inlet is at Whitehaven’s northern end. Confirm Tongue Bay/Tongue Point and allow about 40 minutes for Queensland Parks’ moderate 1.3 km return lookout track.

Can I walk from the south end of Whitehaven to the lookout?

Do not plan that connection. Tongue Point is at the northern end and reached by a separate boat landing; use only the route in your confirmed itinerary.

Can I walk down to the water at Hill Inlet?

Queensland Parks says access to the Beach track is possible from mid to high tide and warns the inlet current can be strong. Follow park/crew advice and do not wade across.
