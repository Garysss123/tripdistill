Field orientation

Build the sleep-and-fuel line before the gorge list.

The Gibb is a remote corridor between the Derby side and the Kununurra side, not a scenic road to sample from Broome in one long day. Main Roads WA reports rapidly changing conditions, unsealed and corrugated sections, variable floodways, and an annual dry-season reopening that depends on safe crossings and repair. Its typical seasonal window is May to November, but that pattern is not today’s permission to travel. Regional tourism guidance recommends at least fourteen days to experience the route; this is a planning estimate for a stop-led trip, not an operator itinerary or guarantee that every attraction is open. Choose a guided tour if you do not have remote-road experience or a vehicle contract that expressly permits the road. Self-drivers must reserve each station or campground, confirm fuel and water, and have a turnaround point before leaving Derby.

Access first

Use Derby and Kununurra as separate gateway beds; neither makes the whole corridor a day trip. A flight or one-way hire can shorten the arrival leg only after the operator confirms baggage, vehicle return, road use and flight schedule in writing. Main Roads recommends a high-clearance 4WD and warns that caravans, motorhomes and some camper trailers are not recommended on unsealed roads. Check the rental contract for named road exclusions, tyres, recovery equipment, river crossings and emergency communications. Side roads have different authorities: Main Roads directs travellers to Derby / West Kimberley, Wyndham / East Kimberley and DBCA park reports. A sealed-road or town alternative is safer than improvising a route around an unknown crossing.

Sequence

Reserve the west gateway and the first camp before departure. If open and specifically permitted, the western plan can select Windjana Gorge / Tunnel Creek or a shorter nearby route as one block; do not assume two park entrances or their connecting road are open together. Continue by booked station bases such as Imintji or Mount Barnett only after checking the operator’s current guest access and fuel. Choose one or two gorge walks, not every pin; heat, footing and daylight govern the day. Before the Pentecost River and eastern stations, read the exact live restriction and never enter water of unknown depth. Finish at a reserved eastern stay and allow a separate Kununurra night before the next flight, vehicle hand-back or Purnululu plan. Each leg is conditional on its own road, park, fire and property status.

Vehicle / water / sleep

The road is the itinerary’s limiting resource.

At each stop, choose one walk only after the access, water crossing, heat and next overnight are known. Losing one gorge is preferable to losing the safe return.

Vehicle

Use a capable high-clearance 4WD allowed by the rental company. Check tyres, spare, compressor, recovery gear, fuel reserve, water and communications before the first unsealed leg.

Crossing

Do not enter floodwater of unknown depth or flow. A photo, recent social post or vehicle ahead does not establish depth, substrate, current or crocodile safety. Wait for official/local confirmation or turn back.

Overnight

Reserve the station, campground or lodge and read its own access and check-in rules. A road opening does not mean every side track, gorge, camp or private property is open.

Four-stage field sequence

Derby gateway → authorised west stops → reserved station bases → Kununurra gateway

01Orient

Prepare at Derby

Buy supplies, confirm fuel and stay bookings, load offline maps and recheck Main Roads, Derby Shire, DBCA, BOM and Emergency WA. If any source cannot confirm the west segment, wait.

02Move

Choose one western block

Use only open and permitted Windjana / Tunnel Creek / Wunaamin Miliwundi access. Keep the day short enough to reach the booked camp in daylight; do not stack closed or distant sites.

03Read

Advance by booked bases

Confirm each station, fuel point, gorge entry, park fee and river-crossing status before leaving the previous overnight. Drive to the road surface and stop for unknown water.

04Return

Close in the east

Reach the reserved Kununurra bed before another remote activity or flight. Keep an extra night for a delay; do not use an unverified side road to make a connection.

Failure points

Three reasons to change the plan.

01

Seasonal opening is not a live clearance

Main Roads says the road is commonly closed in the wet season and normally opens in the dry season only after crossings and repairs are safe. On 13 October 2026 this page does not certify a current open segment, access restriction or crossing depth. If status is unknown, do not depart.

02

Fire, heat or park closure

Check Emergency WA, BOM and every DBCA alert for the exact segment. A park closure or emergency warning removes that stop; do not enter from a second gate or continue toward smoke. Use a confirmed gateway stay and resume only on official advice.

03

Vehicle, fuel or communications failure

Corrugations, punctures and sparse services can turn a small issue into a long delay. Carry the equipment and fuel plan recommended for the vehicle, share an itinerary and use emergency communications suited to the route. If your hire terms, reserves or comms are unclear, take a guided tour or postpone.

Country and care boundary

Access is never implied by proximity.

This route crosses Traditional Owner Country, pastoral leases, parks and private stations with different rules. Main Roads says the land beside unsealed roads may be private property. Stay on the public road and explicitly permitted visitor tracks; use named park passes and booked station access, follow cultural closures and do not enter a community, artwork site, closed crossing or private driveway without permission.

Planning answers

Gibb River Road FAQ

How many days should I give the Gibb River Road?

Australia’s North West recommends at least fourteen days to explore the full corridor and its stops. Plan the gateway and booked station nights separately, then add a buffer in Kununurra. The number is an editorial planning floor, not a guarantee every park or camp is open. If you have less time, choose a guided section and omit the rest.

Can I drive the Gibb in a 2WD or campervan?

Main Roads recommends a high-clearance 4WD; it does not recommend caravans, motorhomes and some camper trailers on unsealed roads. Your rental contract may prohibit the route regardless of advertised capability. Confirm vehicle, tyres, recovery, river-crossing rules and insurance with the actual operator; if any item is unresolved, choose a guided tour.

Is the road open in October?

A typical season is not a live road check. Main Roads says opening depends on crossing levels and damage repair and provides a current Travel Map and status boards at both ends. Check that map, the responsible Shire, DBCA park alerts and local operators on the day. If the chosen segment is closed or its status is unknown, do not enter.

Can I cross water if another vehicle just went through?

No. Main Roads warns against driving through water of unknown depth. Flow, substrate, level and hazards can change; another vehicle or a social post is not a safe assessment. Wait for the responsible authority or local operator to confirm the specific crossing, or turn back.

What replaces a gorge walk during a fire or park closure?

Follow Emergency WA and DBCA closure instructions first. If travel remains permitted, return to a reserved gateway or use a separately confirmed town/operator activity; do not use another gate, private station road or community track to reach the closed area. An active warning has no sightseeing fallback.

Can I add Purnululu to the Gibb route?

Treat Purnululu as a separate East Kimberley park block with its own road or flight access, camp, park opening and nights. Do not add it as a same-day detour from a remote Gibb camp. Reach a confirmed Kununurra gateway first and book the park sequence after checking current alerts.
