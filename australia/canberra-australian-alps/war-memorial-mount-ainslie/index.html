Australian War Memorial & Mount Ainslie memory and sightline.

Give the Australian War Memorial its own quiet visit, then decide whether the Mount Ainslie summit walk, road lookout or a shorter day best fits ceremony access, heat, fire and mobility.

Is this a ceremony day or a summit day?

If the 4:30pm Last Post Ceremony is the priority, keep the Memorial visit as the main event and do not schedule a late uphill walk. For a summit walk, start earlier and preserve daylight for the 4.5km return.

Walk the track or drive to the lookout?

The Kokoda Track has about 250m ascent, steep sections, steps and uneven patches. Choose Mount Ainslie Drive only if the road and reserve are open and a vehicle is suitable; it avoids the walk but remains weather-exposed.

What is the shorter fallback?

Spend time at the Memorial and its immediate grounds, then omit the summit if fire danger, heat, smoke, mobility or ceremony timings reduce the safety margin. The day remains complete without reaching the lookout.

Field orientation

Treat the Memorial and summit as two separate decisions.

The Australian War Memorial stands at the foot of Mount Ainslie on Canberra’s north-south sightline. The grounds connect the national commemorative precinct to a mountain reserve, but a shared view does not mean the collection visit and summit walk should be rushed into one block. Start by choosing whether the Memorial’s galleries, a ceremony, or the summit is the day’s anchor. The Mount Ainslie Summit Walk follows the Kokoda Track from the Treloar Crescent car park behind the Memorial and returns by the same route; the official Parks ACT description gives it as 4.5km return with about 250m ascent. Mount Ainslie Drive provides a separate road approach to the lookout. Select one access method according to ability, weather and reserve status.

On the public path

This is a place of remembrance, service and personal grief, not a backdrop to perform against. Keep voices low, give visitors space at names and galleries, follow the Memorial’s photography rules and step aside during formal observance. The view from Mount Ainslie helps explain Canberra’s planned land axis, but it does not reduce the Memorial to a viewpoint. A pause between the collection and the walk is part of a considerate itinerary.

Access first

The Memorial lists free entry, with galleries open daily 10am–5pm except Christmas Day and progressively closing from 4pm; the Last Post Ceremony is held at 4:30pm. Confirm any special event, gallery access and current conditions on the visit page. Bags larger than 40 × 30 × 30cm must be cloaked. Transport Canberra’s current visitor routes guide lists R54 for the Memorial stop; check its date, direction and final return rather than planning only the arrival. The signed Kokoda Track begins at the Treloar Crescent car park behind the Memorial. Parks ACT describes the summit route as a sealed 4.5km return walk with 250m ascent, moderate overall but steep in sections with steps and uneven ground. A car can use Mount Ainslie Drive to reach the lookout, subject to road and reserve conditions. Neither the bus stop nor the road lookout guarantees that the walking track is open.

Sequence

Check Memorial opening, ceremony and event information first. If you want the Last Post Ceremony, arrive early enough for the collection and use the 4:30pm ceremony as the fixed end point; do not add a summit hike after it. Otherwise, visit the galleries earlier in the day, leave a respectful pause, then reassess the weather, daylight, ACT Parks alerts and fire restrictions. Start the Kokoda Track at Treloar Crescent only if the group can manage the steep sections and return before dark; turn around if heat, smoke, fatigue or track conditions change. If walking is unsuitable, use the legal road approach to the Mount Ainslie lookout when it is open, or finish at the Memorial grounds. R54 is the useful public transport connection for the Memorial, not a summit shuttle. Plan the return from the correct stop and allow for city traffic or a bridge diversion.

Remember first; climb only with time and conditions.

The Memorial’s fixed public hours and ceremony set a different rhythm from the optional nature-reserve walk.

Collection

Australian War Memorial galleries and any confirmed public ceremony.

Walk option

Kokoda Track from Treloar Crescent; 4.5km return and about 250m ascent.

Road option

Mount Ainslie Drive lookout when access is open; it is not a bus-served summit.

Four-stage field sequence

Use the Memorial visit as the anchor and leave a clear return margin.

01Orient

Confirm access and observance

Check gallery hours, event notices, cloaking rules and the Last Post Ceremony time. If the ceremony is the fixed event, organize the Memorial visit around it and remove the late summit walk.

02Move

Reach the Memorial corridor

R54 serves the Australian War Memorial stop on the current Transport Canberra visitor routes guide. Verify the live date, direction, stop and last useful departure; if driving, check parking and any event traffic controls.

03Read

Choose one summit approach

From Treloar Crescent, take the signed Kokoda Track only when the route is open and the group can handle the climb. Otherwise use Mount Ainslie Drive if road access is available, or remain in the commemorative precinct.

04Return

Close before conditions tighten

Return on the track before darkness and reassess smoke, wind, heat and fire warnings. Do not start a climb late to catch a view; keep the return service, accessible route or event boundary intact.

Failure points

Three reasons to change the plan.

01

Ceremonies, bags and remembrance

Formal events can change gallery access, traffic, photography and movement around the grounds. Bags over 40 × 30 × 30cm need cloaking. Follow staff directions and keep noise and photography respectful around the Roll of Honour and formal ceremony.

02

Steep track and exposed summit

The walk is moderate overall but has steep sections, steps and uneven surfaces. Heat, wind, smoke, rain and low daylight increase effort; carry water and turn back early.

03

Fire and reserve alerts

Parks ACT may close reserve roads or walking routes during fire danger or other incidents. Check alerts and total fire-ban information immediately before departure; never route around a closure.

Country and care boundary

Access is never implied by proximity.

The Memorial honours people who served and died; visitors may be attending for personal reasons. Do not stage disruptive photographs, touch or mark names, or cross barriers during a ceremony. On Mount Ainslie, stay on the formed route, respect fire and wildlife closures and leave natural and cultural features undisturbed.

Planning answers

Australian War Memorial & Mount Ainslie FAQ

Is the Australian War Memorial free?

The current visit page lists free entry. Galleries are generally open 10am–5pm except Christmas Day and begin closing from 4pm; special events can affect access, so verify on the date.

What time is the Last Post Ceremony?

The Memorial lists a 4:30pm ceremony. If you plan to attend, make it the day’s fixed endpoint and do not leave a summit walk until after the ceremony.

How difficult is the Mount Ainslie Summit Walk?

Parks ACT describes a 4.5km return walk with about 250m ascent, moderate overall but steep in sections with steps and uneven ground. Check track status and choose the road lookout or no climb if that terrain does not suit the group.

Does R54 go to the summit?

No. Transport Canberra lists R54 for the War Memorial stop. The summit walk begins at Treloar Crescent behind the Memorial; the Mount Ainslie Drive lookout is a separate road approach.

What if the track is closed or the weather changes?

Stay within the Memorial grounds or use another open indoor visit. Do not bypass a Parks ACT closure. Heat, smoke, fire restrictions, wind and fading daylight are reasons to omit the walk.
