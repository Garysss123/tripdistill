Field orientation

Make the decision before the distance.

A central Melbourne walk that uses the numbered street grid and tram corridors to connect selected lanes without turning service alleys into a scavenger hunt. The useful plan begins by asking you to choose one east–west and one north–south spine, then add only lanes that genuinely connect them. It then protects the return before adding another distant stop.

Four-stage field sequence

A route with an exit built in.

01Orient

Choose the station exit

Match the exit to the planned first block; large stations can release visitors on the wrong side of the grid.

02Move

Follow the primary spine

Use a major street to settle bearings before entering smaller lanes.

03Read

Read a few public lanes

Select lanes with clear public access and leave service doors and residents unobstructed.

04Return

Close at rail or tram

Finish beside a direct service before weather or evening crowds change the walk.

Failure points

Three reasons to change the plan.

01

Tram lanes

Look both ways and use marked stops; trams are quiet and share complex road space.

02

Crowded lanes

Narrow routes can become unsafe during deliveries or events; bypass rather than force passage.

03

Fare assumptions

The free-tram area has boundaries. Confirm the journey before boarding.

Planning answers

CBD Laneways & Tram Grid FAQ

How much time should CBD Laneways & Tram Grid receive?

A full day supports one central grid, one interior and meals without lane fatigue.

Can I combine CBD Laneways & Tram Grid with another major chapter?

Pair with Federation Square or Southbank; keep St Kilda for another transit chapter.

What should I verify before leaving?

Check PTV disruptions, station exits, free-tram boundaries, venue bookings, weather and event closures.
