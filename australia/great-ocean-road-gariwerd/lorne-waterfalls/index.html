Lorne & Otway Waterfalls cliff-road to wet forest.

A coast-and-forest day that makes the inland turn, waterfall stairs and narrow access road part of the schedule.

Sheoak or Erskine?

Choose Sheoak for the creek-side walk and picnic facilities; choose Erskine for a brief upper lookout, with the lower viewing platform as a separate 230-stair commitment. Do not select both before checking weather and daylight.

Are the creek and steps safe today?

High water can make Sheoak stepping stones slippery; recent rain also affects footing. At Erskine, the first lookout is the low-commitment view, while the lower route has many stairs and slick rocks. Turn back when the surface or group’s mobility says so.

Can the coach plan reach the falls?

A connection to Lorne town does not solve the last mile to a forest carpark. Confirm the actual dated outbound and return legs or book a suitable transfer; otherwise keep the day in town.

Field orientation

Choose the stairs and road before leaving town.

Lorne gives the coast-and-forest day a useful base, but each waterfall has its own walk, road and ground condition. Parks Victoria places Sheoak Falls about nine minutes from town and describes several nearby trails; Erskine Falls is a further drive, with a short first lookout walk and a much steeper lower platform. Pick one waterfall to match mobility, rain and remaining daylight. The retained hero photograph is Erskine Falls itself; the waterfall view does not show the stairs, the carpark approach or today’s open status.

Access first

A car gives the clearest last-mile control, but the inland roads are narrow and their trailhead bays are not safe places to improvise a turn or park outside a marked space. If arriving by coach, plan two journeys: the dated connection to Lorne town and a confirmed legal way to the selected waterfall and back. Do not assume a coach stop is a park shuttle or walk along an unsafe shoulder. Confirm the exact land manager and notice for each point: Great Ocean Road coastal areas and individual Great Otway sites do not all share one operator after the management transitions. Check Parks Victoria and GORCAPA against the exact place, plus VicTraffic and the local forecast.

Sequence

Start in Lorne with fuel, food and the inland-road decision. For a shorter visit, choose the signed Sheoak Falls route and avoid creek stepping stones when the water is high; the official fallback is the short approach from the Sheoak Falls carpark. For a separate Erskine choice, the carpark-to-first-lookout walk is short; descend to the lower platform only when the group can manage 230 stairs and wet footing, then return the same way. Reserve extra forest time for slow bends and parking, and get back to town before dusk. Hopetoun and Triplet Falls are roughly a 1.5-hour drive from Lorne, so they belong to a separate inland day or overnight, not the tail end of this loop.

Four-stage field sequence

Take the open walk that matches the day’s footing.

01Orient

Check the coast and forest notices

Confirm which authority manages the precise foreshore, road or waterfall, and read its current access notice. Check rain, wind, road works, fire risk, sunset and legal parking before leaving Lorne; a town weather impression does not describe a wet forest track.

02Move

Commit to a single trailhead

For Sheoak, locate the signed falls route and picnic/carpark access; when water is high, use the operator’s short carpark approach rather than the slippery creek crossing. For Erskine, make the upper lookout the default and add the lower platform only if 230 stairs suit the whole group.

03Read

Walk, then reassess

Stay on marked surfaces and use handrails. If runoff, wind, a fallen tree, crowding or a closure changes the route, turn around and use the safe viewpoint already reached. Do not create an off-track shortcut to a waterfall pool.

04Return

Return through daylight

Allow for slow bends, wildlife and parking departure. Reach Lorne with a daylight margin; if continuing west, decide in town whether the driver and road allow the next booked base, otherwise stay overnight and move the coast leg to tomorrow.

Failure points

Three reasons to change the plan.

01

Wet rock and high creek

Stepping stones and waterfall rocks can be slippery. Use the short signed alternative where offered, or skip the crossing and return; a photo is not worth a fall.

02

Narrow forest access

Treefall, storms, fire, road work and limited parking can close a route independently of Lorne town. Read the managing authority’s latest notice and never block a gate, emergency access or traffic lane.

03

Dusk and overpacked itinerary

The farther Hopetoun/Triplet pair takes a long inland drive from Lorne. Remove it from this day rather than compressing the walk or driving tired after dark.

Country and care boundary

Access is never implied by proximity.

Keep to formed walks, steps, platforms and public lookouts. Water high enough to make stepping stones slippery is a reason to use the alternate path or turn around, not to cross faster. Do not enter the plunge pool, climb the waterfall face, trample ferns or leave litter. Roads through wet forest are shared with residents, workers and wildlife; do not stop in a live lane for a photograph and obey storm, fire, treefall and cultural-access closures.

Planning answers

Lorne & Otway Waterfalls FAQ

Is Erskine Falls wheelchair accessible?

The carpark-to-first-lookout route is short, but the lower viewing platform is reached by 230 stairs and can be slippery. Confirm current accessible-route details and choose the upper viewpoint or another confirmed site if stairs do not suit.

Can I see Sheoak and Erskine Falls in the same day?

They are a short drive apart, but the right choice depends on parking, track conditions, daylight and mobility. Choose one as the guaranteed stop and add the other only if the safe return remains comfortable.

Can I add Hopetoun and Triplet Falls after Lorne?

Parks Victoria places them about 1.5 hours from Lorne, so treat them as a separate inland day or overnight rather than a spare stop after the Lorne falls.
