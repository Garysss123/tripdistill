St Moritz & Upper Engadin

Choose one Upper Engadin system each day: lakes and villages, the Bernina rail corridor, or a single elevation gain.

St Moritz is the hotel and network anchor, not the whole Upper Engadin. RhB trains and regional buses connect the lake valley, Pontresina and the Bernina line, while each mountain railway has its own operating season and exposure. Keep the day on one corridor and decide the return to the overnight base before leaving the valley floor.

St Moritz & Engadin Lakes

This lower-valley plan is more flexible than a summit day, but open shore and high-valley wind still affect comfort. Several short transit hops can consume the time better spent on one village and one lake.

Pontresina & Bernina Railway

The railway is the main experience and uses much of the day. Leaving the train at multiple stops needs enough time for the next usable service; the route does not guarantee a particular view or connection.

Allow half a day for a short out-and-back and most of a day for a longer southbound segment. Include waiting and transfer time.03

Muottas Muragl, Pontresina & Diavolezza

The two viewpoints have different elevation, exposure and transport chains. Combining them risks turning a landscape day into two rushed lift visits; Diavolezza needs a more conservative weather and altitude decision.

Allow half a day for one lift and viewpoint, with extra time only for a currently open trail and a conservative descent margin.
Planning answers

St Moritz & Upper Engadin FAQ

How many nights should I plan for St Moritz and the Upper Engadin?

Three nights cover the lake valley and one excursion; four nights create room to move a high-mountain day around the forecast. St Moritz is the broad hotel and rail base; Pontresina shortens access to the Bernina corridor. RhB and regional buses serve different valley segments.

How should I travel around St Moritz and the Upper Engadin?

Check the exact RhB service and reservation conditions, and verify each lift or trail separately. Panoramic services and regional trains are not the same product.

What should I do if the weather or service changes?

Use a lower valley walk, village visit or rail segment. Conditions at lake level do not prove safety on frozen lakes or at a high station.
