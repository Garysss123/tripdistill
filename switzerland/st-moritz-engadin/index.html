St Moritz & Upper Engadin

Choose one Upper Engadin system at a time: the lake valley, the Bernina railway, or one high mountain access chain.

St Moritz is the valley’s best-known base, but the useful planning question is which Upper Engadin system belongs in each day. Keep Lake St Moritz and nearby villages as a lower-valley block; use Pontresina to orient a Bernina rail day; choose only one mountain railway when visibility and operations support it. The base town and the excursion are separate decisions, and each return should be checked before leaving the valley floor.

Fallback

Keep a lower-risk layer ready.

When visibility, wind or an operating notice removes the high route, keep the day in St Moritz or Pontresina: choose an open village visit, a short verified lakeside section, or a rail segment with a confirmed return. Do not substitute a second lift automatically, step onto frozen water, or turn a closed trail into an improvised shortcut.

Route comparison

Three different reasons to leave the hotel.

01

St Moritz & Engadin Lakes

This route keeps the day near the valley floor and leaves a high summit decision for another forecast window. The trade is less altitude and fewer panoramic viewpoints in exchange for a more flexible walk, village stop and rail fallback. Wind, snow cover and path closures still matter at lake level.

Allow half a day for St Moritz town plus one signed lakeside section. A second village or lake only belongs in the plan if the dated bus or RhB connection and return still leave time for the walk and a meal.02

Pontresina & Bernina Railway

The railway is the day’s main activity, so every stop competes with time on the line and the return connection. A panoramic service provides a different booked experience; a regional journey supports a separately planned shorter segment. Neither guarantees clear views, an unplanned stop or a convenient later train.

Reserve at least half a day for a focused out-and-back from Pontresina. A longer Bernina corridor ride can occupy most of a day once the selected service, any stopover, booking steps and return margin are included.03

Muottas Muragl, Pontresina & Diavolezza

Muottas Muragl sits above the lake valley and is the more focused choice for reading the Engadin’s shape. Diavolezza reaches a substantially higher glacial setting near 3,000 metres, with greater cold, wind and altitude exposure. The two are different excursions, not interchangeable viewpoints or a sensible same-day checklist.

Allow half a day for one lift and the upper station. Add walking time only after checking the exact route opening, weather, equipment and descent margin; a summit visit should not be squeezed between a long Bernina train ride and dinner.
Planning answers

St Moritz & Upper Engadin FAQ

How many nights should I plan for St Moritz and the Upper Engadin?

Two nights allow St Moritz and one excursion; three nights separate the lake valley from a Bernina journey; four leave a useful weather window for one high-mountain system.

Should I stay in St Moritz or Pontresina?

St Moritz is a broad valley base with town and lake access. Pontresina is a better fit when the Bernina corridor is the priority. Compare the exact hotel walk, rail or bus connection, and luggage approach.

What should I check before a mountain or rail day?

Use SBB and RhB for the exact service and return, the selected lift operator for operating status, MeteoSwiss for the relevant elevation, and local route information for trail access. Do not infer lake-ice or trail safety from appearance.
