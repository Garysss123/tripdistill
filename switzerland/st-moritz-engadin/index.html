St Moritz & Upper Engadin

The Engadin is a wide high valley, not a single resort: use rail and bus to separate lakes, villages and Bernina scenery.

St Moritz is the transport and hotel anchor, but the Upper Engadin’s identity stretches through lakes, Pontresina and the Bernina corridor. Distances are modest by rail yet elevation and winter conditions remain real. Choose one landscape system per day instead of repeatedly returning to St Moritz between every stop.

Route comparison

Three different reasons to leave the hotel.

01

St Moritz & Engadin Lakes

A lake day is lower-risk than a summit excursion and reveals the broad Engadin landscape. It gives fewer dramatic high viewpoints, making it ideal when cloud sits on the peaks.

Half to full day depending how many lakes/villages you include. One coherent valley segment beats several short station hops.02

Pontresina & Bernina Railway

A long through-journey gives the full landscape sequence but consumes a day; a shorter Pontresina–Bernina segment leaves time for walking or village context. Choose according to whether rail itself is the attraction.

Half a day for a focused out-and-back section; most of a day for a longer Bernina corridor journey.03

Muottas Muragl, Pontresina & Diavolezza

Muottas Muragl is gentler and emphasizes the lake valley; Diavolezza is higher, colder and more alpine. Doing both in one day usually turns the landscape into a lift checklist.

Half a day for one viewpoint, more with hiking. Protect time for the return train/bus.
