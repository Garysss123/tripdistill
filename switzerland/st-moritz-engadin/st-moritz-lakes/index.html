Access first

Know where the day actually begins.

Start at St Moritz station and orient to the town-side edge of Lake St Moritz. Before walking, choose one signed segment from the current Engadin route map: a short lake loop, a route toward Lej da Staz, or a village connection toward Celerina. Mark a real turn-back point and the nearest usable return stop; do not assume a continuous public path around every lake.

Trade-off

What this route gives up.

This route keeps the day near the valley floor and leaves a high summit decision for another forecast window. The trade is less altitude and fewer panoramic viewpoints in exchange for a more flexible walk, village stop and rail fallback. Wind, snow cover and path closures still matter at lake level.

Time envelope

Allow half a day for St Moritz town plus one signed lakeside section. A second village or lake only belongs in the plan if the dated bus or RhB connection and return still leave time for the walk and a meal.

Combine only when it helps

Pair with St Moritz town in the evening. Keep the Bernina rail journey and either high mountain system on separate days so a walk can shrink without breaking the return plan.

Fallback

A useful day even when the postcard disappears.

If the open shore is uncomfortable or a path is closed, shorten the walk to the signed section that is confirmed open, then return through St Moritz or a checked village stop. In winter, keep entirely off lake ice unless an official local notice explicitly identifies an open area; never judge ice strength by color, footprints or other visitors.

Planning answers

St Moritz & Engadin Lakes FAQ

Can I walk around every Upper Engadin lake?

Do not assume a continuous public shoreline. Choose a signed route from the current Engadin map, identify its turn-back point, and verify each access and return connection.

Is a frozen lake safe to cross in winter?

Not from appearance alone. Follow current local notices and stay off ice unless an official authority explicitly opens a defined area.

What is the useful bad-weather alternative?

Keep a short open town-side section, a village visit or a checked rail segment. Save the mountain systems for a separate forecast window.
