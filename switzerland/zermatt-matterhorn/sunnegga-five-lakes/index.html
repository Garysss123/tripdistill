Sunnegga & Five Lakes

Choose between the Sunnegga viewpoint and the full Five Lakes walk only after checking the lift chain and trail condition. The official route descends from Blauherd past Stellisee, Grindjisee, Grünsee, Moosjisee and Leisee to Sunnegga, so the end point and return funicular belong in the plan before you start.

Access first

Know where the day actually begins.

From Zermatt, use the underground Sunnegga funicular and the onward lift connection to Blauherd when operating. The Five Lakes Trail is a point-to-point mountain walk, not a loop that returns to its start: the operator lists Blauherd–Stellisee–Grindjisee–Grünsee–Moosjisee–Leisee–Sunnegga. Confirm the date-specific lift chain, trail notice and any route ticket terms before leaving the village.

Trade-off

What this route gives up.

Sunnegga is the lower-commitment choice for a viewpoint, Leisee visit or short walk. The complete trail is about 9.8 km and rated moderate by the operator, with an official walking estimate of 2 hours 30 minutes and 454 m of elevation change; that estimate does not include your photo stops, breaks or delays. The long route adds lake variety but uses more daylight and legs, and can be a poor fit when the trail is wet, snowy or exposed to heat.

Time envelope

Allow a large half day for the full walk, including ascent to Blauherd, breaks and the Sunnegga descent. The operator’s route estimate is a reference rather than a personal pace guarantee. A viewpoint-only visit can fit a shorter block, while adding another summit system usually makes the day feel compressed.

Combine only when it helps

Pair the short Sunnegga visit with a relaxed village evening. Keep Gornergrat and Matterhorn Glacier Paradise for different days so the walk can end with the Sunnegga return rather than a race to another lift or train.

Four-stage operating line

Arrive, orient, commit, then protect the return.

Check the Five Lakes Trail page for route and seasonal notes, the Zermatt Bergbahnen lifts-and-pistes status for both the Blauherd approach and Sunnegga return, and MeteoSwiss for wind, heat, thunderstorms and visibility. Confirm ticket scope and the last return lift on the date you travel; no schedule or bundled ticket is assumed here.

  1. 01Arrive

    Check both ends

    Confirm that the lift to Blauherd and the return from Sunnegga fit your date. The walking route does not end where it begins.

  2. 02Orient

    Ride Sunnegga to Blauherd

    Use the published lift chain and check conditions again at the top. If cloud, wind or trail notices change the plan, stay at the viewpoint or descend.

  3. 03Commit

    Follow the lake sequence

    Walk Blauherd–Stellisee–Grindjisee–Grünsee–Moosjisee–Leisee–Sunnegga on the signed path. Carry water, sun protection and a layer; pause without blocking the path.

  4. 04Return

    Finish at Sunnegga

    Take the return funicular only if operating. If you shorten the walk, use a signed return route and a confirmed lift rather than aiming for a different valley station by guesswork.

Fallback

A useful day even when the postcard disappears.

If any trail section is closed, snow-covered or uncomfortable, do not improvise an unmarked shortcut between lakes. Keep the visit to Sunnegga’s accessible viewpoint or return by the lift route confirmed for that date. If the upper chain is not running, use the Zermatt village walk and move the lake day; the operator currently lists June–September for the Five Lakes season, but day-specific opening still needs checking.

Three failure points

Change the plan before the problem compounds.

01

Season and snow

The operator lists June–September as the Five Lakes season. Snow, maintenance or trail conditions can still affect an individual date, so verify before setting out.

02

One-way route confusion

The trail begins at Blauherd and ends at Sunnegga. Check the walk direction and lift/ticket legs, not just the phrase “Five Lakes”.

03

Exposure and pace

The official duration excludes your breaks and personal pace. Turn around or shorten the day if heat, wet ground, fatigue or weather reduces your margin.

Planning answers

Sunnegga & Five Lakes FAQ

Is the Five Lakes Trail a loop?

No. The listed route runs from Blauherd via five named lakes to Sunnegga. Plan the lift to the start and the return from the finish separately.

How long does the walk take?

The operator currently lists 9.8 km, moderate difficulty, 454 m of elevation change and a 2 hour 30 minute estimate. Treat that as its route estimate; breaks, pace, weather and trail condition change your actual time.

When can I plan the trail?

The operator currently lists June–September as its best season. Check the route and lift notices for your exact date; that seasonal listing does not guarantee a specific opening.
