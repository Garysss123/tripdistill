Lausanne Old City & Ouchy

Connect Place de la Palud and Lausanne Cathedral to the lower Ouchy quay in one direction: use M2 for the steep transfer and spend your walking energy on the old lanes or lakeside, not a repeated climb.

Upper city to lake

Start at Riponne-Maurice Béjart, visit Place de la Palud and the cathedral, then ride M2 from Lausanne-Flon to Ouchy-Olympique. This minimizes uphill walking.

02

Lake to old center

Begin at Ouchy-Olympique for the quay and Château d’Ouchy, then ride M2 to Riponne for the cathedral hill. Best when the lake is your first light or photo stop.

Short historic loop

Stay around Place de la Palud, Escaliers du Marché and the cathedral, then return from Riponne by M2. Choose this when rain, mobility or a train connection limits the day.

Access first

Know where the day actually begins.

Start at Lausanne-Gare and ride Métro M2 uphill to Riponne-Maurice Béjart. Walk to Place de la Palud, then climb the Escaliers du Marché to Lausanne Cathedral; Lausanne Tourisme notes 150 steps from the square. Return through Lausanne-Flon and take M2 downhill to Ouchy-Olympique. Reverse the order if you want the lake first. The M2 saves the major vertical change; it does not make every old-town lane level or step-free.

Trade-off

What this route gives up.

This is the clearest first-day route for reading Lausanne’s slope: market square, cathedral hill, station interchange and lakefront. It gives up a long museum visit and several neighborhood detours. Pick one interior stop only if its current opening time still leaves a real block for the walk.

Time envelope

Allow four to five hours including a meal and the walk down through the old center. Add a separate museum block for a longer day; do not count the train ride as a promise about current interval or transfer time.

Combine only when it helps

Pair the old center with Ouchy in one direction, then keep Lavaux for another half day. Add the Olympic Museum only if you are happy to give the cathedral quarter a shorter visit. Montreux is a separate rail day, not a casual extension.

Regional context

Fit this field guide into Lausanne & Lavaux, not into an isolated checklist.

Lausanne works as a two-level city day: the old center sits above the station, while Ouchy opens onto the lake below. East of town, Lavaux is a working terraced landscape reached by regional train. A useful stay protects one complete Lausanne route and one selected vineyard section instead of treating every lake village as a stop on the same walk.

Base strategy

Plan two nights in Lausanne for the old center, Ouchy and a half-day in Lavaux. Three nights leave room for the Olympic Museum or a full vineyard walk without compressing both into one day. For an easy first base, stay near Lausanne-Gare or Lausanne-Flon for M2 and rail links; choose Ouchy for a quieter waterfront evening and accept the uphill M2 ride. Consider Cully only when an early Lavaux start or a confirmed local stay matters more than Lausanne’s late connections.

Transfer system

In town, use Métro M2 between Lausanne-Gare, Lausanne-Flon or Riponne-Maurice Béjart and Ouchy-Olympique; walk only one direction across the steep center. For Lavaux, take an SBB regional train from Lausanne to Cully, then follow signed paths through Epesses toward Rivaz only if your legs, daylight and return train allow. The CGN pier at Ouchy is a separate option: compare its live N1 Lausanne–Évian or N2 Lausanne–Thonon sailing with the onward and return plan before buying.

Season gate

Longer spring-to-autumn daylight gives more room for terrace walking, but open vineyard slopes can be hot, exposed or slippery after rain. Harvest work and cellar availability vary by producer and date; ask before entering a tasting room, and stay on public marked paths around working rows. In winter, shorten the walk around daylight and the current trail condition. Lausanne’s indoor museums and M2-linked districts remain the lower-weather plan.

Regional fallback

If rain, heat, a trail notice or a missed train makes the terraces a poor bet, keep the visit to Cully’s village and lakeshore, return by rail, and use the saved block for Lausanne’s cathedral quarter or the Olympic Museum. Skip the boat when its departure, return or border-crossing requirements do not fit. Do not trade a safe return for one more vineyard viewpoint.

Four-stage operating line

Arrive, orient, commit, then protect the return.

Check TL’s live M2 status and the Lausanne Transport Card conditions for your lodging. The official 2026 Lausanne Tourisme listing shows the Olympic Museum open Tuesday–Sunday, 09:00–18:00, with Monday closures except holidays or special events; check the current calendar again before going.

  1. 01Arrive

    Lausanne-Gare to Riponne

    Board Métro M2 toward Croisettes and leave at Riponne-Maurice Béjart. Check the live line notice and station access before setting out.

  2. 02Orient

    Place de la Palud to the cathedral

    Walk to the historic square and follow the Escaliers du Marché up to Lausanne Cathedral. Keep the 150-step climb in view; pause or skip the interior if stairs are not right for you.

  3. 03Commit

    Flon to Ouchy-Olympique

    Return through the upper center to Lausanne-Flon, take M2 downhill, then follow the signed pedestrian route from Ouchy-Olympique toward the port and quays.

  4. 04Return

    Finish by the lake, return by M2

    Walk a chosen section of Ouchy’s tree-lined waterfront toward the landing stage or Olympic Park. Use M2 back uphill when ready; confirm the last connection for your actual date rather than assuming one.

Fallback

A useful day even when the postcard disappears.

In rain or strong heat, shorten the cathedral lanes and use M2 between Riponne-Maurice Béjart and Ouchy-Olympique. Keep the walk around the covered market streets or the Olympic Museum only after checking its date-specific hours; omit the exposed quay if wind or rain makes it unpleasant.

Three failure points

Change the plan before the problem compounds.

01

The stairs are part of the route

The 150 steps from Place de la Palud to the cathedral can be tiring. Use the M2 for elevation and shorten the uphill section when needed.

02

Station works change the approach

Construction around Lausanne station can change pedestrian access. Follow current on-site signs and the TL station notice instead of relying on an old exit.

03

A promenade is not a beach day

Ouchy’s quays are shared by pedestrians and other users. Stay on marked public paths and treat swimming or seasonal facilities as separate, date-specific choices.

Planning answers

Lausanne Old City & Ouchy FAQ

Which M2 stop is best for the old city?

Riponne-Maurice Béjart is a useful start for Place de la Palud and the cathedral. The exact walk includes uphill lanes and stairs; check current station access and choose another approach if needed.

Can I see Ouchy and the old town without climbing twice?

Yes. Walk the upper section once, use M2 between Lausanne-Flon and Ouchy-Olympique, and finish at the lake. Reverse the direction if your hotel or train departure makes that easier.

Is the Olympic Museum open every day?

Lausanne Tourisme’s 2026 listing shows Tuesday–Sunday, 09:00–18:00, and Monday closure except holidays or special events. Hours and special closures can change, so confirm on the museum’s official page for your date.
