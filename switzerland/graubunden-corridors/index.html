Chur, Davos & Graubünden

Graubünden is a network of valleys; use Chur as the rail hinge, then commit to one mountain corridor instead of collecting resort names.

Chur is a compact historic city and the practical gate to multiple Rhaetian Railway corridors. Davos, Arosa and the Rhine Gorge are not neighboring attractions in walking terms; each is a distinct rail or road day. Treat the canton as a set of transport corridors with different weather and seasonal functions.

Route comparison

Three different reasons to leave the hotel.

01

Chur Old Town & Rhine Gorge

Chur plus a short gorge segment gives city-and-landscape contrast in one day. A longer gorge hike requires more time and trail planning, leaving less room for the old city.

Half a day for Chur, full day for a meaningful gorge extension. Allow two to three hours for the old town alone and most of a day when a verified gorge rail or walking segment is part of the plan; the transfer to the chosen exit station belongs inside that time budget.02

Davos & Parsenn

Davos gives robust infrastructure and broad mountain access but less compact old-town character. It suits outdoor travelers; visitors seeking historic urban texture may prefer Chur as the overnight base.

Give Davos a full day even without a major hike. Mountain routes need separate condition checks and more margin.03

Arosa & the Mountain Railway

Arosa offers a self-contained resort basin and scenic rail arrival. It is a poor add-on to another corridor because the train consumes meaningful time in both directions.

Most of a day from Chur; longer if hiking or winter activities are the actual purpose.
