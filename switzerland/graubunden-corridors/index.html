Chur, Davos & Graubünden

Chur is the rail hinge; Rhine Gorge, Davos and Arosa are separate corridor days with different weather and return decisions.

Graubünden is a canton of branching valleys, not a compact sightseeing circuit. Chur is the practical rail and overnight hinge; the Rhine Gorge, Davos and Arosa each leave that hinge by a different route and need their own day. Choose one branch, identify its return station or descent before departure, and use Chur’s old-town streets when mountain visibility, trail status or seasonal operations remove the reason to travel higher.

Stay

Two nights in Chur can hold arrival, a focused old-town loop and one full excursion day; three nights make room for two branches with one flexible day between them. Sleep in Davos for two or three nights when Parsenn or another Davos mountain system is the main purpose, and in Arosa when you want more than a village-and-lake stop. A Chur hotel near the station favors rail departures; an old-town stay favors evenings but adds a luggage walk. In Davos, match the hotel to Dorf or Platz and the intended lift; in Arosa, check the hotel’s station approach before booking.

Transfer contract

Use RhB as the regional spine and SBB to compare the exact date and return. Chur–Arosa is its own branch; Davos is reached through the Landquart corridor; the Rhine Gorge rail line runs between Reichenau-Tamins and Ilanz, with PostBus and local buses serving other access points. The Rhine Gorge Association describes RhB service through the gorge as hourly, but use the current date-specific planner rather than treating that general pattern as a departure promise. A trailhead, exit station, lift and onward bus are separate legs: save each one before leaving Chur.

Season gate

Valley streets remain the robust layer, while high lifts, snow routes, summer trails and gorge paths follow different operating calendars. Shoulder seasons can leave a scenic rail corridor open while a lift or marked path is closed. Rain can make gorge paths slippery; wind, cloud and snow can change the safe choice on Parsenn or above Arosa. Check the chosen operator, local trail notice and MeteoSwiss forecast for the actual elevation on the morning of the trip.

Fallback

Keep a lower-risk layer ready.

Keep Chur’s station-side old town as the dependable plan: shorten the outdoor loop, choose an indoor stop only after confirming its current opening, and return by a checked local train. If the rail line is operating but the gorge trail is not, ride through the gorge without leaving the train. In Davos or Arosa, switch to a confirmed open valley or village activity; do not replace one closed lift with another unverified mountain system.

Route comparison

Three different reasons to leave the hotel.

01

Chur Old Town & Rhine Gorge

The gorge is a landscape to enter by a deliberate rail or signed trail plan, not a single roadside viewpoint. A train-only out-and-back preserves the scenery with less walking; the Valendas–Versam walk adds river-level texture but commits daylight and a fixed exit. A Flims Waldhaus–Il Spir approach reaches a high viewpoint on a separate path and is not a substitute for walking along the river.

Allow two to three hours for Chur’s compact old town. Adding the published Valendas–Versam walk, rail transfers, a meal and the return makes a full day; treat the operator’s roughly 1.5-hour walk figure as a planning reference, not a personal-time or trail-condition promise.02

Davos & Parsenn

Sleeping in Davos removes a long same-day rail approach and gives a more forgiving start for mountain plans, but adds a hotel change if Chur is the main base. Staying in Chur works for one carefully chosen excursion day; it is a poor reason to cram Parsenn together with Arosa or the Rhine Gorge.

Reserve most of a day for Parsenn from Chur, including both rail legs, the approach from the correct Davos station, time at elevation and a conservative descent. From an overnight base, use the saved last descent to set the day’s turnaround. A valley-only Davos visit is a separate, shorter plan.03

Arosa & the Mountain Railway

Arosa is a self-contained resort basin with a scenic rail arrival. A village-and-lake visit avoids a lift commitment but still needs weather-appropriate paths; adding a mountain lift increases exposure and makes the return margin more important. The rail ride consumes meaningful time in both directions, so the Rhine Gorge or Davos does not belong on the same day.

Give the trip most of a day from Chur: count both rail legs, the station-to-village connection, a meal and a return buffer. A single village-and-lake block is the shorter version; hiking or a lift needs a larger weather and daylight margin.
Planning answers

Chur, Davos & Graubünden FAQ

How many nights fit Chur and the mountain branches?

Two Chur nights suit the old town plus one full corridor day. Three let you keep two different branches and move one around the forecast. Choose a Davos or Arosa overnight when the mountain area itself, rather than a single day trip, is the priority.

Can I visit the Rhine Gorge, Davos and Arosa in one day?

No useful plan treats them as one loop. Pick one branch from Chur, save the return connection and leave the other two for separate days; the canton’s short map distances hide different rail corridors and last-mile access.

What is the safest weather alternative?

Keep the Chur old town and a checked rail ride as the low-elevation plan. For any gorge walk or mountain lift, verify the specific path or lift status separately; a train journey or valley forecast does not establish trail safety above it.
