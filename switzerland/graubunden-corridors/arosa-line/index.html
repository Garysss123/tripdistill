Access first

Know where the day actually begins.

Board at Chur station for the RhB branch to Arosa. The railway journey is part of the day, not a quick local hop; once in Arosa, the station-to-village walk, local bus, lift and trail are separate legs. Check the exact train pair first, then use Arosa Lenzerheide’s live mountain information and route planner for the activity you intend to do.

Trade-off

What this route gives up.

Arosa is a self-contained resort basin with a scenic rail arrival. A village-and-lake visit avoids a lift commitment but still needs weather-appropriate paths; adding a mountain lift increases exposure and makes the return margin more important. The rail ride consumes meaningful time in both directions, so the Rhine Gorge or Davos does not belong on the same day.

Time envelope

Give the trip most of a day from Chur: count both rail legs, the station-to-village connection, a meal and a return buffer. A single village-and-lake block is the shorter version; hiking or a lift needs a larger weather and daylight margin.

Combine only when it helps

Pair with Chur in the evening only if the return leaves time to eat and settle. Keep Davos and the Rhine Gorge for different days; changing valleys adds transfers without improving the Arosa visit.

Four-stage operating line

Arrive, orient, commit, then protect the return.

Check RhB/SBB for exact-date trains and the planned return. Check Arosa Lenzerheide’s live lift status and route planner for the chosen mountain or path, then compare the Arosa forecast with MeteoSwiss. Do not infer a trail, lift or local bus is operating from the railway being open.

Fallback

A useful day even when the postcard disappears.

If cloud, rain or a seasonal pause removes the mountain activity, keep a short village visit only if the station approach and local paths are suitable, then return on the saved RhB train. If even that is unappealing, use Chur’s old town instead of traveling up just to wait indoors. Check local service notices before changing the route.

Three failure points

Change the plan before the problem compounds.

01

Rail time is real time

Arosa’s map distance from Chur can look deceptively small. Count the full railway journey in both directions.

02

Separate operating systems

Train, local bus, lift and trail notices are not interchangeable. Check the exact leg you plan to use.

03

Wet, snowy or dark paths

The village and lake are not an all-weather promise. Follow current path notices and return before daylight or surface conditions erode the margin.

Planning answers

Arosa & the Mountain Railway FAQ

Is Arosa an easy add-on from Chur?

It is a distinct corridor day. Count the full Chur–Arosa rail journey in both directions and choose one local activity; do not treat Arosa as a quick stop between other branches.

Can I visit the lake if the lifts are closed?

Possibly, if the path is currently open and suitable for weather, snow and daylight. Check the route planner and local notices; closed lifts do not prove the village path is safe or clear.

When should I sleep in Arosa?

Stay at least two nights when hiking, skiing or using a mountain area is the main purpose and you want to shift that activity around the forecast. For one village-and-lake day, Chur remains a practical rail base if the return works.
