Access first

Know where the day actually begins.

From Interlaken West or Ost, use SBB rail to Thun or Spiez for the efficient route; BLS boats are a slower scenic alternative. The Interlaken West landing sits beside its station, while the Spiez pier is about a 15-minute walk from Spiez station. Thun station is a short walk from the old centre and Aare crossings; confirm castle access separately.

Trade-off

What this route gives up.

Thun gives the longer urban sequence: station, Obere Hauptgasse, Rathausplatz, Schlossberg and the Aare. Spiez is a smaller bay-and-castle visit, but the station-to-pier/castle approach is uphill and deserves time. A boat between towns is rewarding when you want the lake journey; it is inefficient if you only want both town centres.

Time envelope

Allow four to six hours for a focused Thun walk and meal, or three to four hours for Spiez bay and castle grounds. Doing both is a full day when rail or boat connections are convenient; drop the second town if the first visit or return gets compressed.

Combine only when it helps

This is the strongest low-elevation alternative when Jungfrau peaks are clouded. Pair Thun or Spiez with an Interlaken evening, not another major mountain excursion. A two-town loop works best as one primary town plus a short second stop and a train return.

Local context

Follow one coherent town walk; use the lake as a deliberate connector.

Thun is the deeper urban day

Start from Thun station and move through Obere Hauptgasse and Rathausplatz toward Schlossberg, then descend to the Aare-side centre. Keep the castle interior as a separate, date-checked choice rather than assuming it is open.

Spiez trades scale for the bay

The bay and castle sit below the station. BLS notes roughly a 15-minute station-to-pier walk; the climb back and castle’s own season are part of the plan, not incidental details.

Four-stage operating line

Arrive, orient, commit, then protect the return.

Check BLS Lake Thun sailings for the date and exact landing, SBB for rail alternatives, and the official Thun or Spiez castle page for current entry and opening conditions. Mountain visibility and boat service are separate decisions. Check the forecast again before an exposed bay walk; no future boat timetable or castle opening is promised.

  1. 01Arrive

    Choose the town before the boat

    Use SBB to set a protected rail return. Add the BLS boat only when the sailing connects the route you actually want.

  2. 02Orient

    Read Thun on foot

    From the station, reach Obere Hauptgasse and Rathausplatz, climb toward Schlossberg if conditions suit, then follow the Aare-side lanes back toward the station.

  3. 03Commit

    Or make Spiez a compact bay walk

    From Spiez station, follow the signed route down to the castle and bay. Check the castle’s own access calendar and budget the climb back to the train.

  4. 04Return

    Finish by rail or the saved sailing

    If the day included both towns, protect the SBB return rather than chasing another pier. Skip the second stop if the first visit or weather runs long.

Fallback

A useful day even when the postcard disappears.

If boats are seasonal or weather is rough, use SBB between Interlaken, Thun and Spiez and keep one town as the anchor. In rain, choose Thun’s covered old-town arc, then add an indoor castle or museum only after checking its date-specific hours. Keep Spiez for a short bay view if the uphill walk and castle access still make sense.

Three failure points

Change the plan before the problem compounds.

01

Castle interiors have their own calendar

Do not assume the towers, museum rooms or grounds share the same opening window. Confirm the exact date on the official castle page.

02

Spiez is a hillside approach

The bay looks close on a map, but the station-to-pier walk takes about 15 minutes and the return rises. Allow time and consider mobility needs.

03

Boat versus train

The BLS boat is a scenic route, not automatically the quickest connection. Check the dated sailing and return before choosing it over SBB.

Planning answers

Lake Thun, Thun & Spiez FAQ

Should I visit Thun or Spiez first?

Choose Thun for a longer old-town, river and castle-context walk; choose Spiez for a shorter bay-and-castle landscape. If visiting both, protect the rail return and drop the second stop when the day becomes rushed.

Is the Lake Thun boat faster than the train?

Treat it as a scenic journey, not a time-saving transfer. Check the date-specific BLS sailing and both landings, then compare the actual SBB rail connection.

Can I see the castles without booking?

The route does not assume a reservation or guaranteed entry. Check the official Thun and Spiez castle pages for the exact date, opening hours, any booking terms and closures before building the walk around an interior visit.
