Interlaken, Thun & Brienz

Use Interlaken as the transfer hinge, then give each day one job: a Brienz-side boat or waterfall, a Thun-side town walk, or a weather-gated mountain view.

Interlaken links Lake Thun, Lake Brienz and the Jungfrau valley railways, but the two lakes are separate day shapes. Choose one shore as the main anchor, use rail when it protects time, and keep Harder Kulm as a visibility-led extra. This hub compares the Interlaken base walk, the Brienz–Giessbach branch and the Thun–Spiez branch so a short stay does not become a list of rushed piers.

Stay

Two nights suit one lake branch plus a compact town orientation. Three nights let you give both shores a day and move one for weather. Four nights make room for a separate Jungfrau or Harder Kulm window without forcing it onto a lake day. Stay near Interlaken Ost for the Zentralbahn to Brienz, Jungfrau departures and the Harderbahn valley station; choose West for the BLS pier, Thun-side trains and a central evening walk. Base in Thun or Brienz only when most nights and plans stay on that shore; moving hotels adds luggage and check-in time.

Transfer contract

Use SBB for the Interlaken–Thun–Spiez rail corridor and Zentralbahn for Interlaken Ost–Brienz. BLS boats are the scenic, slower connection: the Lake Thun and Lake Brienz timetables are separate, and a sailing is useful only when both the chosen landing and return fit. Interlaken West is next to its Thun-lake landing; at Spiez the pier is about a 15-minute walk from the station. Interlaken Ost is the rail gateway for Brienz and the short walk to Harderbahn. Search the exact date and named station or pier in SBB and the operator timetable.

Season gate

Lake Thun navigation has year-round service, while Lake Brienz boat dates and frequency are seasonal; the 2026 BLS page lists 3 April–6 December, which is not a promise for later seasons. Giessbach’s own arrival page describes its funicular and boat connection as seasonal (April–October), so verify the exact landing and funicular separately. Harder Kulm is also seasonal and visibility-dependent. Check current weather, operator notices, trail access and any date-specific ticket or reservation condition before paying or leaving the valley.

Fallback

Keep a lower-risk layer ready.

If high cloud hides the peaks, move the day to Thun’s old town or a Brienz rail-and-lakeside loop. If a boat, Giessbach funicular or Harderbahn is not operating, use the named train route rather than waiting at a pier. Rain can shorten the exposed shore walk: keep one compact town centre, a checked indoor visit or the hotel-area reset, and leave enough time for the last useful return.

Route comparison

Three different reasons to leave the hotel.

01

Interlaken Base & Harder Kulm

Ost reduces transfers for Brienz and the high valleys; West puts you by the Thun boat pier and closer to the central shopping/evening corridor. Neither station is a universal winner, and changing hotels to chase both can cost a useful half day. Harder Kulm rises to 1,322 metres by a 10-minute funicular ride, but the view is the product: skip it in dense cloud or if the date’s operation does not fit.

Allow two to three hours for the station-to-station town walk, longer for a relaxed meal or a short Harder Kulm visit. Make the funicular a separate weather decision; it can turn the visit into a half-day but should not be squeezed between a long Jungfrau excursion and a fixed evening connection.02

Lake Brienz, Brienz & Giessbach

The lake is a lower-elevation day with a different pace from Jungfrau peaks. A boat makes the crossing part of the outing but uses more time; rail is the reliable time-saving spine for Brienz. Giessbach adds a steep, wet waterfall walk and a seasonal last mile, so choose it for the falls rather than as a casual pier hop.

Give Brienz village three to four hours including a meal and lakeside loop. A Giessbach visit needs a half-day block once boat access, the funicular or uphill path, falls viewpoints and the return are included. A full lake crossing plus both stops can use most of a day.03

Lake Thun, Thun & Spiez

Thun gives the longer urban sequence: station, Obere Hauptgasse, Rathausplatz, Schlossberg and the Aare. Spiez is a smaller bay-and-castle visit, but the station-to-pier/castle approach is uphill and deserves time. A boat between towns is rewarding when you want the lake journey; it is inefficient if you only want both town centres.

Allow four to six hours for a focused Thun walk and meal, or three to four hours for Spiez bay and castle grounds. Doing both is a full day when rail or boat connections are convenient; drop the second town if the first visit or return gets compressed.
Planning answers

Interlaken, Thun & Brienz FAQ

How many nights should I stay in Interlaken?

Two nights work for one carefully chosen lake day and a short base walk. Three nights allow both lake branches with a weather move. Choose four only when you also want a separate mountain or Jungfrau day and can use the flexibility.

Should I stay near Interlaken Ost or West?

Ost simplifies Brienz, Jungfrau-region trains and Harder Kulm; West is convenient for the Lake Thun pier, trains toward Thun and a central evening walk. Check the exact hotel-to-station and luggage route, not only the district name.

What if a boat or mountain connection is closed?

Check the exact-date operator status before leaving, then swap to the rail-supported town or shore plan: Zentralbahn to Brienz, SBB to Thun or Spiez, or the Interlaken walk. Boat, lift, trail and reservation status can change; no future timetable is guaranteed here.
