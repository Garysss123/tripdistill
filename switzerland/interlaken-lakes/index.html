Interlaken, Thun & Brienz

Interlaken is useful because it is a switchboard; the day becomes better when you choose one lake or one valley instead of treating the gateway as the destination.

Interlaken sits between Lake Thun and Lake Brienz and connects onward to the Jungfrau region. The town itself is convenient but not the strongest reason to spend a whole day. Use it as a base, then decide whether the day belongs to Brienz/Giessbach, Thun/Spiez or the high valleys.

Route comparison

Three different reasons to leave the hotel.

01

Interlaken Base & Harder Kulm

The town is convenient, flat and easy for recovery, but it should not displace a stronger lake or valley day. Harder Kulm adds an overhead view when clear, not an automatic must-do.

Two to four hours is enough for the town itself; Harder Kulm can extend the block. The real value is simplifying multiple excursion days.02

Lake Brienz, Brienz & Giessbach

The lake gives a calmer, lower-elevation day than the Jungfrau peaks. Boat travel consumes time but is the point; train travel is faster and better when you want more time in Brienz or at the falls.

Allow a full half day for Brienz or Giessbach; a boat-plus-stop circuit can use most of a day.03

Lake Thun, Thun & Spiez

Thun offers more urban depth and a larger old-town walk; Spiez offers a smaller bay-and-castle setting. Doing both is realistic, but one should remain the main stop.

Half a day for one town, most of a day for Thun plus Spiez with a boat or long meal.
